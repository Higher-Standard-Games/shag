# Authored BSP collision versus generated draw-surface facets

Date: 2026-10-16. Follow-up to the [performance implementation](./v0.9.31-collision-implementation.md).
No runtime collision-source policy change has been made by this investigation.
The subsequent [authored collision trial](./v0.9.31-authored-collision.md) implements
the brush-and-patch-only policy as a separate checkpoint.

## Why revisit the source of collision

Jal clarified q3map's pipeline: collision brushes are established independently
of draw surfaces, including detail brushes unless their shader makes them
nonsolid. Corner softening affects drawing. Automatically generated model
collision can add brushes, but it is a separate source of complexity.

SHAG reads those collision brushes **and** generates collision facets from type-3
triangle-soup draw surfaces. The latter is an engine compatibility extension,
not a requirement of the BSP format. Optimizing that extension does not establish
that it is necessary or semantically correct.

## Source checks

Compared SHAG's `BspLoader.ts` with local Qfusion
`source/qcommon/qfiles.h` and `cm_q3bsp.c`:

- Brushes: 12-byte records with first side, side count, shader index.
- FBSP/RBSP brush sides: 12-byte records with plane, shader and draw-surface index.
- Leaves: 48-byte records; first/count brush references at offsets 40/44.
- Leaf-brush references: indices into the brush array.
- Brush contents: shader contents, separate from rendered faces.
- Qfusion loads collision facets only for patch faces. It skips triangle soups.

SHAG's detail brushes retain solidity through their contents. The parser layouts
match the reference. No swapped draw-face/brush lump interpretation was found.
The extra draw-surface collision is explicit in `SweepCollisionBackend.processFaces`.
`surfaceNum` links a brush side to a draw surface, but is not a complete source
entity/model ownership or collision-authority map. Shader prefixes are likewise
heuristics, not proof that a surface is world geometry or needs collision.

## Independent brush candidate audit

Compared current BSP traversal plus the clip fallback against exhaustive tracing
of the world model's brush range, with all face collision disabled in both.
Used 3,000 seeded queries per map above, with point/small/player/large hulls and
solid, playerclip and combined masks: 18,000 queries total.

No fraction/solid-flag discrepancy was found where **both** traces started outside
solid. Five wfdm5 discrepancies and one wbomb5 discrepancy involved embedded
starts or solid-flag differences at zero fraction. They require further analysis
of leaf references, unused brushes, masks and embedded traversal semantics;
exhaustively colliding every brush lump entry is not automatically authoritative.
The independent named-clip fallback also means raw tree-only and actual runtime
queries are different experiments. Do not remove that fallback based on this audit.

This evidence does not support a broad claim that ordinary player traces are
looping the wrong brush data. It does identify the generated draw-surface path
as independent, and leaves embedded/mask behavior as a separate question.

## Controlled brush-and-patch-only support experiment

Built two otherwise identical sweep worlds from indexed BSP data. One retains
the current collision policy; the other removes only type-3 collision meshes,
retaining authored brushes and patch collision. This is a local diagnostic,
not a shipped player setting or automatic map-name heuristic.

| Map | Spawn probes | Lost ground support | Ground height changed by more than 1 unit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| wfdm11 | 11 | 0 | 1 |
| wfdm5 | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| wbomb4 | 30 | 0 | 0 |
| wbomb5 | 33 | 0 | 0 |
| wdm1 | 7 | 0 | 0 |
| wdm17 | 9 | 0 | 0 |

The differing wfdm11 spawn is at engine position `(-1216, -328, 608)`.
Current generated collision settles the origin at about -367.969; authored
brush/patch collision settles it at -375.969: an eight-unit difference.
One wdm17 spawn starts embedded in both modes, so it is not a clean landing test.
These are long downward hull probes, not complete gameplay reachability tests.

The 580 sampled positions of the supplied wfdm11 recording lose no ground support
with soup collision disabled. Downward ground-probe heights differ at 142 samples,
but replaying the **recorded displacements** through `stepSlideMove` changes none
of their endpoints by more than 0.01 units. This does not reconstruct the intended
input/velocity before collision, so it cannot prove actual movement equivalence
or diagnose the repeated jump sounds.

## Why the older support argument is insufficient

`bsp-triangle-soup-collision.md` reports unsupported surface-centroid probes as
load-bearing gameplay surfaces. Rechecking both triangle centroids and aggregate
face centroids exposes two problems with that inference:

1. Many hull probes start embedded and report recovery normals rather than clean
   ground contacts. Their result must not be classified as a walkable floor.
2. A rendered upward-facing cap, model detail or sky-portal scene is not necessarily
   intended to support gameplay. In wdm1, suspicious examples around
   `(4464, 4048, 4182)` lie in the remote scene around its `misc_skyportal`, whose
   origin is Q3 `(3328, 3328, 3296)`. This makes them candidates for scenery,
   not evidence that a playable route falls out of the world.

There remain non-embedded draw-surface contacts with no nearby brush/patch
support. They need spatial/gameplay classification, not automatic promotion into
collision. No old numerical surface count is accepted as proof of required
triangle-soup collision in this investigation.

## Jump-sound observation

`FPSMovementAudio.ts` plays a jump sound when the previous snapshot was grounded,
the new one is not, vertical velocity exceeds 60, and horizontal travel is small.
It does not consume an explicit jump-activation event. The supplied recording
contains 101 such snapshot edges, concentrated in the repeated stair traversal;
many have much lower upward velocity than the configured jump impulse.

The recording's input arrays are empty, so it cannot establish whether jump was
pressed at those edges. The condition is consistent with step/contact transitions
being classified as jumps, but the actual cause is not proven. Collision-source
testing and sound-event classification should remain separate measurements.
No jump, grounding or audio rules were changed in the performance work.

## Recommended next experiment

Evaluate a clearly identified authored brush-and-patch-only build against the
current optimized policy, first headlessly, then in-game. Cover normal routes,
stairs in both directions, crouch/slide, walls, ledges, high-speed motion,
projectile hits, patches, doors and lifts. Count ground transitions and actual
jump activations separately from sound triggers. Check both client prediction
and the server. Keep an independent checkpoint for any collision-source change.

If coverage confirms authored collision supplies gameplay, prefer removing the
extra soup path rather than simplifying its decorative triangles. Do not invent
a shader-prefix/map-name fallback or a runtime compiler-key handler to bridge
unverified assumptions. Do not decimate authored model-generated brushes without
an explicit error budget and movement validation.
