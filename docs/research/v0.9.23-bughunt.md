# v0.9.23 bug hunt

**Started:** 2026-09-13. **Last progress update:** 2026-09-15.

**Status:** Twelve observations collected across FFA, Duel and Search & Destroy
sessions on `wdm2`, `wdm7` and `wbomb6`. They resolve into **seven clusters**,
of which three are fixed (uncommitted), one is half-fixed, and one was
introduced by this cycle's own work. Nothing here is committed yet. **Update
2026-09-15:** A turned out to have two causes, both fixed: the second-join cache
defect is committed and play-tested, the map weapon landing is uncommitted and
not yet play-tested. **Later on 2026-09-15:** four new reports from play —
projectiles missing, blinking or untextured (H), world surfaces see-through after
a map load (I), the camera rolling (J), and the plasmagun body missing again on
map pickups (A). Two projectile defects are fixed and pinned by tests, and three
console warnings are in so the next session names what the rest are waiting on.

**Source snapshot:** `1de2e9b6`, branch `feature/v0.9.23-gfx-investigation`.

**Scope:** Player-reported defects from live play. Rendering research and its
measurement protocol live in [v0.9.23-rendering-research.md](v0.9.23-rendering-research.md);
this document is only the defect ledger.

**How to read this.** Each item states the symptom as observed, what is
*confirmed* by reading code or running a tool, what is *hypothesis*, and what

## A. Dropped weapon world models missing or partial

**Observed.** On `wdm2` the rocket launcher lying on the ground was invisible,
yet walking over it picked it up, showed it in hand, and dropped it again
correctly. The plasmagun on the ground renders **only its loading chambers** —
the gun body is absent. Grenade and rocket projectile models appear untextured.

**Confirmed (2026-09-15): two causes.**

*The rocket launcher on the ground.* Found in play: on wdm2 the launcher beside
a ledge sometimes lay on the level below, while its pickup still worked from
the spot above. A map weapon's landing was a Havok toss run by each client alone
— `EntityManager` gave it a velocity and `Pickup` a random tumble — while the
server's trigger stayed at the authored origin and never moved. Headless runs
put weapons 27–63u from their origins, mostly from the tumble. Dropped weapons
were unaffected because `DroppedWeaponSystem` simulates their throw on the
server and the trigger follows it.

*Geometry lost on a second join.* A separate, confirmed defect that fits the
plasmagun and projectile reports:

1. `loadClientMap` runs `scene clear` whenever a scene already exists, and
   `SceneCommand.clearScene` disposes every scene mesh with `dispose(false, true)`.
2. That includes the disabled source models cached for cloning by
   `AssetPreloader` (map and dropped weapons) and `ProjectileManager` (rocket,
   grenade, plasma, blade). Neither cache was reset.
3. The next map load found both already cached. A Babylon clone of a disposed
   mesh has **0 vertices**, and `Pickup.loadModel` skips zero-vertex meshes.
4. The plasmagun's chambers load fresh for every pickup
   (`FPSPickup._loadBarrels`), so they would render while a cached body did not.
   Projectiles clone the same empty meshes and keep only their trail and light.

Two more routes to broken models turned up alongside it: `Pickup.dispose`
disposed the material and texture every clone of a weapon shares, and
`PK3TextureLoader.clearCache` on each map load disposed textures that projectile
materials kept using. A separate KTX2 defect is real but explains none of the
reports: a decoder worker that fails to start leaves Babylon's worker pool
waiting forever with no error, so the texture never falls back to its source
image.

**Ruled out**, with the evidence in `docs/research/v0.9.23-asset-probe.ts`:
- **PVS.** It enables and disables whole meshes, never touches materials, and
  never registers pickups.
- **Geometry under the spawns.** Both `wdm2` rocket launchers sit on opaque
  `textures/boqu/treadplate` with Havok collision, and nothing non-solid lies
  within 48u of any weapon spawn.
- **Corpses or drops shoving the visual away.** Real Havok moved a settled
  pickup body at most 7u.
- **The pickup active mask.** It is sent in full every snapshot, never
  delta-encoded.
- **KTX on weapon models.** The weapon GLBs embed PNG textures.

**Fixed.** The cache defects (commit `371d744d`) and the KTX2 worker start
(`0f4b9eac`): `ModelSourceCache` checks a cached source before handing it out —
meshes alive, materials attached, textures not released — and releases only what
no live mesh or material still uses. `AssetPreloader` and `ProjectileManager` use
it; `MapLoader` releases pickup models with their map; `Pickup.dispose` no longer
disposes shared materials; `MD3Loader` unbinds released textures when it reuses a
material; and a KTX2 decoder worker that cannot start fails its decodes so the
fallback runs.

The landing (uncommitted): `shared/items/ItemThrow` throws every map weapon from
its pickup id through the shared collision world to where it comes to rest, and
the server trigger (`HeadlessMapLoader`), the client model (`EntityManager`) and
bot item goals (`BotVision`) all use that spot, while `DroppedWeaponSystem` steps
drops with the same function. `docs/research/v0.9.23-pickup-landing-probe.ts`
shows two independently built collision worlds agreeing on every wdm2 and
acidwdm2 landing, 3–8u from the authored spots. Every fix has a regression test,
each checked by breaking it on purpose and watching the test fail.

The landing alone was not enough (2026-09-16). The client placed each weapon 20u
above its landing and then raycast down for the floor beneath it — a second,
client-only guess laid on top of the shared one. Wherever that ray reached past
the floor the landing had chosen — a gap, a thin brush, a start inside geometry —
the model seated itself below while the trigger stayed above: a weapon you cannot
see but can collect where the mapper put it. That was the rocket launcher on
wdm2's lower floor. A weapon is now placed at its resting spot and left there, and
only a pickup with no computed landing still looks for the floor beneath it.

**Remaining.** Play-test the landing: every weapon should rest where it is picked
up — the wdm2 rocket launcher by the ledge included, and the one on the lower
floor drawn at all.

**Root-caused (2026-09-16) and fixed.** The body was never missing — it was
hidden, which is why the geometry probe kept finding it. `AssetPreloader` caches
every source mesh disabled and invisible, and Babylon clones a mesh together with
its children, but the clone loop turned on only the mesh it named. Anything that
arrived as a child kept the cached hidden state, and nothing downstream turned it
on: `Pickup.loadModel` walks only the meshes the clone handed back. The
plasmagun's chambers are imported fresh per pickup by `_loadBarrels`, so they
drew over an invisible body; the rocket launcher has no such parts, so it drew
nothing while its trigger kept working. A dropped weapon escaped it because a
mid-match drop usually finds the cache invalidated and falls back to
`ModelLoader`, which makes every mesh it returns visible — the asymmetry that
named the culprit. `src/engine/loader/__tests__/AssetPreloaderClone.test.ts`
reproduces both the hidden child and the duplicate clone the same loop produced.

This also explains the randomness: whether a given load draws depends on whether
the cache was still valid when the pickup was built, which is why a reload
"fixed" it. `docs/research/v0.9.23-pickup-render-probe.ts` never saw it because
NullEngine counts vertices, not what is switched on.

## B. Player models float above the floor, or sink into it

**Observed.** On `wdm2` an enemy stood visibly above the floor. On `wdm7` a bot
appeared partially sunk into it. Reloading the same `wdm2` match placed models
correctly. The defect is **intermittent across map loads**, not across players
within a load.

**Confirmed.** Nothing instrumented yet.

**Analysis.** The reporter's own observation is the most valuable evidence:
during the **death animation the model collided with the world correctly** while
the standing model floated. Collision uses the entity position; the model uses a
visual anchor. If one is right and the other is wrong, the entity position is
sound and the **anchor offset** is what drifts.

"Correct after a reload" makes this a race rather than a data error — the same
map and the same models produce different results on different loads.

There is a concrete suspect. `RemotePlayerRenderer` carries a **late model
assign** path (`[RemotePlayerRenderer] Late model assign for …`) for entities
that appear before the player model finishes loading. A model attached through
that path may miss anchor setup the normal path performs. That would produce
exactly this: usually fine, wrong when the load order happens to differ, and
consistent for the whole session once established.

**Ruled out.** PVS, which only toggles `setEnabled` and never writes a
transform. Also the collision facet-bevel change, which cannot move a render
anchor.

**Investigate.**
1. Log anchor Y against hitbox Y at spawn; a constant offset names the bug.
2. Record whether floating entities came through the late-assign branch.
3. Compare the anchor setup either side of that branch.

## C. Distant bots slide without animating

**Observed.** A bot strafing across the reporter's view was "standing and
sliding" — moving with no limb motion.

**Status:** root cause NOT settled. The first diagnosis was wrong and is
corrected below. The animation LOD has been rebuilt on sounder terms anyway,
and a second, stronger candidate has been found and made observable.

### First diagnosis, and why it was wrong

This was attributed to the distance-based animation LOD added in this cycle,
which pauses a player's `AnimationGroup` beyond a threshold while leaving the
model drawn — exactly this symptom. The threshold was 2500 units, and the
reasoning given for it ("limb motion is sub-pixel there") was wrong, so the
conclusion looked safe.

Working the arithmetic out properly undermines it. A 64-unit body at 1080p
under a 59 degree vertical field of view covers 24 pixels at **2545 units** —
within 2% of the constant being blamed. So the old threshold only fired where a
player really is a thumbnail, and unless that bot was more than ~2500 units
away, the LOD did not cause this.

Recorded because the reasoning error is the interesting part: a plausible
mechanism plus a genuinely bad justification read as a confirmed cause, and it
took doing the sums to separate them.

### What changed anyway

The LOD now gates on **projected screen height** rather than world distance:
pause below `ANIMATION_LOD_MIN_PIXELS` (24). Distance was never the question —
"can a viewer resolve limb motion" is about projected size, and the same
distance is a different picture at 70 degrees and at 110, or on 1080p and on a
phone. The radius is solved once per frame from the live camera, so the
per-player test stays a squared-distance compare.

It fails toward animating: an unmeasurable camera, a non-perspective projection
or a zero render height all return "pause nothing".

| render height | vertical FOV | pauses beyond |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1080 | 59 deg | 2545 units |
| 1440 | 59 deg | 3394 units |
| 1080 | 73 deg | 1946 units |

The spread across that table is the reason to prefer it: one constant meant
three different things.

### Leading candidate now: a silently missing clip

`PlayerModelManager.playAnimationOn` returns early when a clip is not loaded:

```ts
const animData = instance.anims.find((a) => a.name === name);
if (!animData) return false;   // leaves the PREVIOUS animation playing
```

It does **not** fall back to idle, despite a comment elsewhere in the file
claiming it does. The previous clip keeps playing, so a bot going from idle into
a strafe with `strafe_l` missing stays in **idle while sliding sideways** —
precisely the reported symptom, and with no console output to hint at it.

Both `strafe left.glb` and `strafe right.glb` exist on disk, so this is not an
obviously absent file; loading could still fail for other reasons, and the
directory also holds `strafe.glb` and `strafe (2).glb`, which suggests the set
has been reworked.

A one-time warning per missing clip now makes this loud:
`[PlayerModelManager] animation "…" is not loaded; players needing it keep their
previous pose`.

**Next step.** Play a round and read the console. Either the warning names a
clip — in which case this is solved — or it stays quiet, which rules the
hypothesis out and points back at the LOD or at `deriveAnimation` choosing a
name that never plays.

## D. End-of-match screen

**Observed (FFA, 8 players).** The board showed `TEAM SOBA 0` / `TEAM UDON 0`
in a free-for-all; every player was listed under `SPECTATORS (8)` with no
scores; the winner's name flickered in size; the winner's model stood in the
wrong place.

**Confirmed — and half fixed.**

*The team layout* came from `Scoreboard.ts` deriving team-ness as
`Object.keys(state.teamScores).length > 0`. `MatchState` initialises
`_teamScores` with both `udon` and `soba` **in every gametype**, so that test is
always true. `BaseGametype.teamBased` already existed and
`GametypeController` already passed it to `MatchState`; it simply never reached
the snapshot. It does now, and the three widgets that guessed —
`Scoreboard`, `PauseMenu`, `SpectatorOverlay` — read it instead. A later
screenshot confirms the team columns are gone.

*The spectator list* is a **separate bug, now root-caused.** The chain:

1. The match ends and every client is flagged `isSpectator` — the post-game
   camera puts the whole lobby into spectator state.
2. `SnapshotBuilder` builds the scoreboard's spectator roster from exactly that
   flag, so it collects all eight players.
3. Their simulation entities are gone, so `gameState.players` is empty and
   `HUDState` produces **no scored rows at all**.
4. Every name then arrives through the roster append, hard-coded
   `teamId: "spectator"`, and lands in the SPECTATORS block.

That matches the screenshot precisely: a `SCORE` column with no rows under it,
and `SPECTATORS (8)` holding the entire match.

The deeper problem is that **final standings have no source once the match
ends**. The scoreboard reads score and deaths from live entities, and
`MatchStateSnapshot` carries `winner` but no per-player score table — so at the
exact moment the result matters most, the data is gone. `MatchState` still holds
`_scores` and `_deaths` server-side; they simply are not shipped.

**Fixed.** `MatchStateSnapshot` gained `standings` — player id, name, score,
deaths and ping, built in `SnapshotBuilder` from the match's own tally rather
than from entities. The scoreboard uses them whenever the live pass yielded no
rows, which is exactly the post-game case, and the spectator roster appends
after that so nobody is listed twice.

Found while testing it: `MatchStateManager.reset()` cleared phase and scores but
**not** `_spectators`, and would not have cleared the new `_standings` either.
Both now clear. Left as they were, the next match's result screen would open
showing the previous match's names until a snapshot replaced them — a
pre-existing bug for spectators that the new field would have joined.

Covered by `EndgameStandings.test.ts`: standings survive the entities, absent
standings default to empty rather than undefined, free-for-all reports as
teamless, and reset clears the roster.

*The flickering name and misplaced model* are untouched. `PostGameShowcase`
carries a comment claiming the "model in a weird map position" problem was
already addressed, so either it regressed or the fix is partial.

**Investigate.**
1. Where scoreboard entries get their `teamId` at match end, and why FFA players
   are marked spectators there.
2. Whether the winner-name flicker is a CSS/layout loop or a per-frame rewrite.
3. `PostGameShowcase` model placement against its own stated intent.

## E. Remote players warp, drift into walls, float and sink

**Observed.** Warping on `wdm2`. A bot drifting *inside* a wall. Earlier in the
same cycle: a player floating above the floor on `wdm2`, a bot partly sunk into
the floor on `wdm7`, all intermittent and none reproducible on demand.

**Root-caused.** These are **one mechanism**, not four bugs. When the snapshot
buffer runs dry — renderTime past the newest snapshot, i.e. a late or dropped
packet — remote entities are dead-reckoned from their last known velocity, and
that extrapolation is **completely collision-blind**. It was capped at 200ms.

| extrapolation | at run speed (320 u/s) | off a rocket jump (~900 u/s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 200ms (old) | 64 units | 180 units |
| 100ms | 32 units | 90 units |
| 60ms (new) | 19 units | 54 units |

A player hull is 32 units across, so the old cap permitted **two full player
widths of travel through solid geometry** before the authoritative position
arrived and snapped the body back. That snap is the warp; the interval before it
is the body standing inside the wall.

The vertical branch explains the rest. Airborne entities have gravity applied
during extrapolation, so a bot caught mid-jump projects *upward* — a player
floating above the floor — and one caught falling projects *downward* — a bot
sunk into it. Grounded entities hold their Y, which is why this never affected
anyone standing still.

It fires only when a snapshot is late, which is exactly why every symptom in
this family has been intermittent, unreproducible on demand, and "fine after a
reload".

**Fixed, partially.** The cap is now a named, documented constant at **60ms** —
one snapshot interval at 30Hz plus margin. Past that a body freezes where it was
last seen, which reads as a brief stall rather than a player inside a wall.

**Not yet fixed: extrapolation is still collision-blind.** 60ms still permits 19
units at run speed and 54 off a rocket jump. The proper fix is to sweep the
extrapolated path against the client's collision world — which this client
already has — and clamp at the first hit, making penetration impossible at any
cap. That is the next step for this item.

**Also unmeasured: how often the buffer actually runs dry.** The cap bounds the
damage but says nothing about frequency. If it is running dry routinely rather
than rarely, that is a separate problem in snapshot pacing or the interpolation
delay, and shortening the cap would trade wall-clipping for visible stalling.
Worth instrumenting before tuning further.

## F. Projectile trails are flat, not conical

**Observed.** Trails render as flat triangles rather than a volume — "missing
the other part of the X shape". The reporter also noted the projectile appears
to start somewhere other than the player's own position.

**Root-caused and fixed (the trail half).**

The trail was never meant to be an X. It is a single **camera-facing** ribbon:
the half-width direction is `Cross(tangent, toCamera)`, which should keep its
face toward the viewer and read as a tapered streak.

That construction degenerates in the one case that matters most. A projectile
travels along the view axis, because players shoot where they are looking, so
`tangent` and `toCamera` become antiparallel and their cross product collapses
toward zero. The guard at `perp.lengthSquared() < 0.001` then falls back to
`Cross(tangent, Vector3.Up())` — a **world-horizontal** ribbon. Hence "flat
level triangles", which is the wording that identified it.

A billboard viewed down its own axis has no valid orientation, so the fix is not
a better fallback but a second blade: a crossing ribbon at 90 degrees
(`Cross(tangent, perp)`), sharing the material and disposal of the first. Two
crossed quads read as a volume from any angle, including straight down the
barrel — which is the classic shape and what the reporter's "X" intuition was
describing.

**Still open: the spawn position.** `computeMuzzleOffset` deliberately starts an
own-shot at the viewmodel muzzle — `MUZZLE_FORWARD` 34 units ahead and
`MUZZLE_DOWN` 9 below the camera — and converges onto the authoritative
trajectory over time, so the projectile leaving from somewhere other than the
player origin is *by design*. Whether it currently looks wrong because those two
constants are mis-tuned, or because the convergence is not running, is not yet
established.

**Investigate.** Fire at a wall at close range and watch whether the visual
converges onto the true path or stays displaced for the whole flight. That
separates a tuning problem from a broken convergence in one shot.

## G. Frame-rate-dependent presentation smoothing

**Observed.** Running the browser with VSync disabled (to measure peak FPS)
made "a lot of things flaky" at ~234 FPS.

**Confirmed.** The pattern exists in the codebase:
[FollowController.ts:172](../../src/engine/camera/controllers/FollowController.ts#L172),
[IsometricController.ts:330](../../src/engine/camera/controllers/IsometricController.ts#L330)
and [SideScrollController.ts:173](../../src/engine/camera/controllers/SideScrollController.ts#L173)
all call `Vector3.Lerp(current, target, smoothing)` with a constant factor,
which closes a fixed *percentage per frame* and therefore converges about four
times faster at 240 FPS than at 60.

**Ruled out.** Simulation timing. Movement derives its clock from
`sequence * dt` rather than accumulating frame time — the property that makes
reconciliation replays idempotent — so physics cannot fast-forward at high frame
rates, and `server:tick` / `server:fuzz` determinism would fail if it could.
Two of the four causes commonly cited for this class of problem (frame-dependent
timers, fixed-timestep mismatch) therefore do not apply here.

**Gap.** The three confirmed sites are the follow, isometric and side-scroller
cameras — **not** the FPS gameplay camera. So the class of bug is present but
the specific instances found are not the ones the reporter felt.

**Investigate.** The FPS camera and viewport effect stack, snapshot
interpolation timing, and weapon bob/recoil decay. The fix shape is the standard
frame-rate-independent form, `1 - Math.pow(1 - rate, dt * 60)`, so a smoothing
constant means the same thing at any frame rate.

**Priority.** Low. The game ships with VSync on, so this is latent rather than
active.

## H. Projectiles missing, blinking or untextured

**Observed.** On some loads plasma bolts were missing from shots while a trail
showed, and rocket and grenade projectiles were drawn untextured; reloading the
page fixed both. Later, mid-game, plasma bolts and their trails were both gone
while impacts and the muzzle flash still showed.

**Confirmed, fixed.** Two defects, each reproduced by
`src/engine/__tests__/ProjectileVisibility.test.ts` before the fix:

1. Every shot of a type shares its model's material, and each new shot set that
   material's alpha to zero to fade in, restarting the fade of every shot in
   flight. The plasmagun fires ten a second, so the newest bolt is always under
   100ms old and every bolt on screen spends most of its flight partly
   transparent. Shots now fade through their own meshes' visibility.
2. `ProjectileManager.update`, which moves, fades and builds trails for every
   projectile, ran only inside `WeaponViewManager.update`, after a return for a
   missing view weapon. A weapon switch clears the weapon before loading the
   next, so shots in that gap stayed transparent with no trail. It now runs
   before that return, together with hitscan effects.

**Confirmed, instrumented.** A server projectile is drawn only once its
`projectile_spawn` event has named its type, and the server sends that event
once, with the projectile's first snapshot. It is lost with a delta snapshot the
client drops for a missing baseline, and a projectile that leaves the client's
PVS mid-flight loses its type when its visual is despawned and comes back
undrawn. Both now warn: `[SnapshotReceiver]` and `[ProjectileSync]`.

**Ruled out.**
- *The projectile pk3 or its shaders loading late.* `data0_21pure.pk3` alone
  holds the projectile models and the shader scripts that make their glow
  surfaces additive. `docs/research/v0.9.23-pk3-dependency-probe.ts` walks
  MapLoader's dependency phases for a fresh session joining wdm2 and acidwdm2 in
  both orders: the pk3 is loaded before the shader scripts are read every time,
  every projectile model is found, and every glow shader resolves as additive.
- *Recycled entity ids.* `WorldState.generateEntityId` only counts up.
- *The client skipping snapshots.* There is no latest-only processing and no
  send-buffer skipping.

**Latent, not shown to matter.** `Q3ShaderRegistry.resolveMaterialProps` caches
its results and `loadShaders` does not clear them, so a name resolved before its
shader script loads stays unshaded for the session:
`docs/research/v0.9.23-projectile-texture-probe.ts` shows the grenade resolving
to `grenade.jpg` rather than its shader's `grenade.tga`. A normal join never
resolves a projectile shader that early.

**Remaining.** Untextured rockets and grenades, and bolts vanishing mid-game with
no weapon switch involved. Play-test the fixes and capture both warnings.

## I. World surfaces see-through after a map load

**Observed.** Right after joining, parts of the walls and ceiling did not draw
and the geometry behind them showed through. They appeared after a while.

**Confirmed.** Before gameplay entities spawn, map loading waits for
`scene.whenReadyAsync()`, which has no timeout, and the map's textures are
awaited before that, so the world was ready when that check passed. What runs
after it is not checked again: the FPS client's post-load work and the join
itself. A surface can also stop drawing later with no texture lost, because
Babylon skips a mesh while a changed material's shader compiles.

**Fixed alongside.** The overlay's first animation frame overwrote whatever state
it had reached, so a load that completed before that frame (a background tab runs
none) never faded out once its hold was released.

**Instrumented.** When the loading screen closes (`loading:hidden`), `MapLoader`
lists world meshes that are enabled and visible but not ready to draw, each with
what it is waiting on: a shader error, textures not ready or released, or the
material itself. No warning means the holes appear later; a warning names them.

**Remaining.** Capture the warning in a session that shows the holes, then decide
between holding the screen for readiness and fixing whatever changes the
materials.

## J. Camera rolls until the player moves forward

**Observed.** Without full focus, moving the mouse left the view rolled by tens of
degrees and lowered; pressing forward levelled it.

**Confirmed.** Only two things write camera roll in play: view effects (walk bob
and strafe lean, under two degrees) and the FPS controller's fallback death
camera, a 0.7rad roll with a 20u drop, which matches the screenshot. That
fallback applies only while the client believes the local player is dead.

**Does not fit.** `combat_state` carries the dead flag in every snapshot, and a
respawn needs fire or jump, not forward, so the report is not explained yet.

**Reproduced (2026-09-16).** It happens on returning to a backgrounded tab. The
first reading — a frozen frame keeping the roll it was drawn with — was wrong:
levelling on tab return changed nothing in play. The tilt survives looking
around and lifts only on pressing forward.

**What that rules out.** The controller sets roll to zero on every frame it runs,
so a tilt that survives is written after that point, by one of two writers. The
view effects are out by arithmetic: `cg_strafetilt` caps at 5° and `cg_bobroll`
at 2° against a 2× speed factor — about 9° together — and both return a neutral
delta while standing still, the walk bob below 10u/s and the strafe lean with no
strafe input. The observed tilt is larger than that and holds while stationary.

**What is left.** The death-camera fallback, `smooth * 0.7` radians: 40° at full
ramp, 15–20° partway. It writes roll absolutely on every frame its phase is above
zero, and that phase resets only when the local player is seen alive again.
`applyDeathCamera` levels the view for a spectator or a round past the first, but
a duel at round 0 falls through to the fallback. The flag it rests on is driven
purely by combat_state event edges gated on `localEntityId`, which resolves to
undefined whenever the local player is absent from the snapshot's player map —
and then nothing can clear it.

level. Which change ended it was never captured: the levelling on tab return was

## K. The player freezes mid-match and the enemy hangs in the air

**Observed.** In a duel against a bot, the player stopped moving and firing and
the enemy froze mid-jump, while the server went on ticking at 125Hz with the bot
moving.

**Confirmed, from the client and server logs.**

1. The server stopped receiving the player's messages at about command 19,066. It
   logs every 1,000 messages per connection, and afterwards only the bot's
   connection did, while the client went on counting commands sent past 20,500.
   Neither side logged an error or a close.
2. With no inputs arriving, the player's entity stood still with an empty input
   queue and its acknowledged input stayed at 19,066, so the client twice
   discarded its unacknowledged inputs to resynchronise, snapping back each time.
3. The server culls each client's snapshot by what is visible from that player's
   server-side position. Frozen there, the player stopped receiving the bot, which
   stayed where it was last seen: mid-jump.

**Ruled out, by reading the code.** An exception in the server's message handling:
dispatch is wrapped and counted zero errors, and nothing catches process-level
exceptions, so one escaping would have crashed the server. The command sequence
going backwards: the wire counter only increments. The anti-warp validator: its
rate cap resets every tick. A closed socket: the client would log sampled send
failures and the server a disconnect. A latency simulator: none is in the client
path.

**Instrumented.** The client warns after 2s without a snapshot, with whether its
socket is open and how many bytes wait to send, and logs an error after 8s, not
counting time it was hidden or blocked. The server warns when a playing client has
sent nothing for 2s, and when it is heard from again.

**Softened.** A dropped or stalled client no longer loses the match. The server
keeps its seat — record, entity, score, weapons and slot — for twenty seconds and
hands it back to the resume token issued at join; the client drops a stalled
connection itself, reopens it and claims the seat. A freeze is then seconds long
instead of permanent, and `src/games/fps/server/__tests__/SessionResume.test.ts`
drives that over a real socket against a real server.

**A neighbouring failure, now closed.** Every tick scheduled the next one at the
end of its own timer callback, so anything thrown in that callback ended the
loop: the simulation stops, the sockets stay open, and nothing further is logged.
That is not what happened here — this server went on ticking at 125Hz with the bot
moving — but it looks the same from the outside, and the silence monitor above
could itself throw on a client record with no connection. The loop now always
schedules its successor and names what failed, at most once per 5s.

**Remaining.** The stall itself is still unexplained, and resuming only hides it.
Capture both warnings, and the browser's WebSocket frames, in the next freeze.
