# v0.9.30 authored autosprite2 axes

## Finding

The previous BSP billboard path locked `autosprite2` to world Y and reconstructed
its vertices from axis-aligned width/height fractions. Qfusion instead finds a
quad's long axis from its first triangle, excluding the diagonal, and rotates
only the transverse coordinate around that axis.

Source verified in the local warfork-qfusion tree:
`source/ref_gl/r_vbo.c` (quad centre, short/right and long/up axes) and
`source/ref_gl/r_program.c`, `DEFORMV_AUTOSPRITE2` (projected camera direction,
transverse rotation and facing normal).

Of wfdm11's 35 autosprite2 surfaces, 31 have vertical axes and four `halo`
surfaces have horizontal axes. Those four were being rebuilt vertically.

## Implementation

- Cache the original positions and quad frames during BSP mesh construction.
- Restore source triangle order for axis extraction and account for the
  coordinate boundary's handedness when computing the new transverse axis.
- Preserve each quad's authored centre, longitudinal coordinates and vertex/UV
  association; do not regenerate a world-up rectangle.
- Compute a camera-facing normal perpendicular to the long axis.
- Refresh bounds with the rotated positions and retain the existing unchanged-
  camera, PVS visibility and map-owned callback behavior.
- Retain the previous orientation when the eye lies exactly on the rotation
  axis, avoiding an undefined normalized direction.

Full `autosprite` keeps its existing camera-facing geometry. The preceding
uncommitted cutout/blend fix remains intact; no asset or FPS submodule changed.

## Assumptions and limits

The requested correction concerns BSP autosprite2 surfaces. The map contains
four horizontal halos, corroborating the reported failure mode. This does not
add MD3 vertex deforms or alter full-autosprite sizing. Matched-camera Qfusion
pixel parity, live multiplayer and low-end performance were not measured.
Source Qfusion packs axes into lat/long attributes; this CPU path retains full
axis precision. Quad grouping follows Qfusion's six-index pairs of triangles.
