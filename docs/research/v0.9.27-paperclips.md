# v0.9.27 Paperclips: four modes, made well

**Status:** v0.9.27 closed 2026-10-10. WO-P1 continues in v0.9.28: learned routes load and route, but bots do not yet choose them in play (see the last log entry).

## Goal

1.0 is four gametypes, each built for one audience. Make those four excellent
rather than adding more:

| Mode | Audience | What a good match feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Duel** | Competitive 1v1 | Item control and timing decide it; fair spawns; a clear winner, never a draw |
| **Amphi** | Casual and competitive; aim training | Short, readable rounds in a square arena; straight back into the next one |
| **FFA** | New players; "no-brain shooting fun" | Constant action, quick respawns, no punishment for being new |
| **SnD** | Competitive teams | Rounds decided by plants, defuses and trades, not by the clock |

Players, new ones especially, meet bots first. A mode that plays badly against
bots plays badly for new players, so bot behaviour in each mode is part of that
mode's quality.

## Method

1. Read the four mode classes, `MatchState`, `GametypeController`, the lobby
   mode list and `config/maps.json`.
2. Played headless bot matches in every mode with `npm run demo:record`, then
   read the recorded demos with throwaway scripts (fire, damage, death and
   match-state events; player positions over time). Bots ran at the default
   difficulty unless noted.
3. Compared the match settings with the original gametype scripts in
   `data0_21pure.pk3` (`progs/gametypes/*.as`) as a reference point, not a spec.

## Findings

### Tooling (fixed during the audit: WO-P0)

- `demo:record` had no `amphi` case, so `--gametype amphi` silently ran
  Deathmatch.
- It started the match by calling `matchState.transition(Active)` directly.
  That skipped `onMatchPhaseChange` and `onRoundStart`. Amphi never marked its
  round live, so it counted no frags and extended a 16–7 round into overtime
  three times. S&D skipped its first round's setup (later rounds start through
  the normal freeze). Bot recordings of round modes made before this fix are
  not reliable.

Both are fixed in `src/cli/record-demo.ts`: the tool now runs out the warmup
timer and lets the match start the normal way.

### SnD — not playable against bots (severity: blocker)

| Map | Bots | Shots fired | Rounds | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| wbomb2 | 4 | **0** | 3 | 2 timeouts, 1 detonation |
| wbomb6 | 6 | **0** | 2 | 2 timeouts |
| wbomb6 | 4 | **0** | 2 | 2 timeouts |

- Nobody fired a shot in 12 minutes of SnD across three matches. Bot status
  lines (`SHAG_DEBUG=bot`) show `E:0 A:1` throughout: each bot sees its
  teammate and never an enemy. Enemies are culled from snapshots by PVS, which
  is correct, so the teams simply never come within sight of each other.
- Positions explain it. On wbomb6 both attackers walk about 600 units from
  spawn and stand at the same spot (176, 136, 528) for 80 seconds. Both
  defenders stop near their own spawn and hop in place. Nav status reports the
  plant and defend goals as `unreachable`.
- Kits are fine: every bot spawns with its chosen weapons.
- One bomb was planted and detonated in three wbomb2 rounds; none on wbomb6.

The rules code (`SearchAndDestroy.ts`, 1,147 lines, 5 test files) looks
thorough. What fails is bots getting from spawn to the sites.

### Amphi — rules work; the arena hurts (severity: high)

- With WO-P0 the match plays correctly: frags decide each round, and it ended
  2–0 for the player who won both rounds on frags.
- On wamphi1, 13 of 72 deaths in the match were to the poison around the arena
  (16 of 55 and 18 of 36 in the two earlier runs). **By design:** players drop
  from the ledges into an arena surrounded by poison, and fights are meant to
  turn chaotic. Not a bug.
- A round is 3 minutes with respawns, plus 60 s overtime on a tie, best of
  three; the match took about 6 minutes. **Decided: this is the right shape**
  for casual blasting and aim training.
- The lobby description, "1v1 deathmatch, best of three 3-minute rounds", is
  accurate but doesn't tell a player why to pick it.
- The lobby hides Amphi unless its map pk3s are on disk (`wamphi1`, `wda1`,
  `wda2`). That's correct, but the release must ship them.

### Duel — sound; bots decide the experience (severity: medium)

- Rules match the competitive expectation: 10 minutes, no score limit,
  overtime instead of a draw, furthest spawn, map weapons and items.
- wdm2 (hard bots, 3 min): 9–7, 20 deaths, 70 hits, rockets did the most
  damage. A real duel.
- wdm12 (default bots, 2 min): 0–0. 189 shots, 7 hits, no frags. The bots
  barely met, and most shots (166 voltaic) were fired with no one hit.
- Default difficulty decides what a new duelist sees. On a larger map it's an
  empty arena.
- Respawn waits 3 s minimum, 6 s automatic. **Decided: keep.**
- The original duel has a challenger queue (winner stays, next in line plays);
  ours relies on the lobby. Fine for lobby matches; a question for a public
  server.

### FFA — works; tune for its audience (severity: medium)

- wdm2 with 4 bots: 57 deaths in 161 s, everyone scoring. Healthy action.
- Settings: 20-frag or 10-minute limit, 3 s minimum respawn wait (6 s
  automatic), full self-damage, a kit choice before joining. **Decided: keep.**
  Core rules (self-damage, fall damage, movement) are the same in every mode;
  no mode softens them.
- Naming: the lobby says **FFA**, but the mode reports itself as
  "Deathmatch" in match state, the HUD feed and demo file names. A new player
  should see one name.

### Cross-cutting

- **Bots are the first opponent in every mode**, and the weakest part of the
  roster. They work in Duel on wdm2, in FFA and in Amphi, rarely meet on wdm12,
  and are stuck in SnD.
- **There's no quick way to check a mode.** A `demo:summary` tool (scores,
  rounds, deaths by cause, hits, positions over time) would make every finding
  above a single command, and catch regressions. The throwaway audit scripts
  are the starting point.
- **TeamDeathmatch** still exists on the server and in the dev CLIs but is
  not in the lobby. **Decided:** it stays as a dev leftover and a base for
  possible future team modes; not part of the 1.0 release.
- Duel, Amphi and SnD have tests; FFA (Deathmatch) has none of its own.

## Decisions (2026-10-10)

1. **Amphi** stays as it is: 3-minute rounds, best of three. The poison around
   the arena is part of the challenge.
2. **Respawn wait** stays at 3 s minimum, 6 s automatic.
3. **FFA** keeps full self-damage and 20 frags / 10 minutes. Core rules are the
   same in every mode: no mode turns off fall or self-damage.
4. **TeamDeathmatch** stays in code as a dev leftover, outside the release.

## Implementation log

- **2026-10-10, WO-P0.** `src/cli/record-demo.ts`: `amphi` case added; the
  match now starts by expiring the warmup timer instead of jumping to Active.
  Verified: Amphi on wamphi1 now scores rounds on frags (2–0); SnD runs its
  round setup (bomb picked up, planted and detonated on wbomb2).

- **2026-10-10, WO-P1 (part 1: walking).** The S&D stall was not S&D code.
  The bots' navigation graph could not route from any spawn to either bomb
  site on wbomb6 (127 disconnected pieces), and the cause hits every map:
  - Walk links needed a straight sweep between grid nodes 256 units apart, at
    most 40 units of rise. Stairs and ramps that climb further between nodes,
    a crate in a corridor, or a doorway off the line left no link. Climbs were
    only possible as rare simulated standing jumps; drops only as falls with
    line of sight.
  - The sweep passed when blocked over its last tenth (`fraction > 0.9`): on
    wbomb2 a 1,460-unit "walk" edge ended in a wall 146 units short, and the
    attackers stood against it for the rest of the round. It also checked no
    floor beneath, and its box reached 88 units above the floor, so doorways
    a player fits through read as walls.

  Fixes (`src/bot/BotNavGraph.ts`, `src/bot/BotVision.ts`,
  `src/shared/collision/WalkFlood.ts`):
  - A walk flood, moved out of the SHAGGED player-reach check into shared
    code, links each node to the neighbours within 400 units that the
    player's own movement (`stepSlideMove`, 32-unit steps, drops short of fall
    damage) actually reaches, one direction at a time. SHAGGED's reach report
    uses the same code.
  - Straight walk links need a wholly clear standing-hull sweep and floor
    every 64 units.

  Reach from a spawn (median nodes reachable, before → after): wdm2 34 → 55
  of 97, wdm12 39 → 72 of 158, wbomb2 213 → 256 of 386, wbomb6 27 → 205 of
  650; wamphi1 73 → 68 (false links removed). A default-difficulty bot Duel on
  wdm12 went from 0–0 (7 hits in 2 minutes) to 5–4 (27 hits, 11 deaths).
  Graph build time grows (wbomb6 about 2 minutes); it is cached per map.

  Still open after part 1: the wdm2 Red Armor, the wbomb2 attackers'
  corridor, and wbomb6's sites. User's map knowledge (2026-10-10): wbomb2's
  attackers leave by a jump pad; wbomb6 has jump pads and one staircase, no
  doors; the wdm2 Red Armor is one wall jump off the wall above the teleporter
  in the upper atrium (or, advanced, a jump off the boxes below and a wall
  jump onto the ledge).

- **2026-10-10, WO-P1 (part 2: jump pads, route ends).**
  - **Jump pads landed in mid-air.** A pad's destination node sat at its
    `target_position`, the aim point of the throw. On wbomb2 and wbomb6 most
    pads throw straight up, so that point hangs over the pad with nothing to
    walk to. The graph now flies each pad with the game's movement, steering
    in 16 directions, and gives it one destination node per landing on the
    level above that a flight steered at it also reaches (2–3 per pad). The
    traversal sequence steers at the edge's landing instead of the aim point
    and counts landing there as arrived.
  - **Routes ended on the wrong node.** `findPath` took the nearest node to
    the target by straight distance. A bomb site's indicator floats over its
    floor, and the nearest node was an item on a ledge beside it that nothing
    walks to. The goal is now the nearest node within 256 units that the
    route can reach. The start is the nearest node the bot can walk straight
    to from where it stands (height counting double), so a bot thrown onto a
    barricade no longer starts its route on the far side of a wall.

  Result (4 bots, 5 minutes each):

  | Map | Before WO-P1 | Now |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | wbomb2 | 0 shots, 0 plants | 538 shots; planted and armed in 3 of 4 rounds |
  | wbomb6 | 0 shots, 0 plants | 264 shots; a plant; rounds won on eliminations |

  Every spawn on wbomb2 reaches 277–279 of 389 nodes; both teams route to both
  sites on both maps.

  Next: the wdm2 Red Armor wall jump (technique search, with the user's route
  as the seed case), and a scripted-input queue so bots replay a found script
  one command per server tick.

- **2026-10-10, WO-P1 (part 3: wdm2 Red Armor, in progress).**
  - The bot client can now replay a script: `BotInput.script` carries one
    input per server tick, and the client sends one per tick, catching up
    after late timer firings (at most 4 at once, under the server's 6-command
    buffer). The server consumes one command per tick, in order, so the replay
    matches an offline rollout.
  - Two brute-force searches (32,400 and 43,200 rollouts of running jumps,
    wall jumps and slide jumps) found no way to the Red Armor. Cross-sections
    show why the first search could not: the niche (floor 432, x −812..−652,
    z 128..216, roof 608) is open only to the north, over the lower atrium
    (floor about 150). A wall separates it from the upper corridor holding
    teleporter 1, where the search stood. Which wall face the user's route
    kicks off is not readable from collision data alone.
  - Proposed next: **routes taught by demo.** The user records the trick;
    a tool cuts the inputs from takeoff to landing out of the demo (or the
    exact per-tick `.shagverify` bundle), re-simulates them to verify they
    land, and stores them as a movement link in the map's navigation data.
    Bots then replay them with the script queue. The same tool covers every
    trick a map has, without a search per trick.

- **2026-10-10, WO-P1 (part 4: `training_demo`).** The user's suggestion: wrap
  `map` so recording a trick is one console command.
  - `training_demo <map> [name] [gametype]` launches like `map`;
    `training_demo stop` leaves, and the server saves
    `public/data/demos/training/<map>_<name>.shagdemo` as the last human goes.
  - The training server stays in warmup (`SHAG_WARMUP=0`), starts recording

  - Plumbing: `GameplayMapLaunchOptions.trainingDemo` → `LobbyClient.spawnDevServer`
    → `dev:spawn_server.trainingDemo` (validated: letters, digits, `_`, `-`)

  - Verified headless: a client joined a `--training-demo e2e` server on wdm2
    for 8 s and left; the server saved `wdm2_e2e.shagdemo`, 1,002 frames at
    125 Hz.
  - Next: the learning tool that turns a training demo into a movement link.

  - Why bots skipped the Red Armor, from the scorer's own traces: weapons

    never the blind jump); new guns past a working pair are worth 0.5, then
    0.3; per-mode `majorItemBias` in the weight profiles (Duel 1.8, FFA 1.0,
    S&D 0.7, Amphi 0.5) and a new frags-first Amphi profile, per the user's
    direction for each mode.
  - Live, 4-minute bot Duel on wdm2: a bot took the Red Armor 3 times through
    the learned wall jump (301 frames on the ledge).
  - Next: Duel item timing — bots knowing when the armors and Mega come back
