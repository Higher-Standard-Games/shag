# v0.9.29: demo player tuning

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-13. **Source:** engine `4544f1e2`, FPS `9400513`.
**Status:** DP01–DP06, ragdolls and the watched player's own motion done.

The goal: a demo shows what the player saw while playing, one to one — the same
first-person camera, the same HUD, the same smoothness — before any new playback
features.

## How playback works now

| Part | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frames | `DemoViewer` | Steps recorded snapshots (the server's snapshot rate) by a playhead in recorded ticks; play, pause, speed, seek. |
| Entities | `DemoEntityTimeline` via `RemotePlayerRenderer.getDemoEntitySample` | Interpolates position, yaw and velocity between the two frames around the playhead. |
| Camera | `SpectatorController` on `fpsCamera`, fed by `DemoService`'s target provider | Interpolated position and yaw; pitch from the latest raw frame; walk bob and lean from `DemoPovState`. |
| Events | `DemoService` | Its own handling of chat, weapon fire, voltaic beams, deaths, damage and awards. The live game's `GameEvents` handles about thirty event types. |
| HUD | `DemoViewerHUD` | Transport bar, a player list, and one line of the followed player's name, health, armor and weapon. The game's `FPSHud` is not started. |
| Overlays | `DemoOverlayController` | Transport bar, cinematic HUD and chat; `H` shows or hides them all. |

## Findings

| ID | Problem | Evidence | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DP01 | The first-person camera tilted sideways in the air, up to 29°. Babylon applies roll through the up vector and rebuilt it only on frames where the roll changed. The walk bob's roll drops to zero at a jump, so the up vector kept that frame's pitch, and turning in the air swung it to the side. The live game's camera has the same roll path (`FPSApplicator`). | Browser probe: wdm17, 1073 of 3703 frames tilted over 3°; wamphi1 334 of 3954; after the fix 0 of 3661 and 0 of 3961. | S, **fixed** |
| DP02 | No game HUD in demos. Health, armor, ammo, weapon slots, timer, frags, killfeed, crosshair, damage indicators, death screen — none of it shows; only a line of text. | `FPSHud` starts only in `Game`'s network path; `DemoService._updateDemoHud` feeds only `DemoViewerHUD`. | M–L |
| DP03 | Demos handle six of the game's event types in a parallel copy, so anything the HUD and effects learn from other events never reaches a demo. | `DemoService` event loop against `GameEvents`. | M |
| DP04 | Pitch is not interpolated: looking up and down steps at the snapshot rate while position and yaw are smooth. | The target provider reads `transform.rotation.x` from the latest frame; `DemoEntityTimeline` carries no pitch. | S |
| DP05 | The transport bar is unstyled: none of the game's look. | `DemoViewerHUD` against the HUD's Washiki theme. | S–M |
| DP06 | Watching someone is done three ways: demos follow with `SpectatorController` fed by `DemoService`, live spectating with `SpectateView` and `SpectateEyeCamera` from the raw latest snapshot, and the local player with `FPSController`. Each rebuilds the first-person view on its own. | The three providers. | M, with DP02 |

## Proposed order

1. **DP04:** interpolate pitch with the rest of the pose.
2. **DP02 + DP03:** run demo frames through the live game's event path with the
   followed player as the viewed entity — the path live spectating already
   uses — and start `FPSHud` for playback. The HUD shows by default; the
   transport bar gets a HUD switch.
3. **DP05:** the transport bar in the game's style.

## Done

- DP02, DP03: demo frames go through the game's own snapshot path —
  `storeSnapshotState`, then `handleServerStateUpdate` — with the followed
  player as the spectated one. `DemoService` builds a `GameEventContext` over
  its own systems (renderer, followed viewmodel, demo-clocked gibs and hit
  numbers, projectile bookkeeping, `playback: "demo"`) and lost its parallel
  copy (some 250 lines). A seek's landing frame passes `presentOneShots:
  false`: state applies, the moments do not. The game's `FPSHud` runs watching
  (`init({ watchOnly })`: no chat input, menus or lobby return), on by
  default with a HUD switch on the transport bar; the crosshair follows the
  followed player's weapon. Live spectating gains on the way: the award banner
  follows the watched player.
- Found on the way: the `filterBlend` and glow overlay shaders used the fog's
  `vAtmWorld` in their fragment stage without declaring it, so decals and glow
  surfaces failed to compile on maps that have them (wdm17). Fixed, and
  `AtmosphericFogVaryings.test.ts` checks every fogged shader stage.

- DP05: the playback bar is rebuilt in the HUD's look (ink plate with
  scanlines, gold play keycap, ink chips, the followed player in gold, Orbitron
  time) from its own stylesheet, and fades away 2.5 s after the mouse or a key
  last moved while a demo plays — paused, hovered or dragging, it stays. The
  collapse button it had is gone; fading does its job.

- DP04: the snapshot entity carries the view pitch (`EntitySnapshot.pitch`,
  from the transform), and both interpolators — the demo timeline and the live
  snapshot buffer — interpolate it with the position and yaw. On wdm17 the
  demo camera's pitch now moves on 1945 of 1962 frames where it moved on 1398
  of 1473, stepping at the snapshot rate.

- DP01: `fpsCamera` and the cameras `FPSController` and `SpectatorController`
  attach rebuild their up vector from their rotation every frame
  (`updateUpVectorFromRotation`). Test: `CameraRoll.test.ts` reproduces the
  tilt with Babylon's default and holds the view level with the fix.

- DP06: one first-person view for every view behind someone's eyes. The
  movement effects of the local camera (walk bob, landing dip, strafe lean,
  speed FOV, idle breathing) are one list, `createFirstPersonEffects`; the
  local camera registers it, and a watched view runs its own copy
  (`FirstPersonEffectStack`) inside `DemoPovState`, on the demo playhead or on
  wall time, so the watched screen moves by the same code rather than a
  rebuilt bob. `watchedEyePose` makes the pose — the interpolated sample the
  player is drawn with, their eye height, those effects — for the demo viewer,
  live chase spectating and the dead-teammate view alike, and
  `applyFirstPersonPose` writes it through the local camera's `FPSApplicator`.
  The two live views read the latest raw snapshot and smoothed it on their
  own (two different smoothings); they now take the drawn sample, so the view
  stays on the interpolation clock the world around it is drawn on, and
  neither camera smooths. Measured: on wdm17 demo playback the camera sits
  within 0.9 units of the drawn player across 3851 frames (the bob), landings
  dip it 2–5 units, the speed FOV reaches 1.29×; live chase of a bot, within
  0.94 units over 2418 frames, FOV to 1.10×; no tilted frame in either.

- Ragdolls: deaths that ragdoll in the game ragdoll in a demo, where they
  burst into gibs because the demo had no ragdoll system. Playback owns a
  `RagdollSystem.create` instance, as it owns its gibs, and hands the game's
  death path to it. Ragdoll ages run on the time `update()` is handed (frame
  time live, the playhead in a demo) instead of `performance.now()`, and the
  demo steps Havok itself by the playhead's time with the scene's own
  stepping off for the length of playback, so a paused demo holds every body
  still and slow motion lets them fall slowly. A seek clears them, as it
  clears gibs. Browser check: a death in wdm17 left a ragdoll that stood
  exactly still over a second of pause and moved on resume; leaving playback
  hands the scene its stepping back.

- A watched player's own motion, live and recorded. Each snapshot carries a
  player's view behind bit 7 of the movement byte (`BinaryProtocol`): their
  keys, recovered on the server from the wish direction and yaw
  (`movementKeys`), the slide and ledge grab phases, and the exact crouch
  view offset, which was never on the wire before (a watched crouch snapped
  by the guessed 24 units). `DemoPovState` feeds the keys to the effects, so
  the strafe lean runs, adds the slide dip and ledge lift, and eases the eye
  up steps; the formulas are `FirstPersonMotion`, which the local camera now
  uses too. Demo format v5 marks recordings with it; v4 and older still play
  without. Browser: on a fresh v5 duel demo every followed frame had keys,
  398 frames strafed (lean to 1.81°), 347 slid (dip to 0.22 rad), 208
  ledge-grabbed and 243 showed a mid-crouch eye; live chase of a bot, keys
  and crouch eye on all 1851 frames, lean to 1.84°.
