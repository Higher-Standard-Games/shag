# v1.0.0-alpha.5

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

- World materials stay unfrozen through shader warmup, reflections and final
  scene readiness, then freeze for gameplay. Explicit preparation also unfreezes
  an already frozen material until its replacement effect compiles. A pending
  PBR shader could otherwise inherit the previous draw's ready flag and crash
  the render loop, leaving a white game view.

- Texture decoders discard results whose destination was disposed during a
  timeout or map change, and remove retired loads from scene readiness tracking.
  This prevents late KTX2 uploads into released GPU textures. Obsolete sidecar
  requests also stop before starting a source fallback in a replacement map.

- Loading operations carry cancellation and identity through scene cleanup,
  map loading and match/demo preparation. Older loads cannot dismiss a newer
  loading screen or repopulate cleared pickup caches. Required collision,
  player models and combat models are checked before opening play; normal
  demo Watch uses the same held-weapon/projectile preparation as live matches.
- GPU preparation pauses its timeout budget while the tab is hidden or frozen.
  Returning checks healthy resources without forcing another full shader
  recompile. Context/device loss reports a retryable load failure.
- Package persistence queues archive identities behind its bounded memory cache,
  fixing downloads skipped when two disk writes were pending. Cache writes keep
  an existing valid entry until replacement succeeds. Optional texture metadata,
  sidecar preparation and audio have bounded waits; audio begins alongside the
  map load instead of after it.
- Generated manifests reference an immutable PK3 index payload. Concurrent
  index requests share work, and an incomplete published update retries or keeps
  the last coherent snapshot instead of scanning every archive.

- Map shader warmup and hidden-tab recovery have cancellable readiness checks,
  bounded frame waits and cleanup that restores frozen materials after errors.
  A shader or stopped render loop could previously leave loading stuck forever.
  Required scene-readiness failure now reports a failed map load instead of
  entering the match with materials still unable to draw.
- Optional browser-cache reads and writes cannot hold archive downloads forever.
  Validated packages are usable before disk writes finish; a bounded write queue
  limits retained download bodies. Cached index fallback preserves content hashes.
- Textures acquire their indexed archive before attempting resolution, and
  concurrent texture requests and ZIP-entry reads share work. Indexed failures
  no longer fall through to loose-file requests. Independent map dependencies
  use the existing two-download scheduler concurrently.
- Water highlight preparation uses a spatial triangle index instead of scanning
  the world's triangles for every visibility ray, reducing a measured multi-second
  loading freeze without changing the intended blockers or light-mask sampling.
- Depth capture restores Babylon's framebuffer state on every exit, fixing a
  reproduced WebGL feedback-loop error during startup/demo rendering.
  Map changes also reject disposed shared depth captures, and water replaces
  unavailable capture samplers rather than retaining dead textures that prevent
  its material from becoming ready.
- Held-weapon and projectile preparation have bounded, cancellable readiness
  waits. Missing required projectile models fail preparation instead of silently
  reporting ready.

- Loadout weapon previews read packaged models through the shared asset loader,
  fixing missing previews and loose GLB requests that returned 404 in production.

- Packaged weapon models without optional attachment overrides no longer make
  repeated loose `tags.txt` requests that return 404. Custom loose models retain
  their sidecar lookup.

- Regional deployment follows engine `main` again, using that commit's matching
  game revision. Successful updates automatically restart both backends even
  with connected players. Explicit pinned pairs remain available for rollback.

- The winner on the end-of-match screen shows its armour and detail again. It
  was drawn with a forced full-bright glow and outline, which washed the model
  into a flat shape of the player's colour. It keeps the colour, lit by a light
  of its own; players in a match look as before.

- The Play page has a speaker key beside **Credits** that turns the lobby's
  music off and on. It stays pressed down while the music is off, match music
  still plays, and the browser remembers the choice. Before, the only way to
  quiet the lobby was turning all music off in the settings.

- **The weapon package is 4.9 MB instead of 86 MB, and weapons have their normal
  maps.** The package shipped the whole original asset bank (Warsow-era TGA,
  KTX and IQM files the game never loads) beside the GLB models, plus retired
  models such as the gunblade. It now carries only what the game loads. Every
  texture is WebP, at most 1024 px. The weapons gain the normal and gloss maps
  their conversion had left out, so panel lines, bevels and worn metal show on
  every gun. The Voltaic's model now lives under `voltaic/`; `lasergun` stays an
  alias. Findings are in `docs/research/weapon-pack-alpha5.md`.

- **Map texture downloads are 34 MB instead of 181 MB.** Each map's texture
  bank (`tex_<map>.pk3`) shipped its TGA and PNG textures as copied from the
  master bank; washoi_dm5's alone was 59 MB. They are now WebP, with the
  quality chosen per texture (normal maps, sky boxes and cut-out edges get
  more), from a list in `config/map-texture-encoding.json`. The loader finds a
  texture's WebP form first, so shaders naming `.tga` files keep working. The
  KTX2 sidecars that draw most textures are unchanged and still encoded from the
  lossless master. Findings are in `docs/research/map-texture-webp-alpha5.md`.

- The cartoon hit-effect package is `washoi_effects.pk3`, named like the game's
  other packages; it was `effects.pk3`. Its contents are unchanged.
