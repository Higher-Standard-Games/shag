# v1.0.0-alpha.10

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### Firefox frame drops in fights

Firefox players saw the frame rate collapse during fights, worst when they were
hit. Profiling Firefox 159 on a recorded duel found two causes.

- **The damage flash** redrew a full-screen 2D canvas, gradients and up to 60
  triangles per hit, on every frame while it faded. It is now a red vignette
  and a glow on the screen edge facing the damage, painted once and faded by
  the browser's compositor, so a hit costs almost no frame time. In a Firefox
  window, frames over 33 ms in the 400 ms after a hit fell from 18 to 6, against
  5 with no flash at all. The flash no longer draws triangles.
- **Shaders compiled again mid-match.** Firefox lacks
  `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`, so each shader program it links stops the
  frame for 150–300 ms. Switching weapons disposed the view model's materials
  and their shaders with them, and switching back compiled them again. Compiled
  shaders now stay cached for the session (`Effect.PersistentMode`), so
  anything rebuilt with the same shaders finds them ready.
- **Shaders compiled in play that the loading screen should have built.** The
  weapon and player warm-up compiled variants play never draws: with no active
  camera between frames they lacked the perspective-camera define, the player
  model was never warmed at all, and a weapon mesh drawn with the default
  material was skipped. Switching to the blade also built two variants, because
  its light was turned off for every gun and the new blade was drawn once
  before the light came back. The warm-up now compiles with the camera play
  uses, warms a throwaway player model, and includes default-material meshes;
  the blade's light is always on and reaches only the blade.
- **Effects compiled on first use.** The blade's slash trail, shotgun pellets,
  floating damage numbers and the teleport afterimage each built their shader
  the first time a fight drew them, 17–38 ms apiece. They are now compiled
  behind the loading screen through the same code that draws them; the
  teleport afterimage was loaded there before but not compiled.

On the demo first profiled, frames over 80 ms fell from ten to none. On it, on
an FFA on the same map, and on a duel and an S&D round on two other maps, no
shader compiles after the loading screen.

### Faster rendering in fights

The game is limited by the browser main thread rather than the graphics card on
most machines, and fights added the most work: every effect drew separately.

- **Wall marks, shotgun pellets and gibs are batched.** Each was a mesh and a
  draw of its own, and each pellet had its own material too: fifty wall marks
  were fifty draws, a shotgun blast twenty, a death twenty-eight for eight
  seconds. Each kind is now one mesh drawn once with all its copies (a gib
  shade twice, solid and fading), so a fight adds a handful of draws.
- **Hidden teleport afterimages stop animating.** The four pooled ghosts played
  their idle clip while unused, which was over half of all animation work.

Uncapped on a recorded duel, the frame rate rose by 7 to 9%, and the slowest
5% of frames got about 10% faster. These batches are
also compiled behind the loading screen, as the instanced shaders they draw
with.

### New maps: washoi_amphi2 and washoi_snd6 (Depot)

Two maps from the Warfork map sources, compiled with 2048 × 2048 lightmaps and
full lighting:

- **`washoi_amphi2`** (`wda5`, "Octagon Obsession") joins the Amphi pool. Its
  nine wall pipes come from a 2013 model the current sources lack. Its signs are
  Washoi's: an ammo board, a red WASHOI neon and a slim vertical neon that shows
  the whole of its art.
- **`washoi_snd6`** (`wbomb6`, Depot) joins the S&D pool, with Warfork's
  levelshot. A street lamp's glow and the end of a plate strip that showed a
  developer marker texture now draw their intended materials.
- **Collision as the mapper made it.** These maps date from the old compiler,
  where placed models had no collision unless marked solid and clip brushes did
  the rest. The new compiler made every model solid, which left invisible walls
  in the open: pipes above Amphi2's tower jump pad stopped every jump. Models
  not marked solid have no collision again; crates and props stay solid through
  the original clip brushes.
- **Amphi1's red "WF" neon** now spells WASHOI.
- **Dirt decals blend.** Depot's tower showed its dirt decal as solid olive
  panels: the decal's material used a template the game did not expand, so it
  had no blending at all. Material templates now work as in Warsow: any material
  can be one, and a `template` line expands wherever it stands in a material.

Each map has its own texture bank (`tex_washoi_amphi2.pk3`,
`tex_washoi_snd6.pk3`); their 273 new files and 52 shader definitions are merged into
the `washoi_textures.pk3` master. The new packages and the rebuilt master and
billboard bank must be published with the release. Build record:
[amphi2-snd6-packaging-2026-11-02.md](../research/amphi2-snd6-packaging-2026-11-02.md).

### Steadier frames in fights

A second pass over the frame, on a recorded four-player FFA on `washoi_dm2`,
uncapped: the mean rose from 196–202 to 232–241 fps, frames over the 165 Hz
budget (6.06 ms) fell from 27–29% to 10–12%, and the slowest frame from 41–47
to 26–30 ms. The full matrix is in
[the rendering performance audit](../research/render-perf-audit-2026-11-02.md).

- **Hidden meshes cost nothing.** Each camera skipped disabled meshes only
  after Babylon had checked them; they are two thirds of the scene (model
  templates and pooled effects). They are now left out before that check.
- **Guns are reused.** Other players' guns, and weapons dropped on the floor,
  were cloned on every switch, death, respawn and drop, 2–12 ms each, a few
  times a second in a fight. They are now parked and reused. One gun of each
  kind is built behind the loading screen, and every weapon's world model is
  preloaded, not only the ones placed on the map.
- **Cheaper copies of a model.** A clone takes its source's bounds instead of
  posing every vertex on the CPU to measure itself: switching your own weapon
  costs half as much.
- **Sounds and the death camera.** Each positioned sound ran its own update
  every frame; it no longer does. The camera watching a dead player traces the
  collision brushes instead of every map triangle. The moon light, unused on
  maps, is off rather than dark.
- **Batches upload their count first.** A new gib, pellet or wall mark no longer
  draws one frame of stale data.
- **`perf capture` names the GPU** the browser draws with, and the render size.

### Electrobolt ammo counter

The digits floated above the sight's holo pane. The new sight moved the pane
and the counter's mount down, but the weapon's animation still held the mount
at its old height. The animation now matches; `washoi_weapons.pk3` is rebuilt
and must be published with the release.

### Ultralow video preset

A fourth preset below Smooth, for the most frames on slow machines: half render
resolution, no antialiasing, no dynamic lights, gibs, wall marks, teleport
afterimages, fog or outlines, and map surfaces in flat colour (their lighting
kept). It is the only
preset that turns outlines off; the others turn its other switches back on but
leave outlines as they are. On a recorded duel it ran from 21% to more than
twice as fast as Balanced.

Each switch it uses is also its own setting and cvar: render resolution
(`r_renderscale`), antialiasing (`r_msaa`, next start), dynamic lights
(`r_dynamiclight`, next map; off, no material carries the dynamic-light
cluster at all), gibs (`r_gibs`), wall marks (`r_marks`), the teleport
afterimage (`r_teleport_ghost`) and fog (`r_fog`). With gibs off, a gib death
still pulls the view back from where the body was.

### Loading screen

- **Shader compiling shows its progress.** After the map has loaded, the
  loading screen used to say only "Setting up" while the game prepared its
  weapons and effects. That step now reads "Compiling shaders" with a
  percentage, and the progress bar moves through its last stretch with it.
- **No match sounds before the loading screen.** The server streams the match,
  shots and footsteps included, from the moment it greets the client, but the
  loading screen muted them only once the map load had started, so up to a
  second of a running match (bots firing, often) played first. Sounds are now
  muted from the greeting until the loading screen is gone.

### Watching a player die

Spectating someone, or watching a demo, the view stayed behind the player's
eyes when they died: at the body, inside the weapon they dropped at their feet,
which filled the screen with a huge, garbled gun. The dead often have no
position in the snapshots, so the view simply held there. It now pulls back to
the same chase of the body that your own death shows, behind and above it and
kept out of walls, and returns to their eyes when they respawn.

### New soundtrack song

A new song, "Noodlesoup Telefragged Me", is in the soundtrack. It plays in the
in-game playlist, and the lobby now picks one of two songs at random each
session, this one or "Washiki Washoi".

### Faster strafing

Base ground speed is 320 units per second, up from 280: strafing,
back-pedalling, diagonals and running forward without sprint. Sprinting
straight forward stays at 400, and weapon speed bonuses apply on top as before
(the Blade strafes at 330). Swimming, which is half the base speed, is 160.
Crouching at the new speed still walks: a slide needs 375. Recorded demos play
at the speeds they were recorded with.
