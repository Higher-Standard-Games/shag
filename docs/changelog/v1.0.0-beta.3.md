# v1.0.0-beta.3

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### WebGPU whole-frame snapshots (experimental, `?snapshot=1`)

On WebGPU, `?snapshot=1` in the page address records a frame's render passes
once and replays them on the frames that follow. While the scene's draw list
holds, a replayed frame skips Babylon's mesh selection and material binding.
Before each replayed frame the controller writes what changes in place:
moved meshes, skeletons, effect batch counts, outline and shader-material
matrices, and light uniforms. On the dm2 FFA demo, 87% of frames replay, and
frame rate is ×1.5 against WebGPU without snapshots. The design and
measurements are in `docs/research/webgpu-snapshot-plan-2026-11-03.md`.

The first build froze players, projectiles and beams in replayed frames. Their
per-draw matrices, light directions, and the collapse of just-hidden meshes
were written where the recorded draws did not read them. These now update
every replayed frame.

Material animation stood still in replayed frames as well: explosions, sparks,
beams and water stopped at their recorded time, scrolling Quake stages
stopped, and fades, pickup pulses and the weapon glow held. The controller now
learns which materials change from frame to frame and binds those again in
replayed frames, at about 4% of a replayed frame's time.

Sprites and projectiles lingered for a frame after they were gone: the draw
list was compared after a replayed frame had already drawn. It is now
compared before the frame draws. Weapon parts with a distance level of
detail no longer force new recordings on every weapon switch. Learning and
rebinding material values were made cheaper. On the dm11 FFA demo, snapshots
now run at 172 fps against 110 fps without.

Trails and hit sprites that were gone still flickered back at their last
position a few times a second: each recording frame drew the hidden objects
it has to include where they last were. They now draw collapsed in that frame.

Recordings, the slow frames under snapshots, are cheaper and rarer:
recordings reuse each draw's cached bundle, the controller waits longer after
a recording that replayed only a frame or two, and pickup and teleport bursts
are pooled instead of created and disposed per use. After a minute of the
dm11 FFA demo, snapshots run at 177 fps against 94 fps without.

Walls and map models could vanish for a moment while moving, showing the
rooms behind: map surfaces that the visibility culling hid and showed again
were left collapsed in replayed frames until the next recording.

Every pool map was played with snapshots on and compared against normal
frames. Two differed: the dm12 jump pad, whose cycling images stood still,
and the amphi1 slime, which the water drew without depth because it saw no
liquid in replayed frames. Both now draw as without snapshots.

Snapshots stay opt-in for now.

### First weapon pickups and hits no longer stall (WebGPU)

The first switch to each weapon, the first player in view, the first damage
number, gib, rocket or pickup burst each stalled a frame: WebGPU builds a
render pipeline on a mesh's first draw, and Firefox builds it synchronously
(the first electrobolt took 443 ms there). The loader compiled those materials
but drew none of them, because they are hidden until play needs them. It now
draws them behind the loading screen: each view weapon for two frames, the
player warm-up model with its outline, and every hidden mesh in the scene
(parked weapons, pooled effects, projectile models, damage numbers, gibs and
pickup bursts, which are now built with their pickup). On a dm2 duel demo,
pipelines built during play fell from 21 to 4 in a minute, and the longest
frame in Firefox from 443 to 47 ms. The rocket trail and the blade's slash
trail still build theirs on first use.
