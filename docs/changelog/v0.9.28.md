# v0.9.28

**Release Date:** 2026-10-12

## Summary

Bots play the items in v0.9.28, rankings become real skill ratings, and the
lobby gets ready for its first public server.

- **Bots and items.** Bots run the routes learned from training demos in live
  play, including the `wdm2` Red Armor wall jump, and every armor and the Mega
  Health is reachable from every spawn. Each mode values items its own way, and
  Duel bots time the Mega Health and armors.
- **Rankings.** Career TrueSkill ratings with separate **Public / Private**
  boards, a verified and replayable results journal, durable result delivery,
  and a profile with gameplay stats and match history. Bot skill updates wait
  for calibration against real players.
- **Lobby.** Bots only in private lobbies, a player cap with a "Servers are
  full" card that retries on its own, and a start-up screen that says plainly
  when it is ready.
- **Speed.** Game servers start in about a second instead of 35–45, and fresh
  material clones are set up in one batch.

Plan and status: `docs/research/v0.9.27-paperclips.md` and
`docs/research/ranking_matches.md`.

## Changes

- FFA rooms can start with two ready players while retaining eight-player
  capacity. Previously a room had to fill all eight seats before starting.
- Lobby and gameplay now use the same Soba/Udon mapping when saving results.
  Amphi and S&D could otherwise falsely lose rating eligibility with
  `team_changed`. Duel also preserves its assigned lobby side regardless of
  which player connects first, while retaining its two-player limit.
- Finished matches now preserve authoritative personal score, frags, team kills,
  deaths, enemy/friendly damage, plants, defuses and active participation time.
  Reconnect-grace expiry no longer drops those counters. They are saved once
  with verified results and checked during journal replay.
- Profile records and gameplay totals work even while bot skill is pending.
  Select a mode in Profile to browse your recent matches, including committed
  before/after ratings or a readable explanation for an unrated result.
- Profile records and history remain accessible in smaller browser windows.
  Account controls and records stack below 1100px instead of hiding records;
  compact rows and wrapped history text prevent horizontal overflow.
- Post-game winner chips now distinguish mode score from actual frags. K/D uses
  whole-match frag/death totals instead of treating S&D objective score as kills.
- Ranking now has a repeatable `ranking:acceptance` check, saved human-calibration
  recipes and read-only pilot export/draft tooling. Reviewed bot references can
  bind to the exact map bytes played; no guessed strengths are shipped.
- Cold bot preparation has a deadline and cancellation, preventing cancelled
  invitations from joining later. Result retries use a rotating bounded budget;
  `ranking:delivery` inspects recovery state without secrets and previews safe
  cleanup before explicit application.
- The leaderboard has independent **Public / Private** boards and defaults to
  public-lobby results. The choice is remembered in the browser and applies to
  your ranks in every mode. Private results now include a human
  losing to bots, which previously disappeared. New demos carry lobby visibility
  and private recordings stay out of featured picks.
- Public/Private now forms a grouped toggle with the same button dimensions
  as the gametype selector, a shared frame and twice the normal gap between
  groups. The selected scope has a visible cream face; previously its missing
  color variable left dark text on the page background.
- Lobby result logs now explain stored, duplicate, skipped, rejected and failed
  reports. `lobby:server -- --results-info` reads scoped counts and the latest
  stored result without starting a service or changing its database.
- Finished lobby matches retain players who disconnected, including their FFA
  scores and placements. A resume keeps the same result seat. In started
  Duel/Amphi matches, a player whose 20-second reconnect grace expires forfeits
  to their original opponent if that opponent is still playing. Abandoned
  matches and graceful mid-match stops add no wins or draws. Tied FFA leaders
  receive draws while lower finishers receive losses.
- Ranking development now has a versioned TrueSkill math adapter and
  `ranking:simulate` for reproducible human Duel, FFA and 5v5 numerical checks.
  Reference, tie and replay-order tests cover the adapter. It also simulates
  repeated wins/losses against explicitly hypothetical fixed bot opponents.
- Verified lobby results now save a complete played-seat snapshot and processing
  reasons in a transactional ranking journal. Aborted/ineligible results remain
  available for audit without adding wins. Reordered duplicate reports cannot
  overwrite a result or apply ratings twice. `lobby:server -- --ratings-info`
  inspects the journal; `--ratings-verify` replays applied updates read-only.
  Complete human matches now update separate public/private skill pools for
  Duel, Amphi, S&D and FFA. Team results drive S&D updates, and FFA uses every
  placement and tie. Late joins, changed sides or interrupted participation
  retain records with a skill exclusion. Rejoining after spectating cannot hide
  the interruption; held reconnect seats and valid Duel/Amphi forfeits remain
  eligible. Private bot skill updates await calibration.
- Private bot results now retain their effective difficulty, behavior build and
  loaded model identity from the authenticated bot service. Renaming a bot or
  moving identical models does not change its reference. Difficulty changes
  after match handoff retain the result with a skill exclusion. Bots prepare
  models before lobby authentication; missing/refused weights are recorded as
  fallback. Bot skill ratings still await calibration.
- The leaderboard, podium, rail and profile now rank by career TrueSkill rather
  than wins. They show Rating and provisional status alongside match records;
  rows also show career rated-match count. Last 30 days filters active players
  without resetting their career skill. The profile can read Public or Private.
  Skill gaps replace wins-to-pass hints, and older records-only servers cannot
  supply fabricated ratings. Featured top-ten players now come from public skill.
- Private bot skill updates now have an immutable calibration registry and
  `ranking:references` check/list/install tooling. Only human ratings change;
  bot strengths remain fixed. The registry ships empty until human pilots
  establish real strengths; uncalibrated/custom opponents keep records and
  visible pending status. Installing a reference does not rewrite old results.
- Finished supervised matches now save their signed report outside the demo
  archive and persist verification context before server launch. The lobby
  retries delivery after startup/storage failures and acknowledges only after
  commit. `lobby:server -- --results-recover --data-dir ...` supports offline
  recovery. Retries cannot apply ratings twice. An unfinished killed child
  creates no fabricated result; lost lobby bot-control continuity excludes
  uncommitted bot skill while retaining its record.
- Fresh PBR model and player-style material clones batch copied properties and
  the renderer light policy before first draw, avoiding repeated scene scans.
  `r_material_clone_batch 0` restores the original setup for benchmarking.
  Plasma explosions also stop allocating an invisible dummy shockwave mesh
  and material. Profiling captures now include backend/GPU descriptions.
- An experimental `r_weapon_candidates 1` setting filters first-person weapon
  camera candidates before Babylon's readiness and LOD work. It defaults off;
  world cameras keep their existing candidate provider. Timedemo profiling can
  attribute active-mesh work per camera with `--trace-cameras`.
- The timedemo profiling runner respects an explicit fixture pose rate, including
  `cl_player_pose_hz 0`. Previously it forced 30 Hz after applying the fixture,
  causing a full-rate animation benchmark to fail its profile check before playback.

### Bots take the Red Armor on wdm2, by the wall jump you showed them

The routes learned from training demos in v0.9.27 were in the bots'
navigation, but no bot ever used one: the brain started a fixed sequence for
each kind of jump and never asked the learned-route one, so a bot reaching the
wall jump simply ran at the Red Armor and fell into the atrium below. Bots now
walk to the route's start, stop on it and play the recorded inputs tick for
tick. In a four-minute bot Duel on `wdm2` a bot took the Red Armor three times
this way; before, no bot had ever reached that ledge.

### Each mode values items its own way

- **Duel** is about controlling the armors and Mega Health: they are worth far
  more than a third or fourth gun, on top of Duel's existing weight on denying
  them to the opponent.
- **Amphi** is played for frags: bots fight harder, take more risk and care
  less about items. It used to play the Deathmatch profile.
- **FFA** keeps the middle-of-the-road Deathmatch profile, and **Search &
  Destroy** stays objective-first, with the big items worth a little less.

A bot also stopped treating every new gun as nearly as good as its first: past
a working pair, another weapon is worth half as much, then less. Before, an
armed bot crossed the map for a third gun rather than the Red Armor.

### Duel bots time the Mega Health and armors

A duelist knows when the Mega Health and armors come back and is standing there
when they do. Bots now note when they see a major item taken, know when it
returns (the same respawn times the server uses, now shared), and head off in
time to be beside it when it appears; until then they wait next to it instead
of running past. Timing is a per-mode setting: full in Duel, half in FFA, off
in Amphi and Search & Destroy, where items come back per round.

In bot Duels on `wdm2`, Mega Health now sits on the map a median of about 2
seconds after it respawns, against 11 seconds before, and the Yellow Armor about
8 seconds against 14. The Red Armor, reachable only by the wall jump, is not
yet timed as well.

### Game servers start in about a second

A game server started from the console (`map`, `training_demo`) or by the
lobby took 35–45 seconds to start and then failed with "Server start timed
out". Nearly all of it was file watching: each server set up watchers over the
whole project, gigabytes of maps and demos and cached snapshots included,
before running any game code. Servers no longer watch files, and the dev
server skips game data and caches. A server is now ready in about 1.5 seconds.

### Every armor on wdm2 is in the bots' reach

From five of the six spawns bots could not reach the eastern Green Armor, and
they scored items differently from how they routed to them, so some reachable
items looked out of reach. Bots now learn the jump over the Mega Health and
the slide through the lower teleporter onto the far ledge from training
demos, recognise jumps across pits as well as wall jumps and high jumps, and
judge reachability the same way they plan routes. Every spawn now reaches
every armor and the Mega Health. In bot Duels on `wdm2` the Red Armor is taken
half again as often and sits on the map half as long after it respawns (a
median of 35 seconds, against 75); the Yellow Armor sits under 4 seconds.

### The start-up screen says plainly when it is ready

It used to offer "Click to start" while the lobby was still connecting, with
the bar three quarters full, and the ring around the logo looked like a frozen
spinner, so a ready screen looked stuck. Now the ring spins while anything is
loading; when everything is done the bar fills, every step reads done, the
ring closes into solid gold and a gold **Click to start** button pulses under
it. A click made earlier still counts. With the system's reduced-motion
setting on, the ring spins more slowly and the button glows instead of
growing. The screen holds still as the button appears and when it is clicked;
before, the status line jumped up when the button took its place.

### Bots are for private lobbies

"Add bot" works only in a private lobby now. In a public one the key is shown
but off, with "Private lobbies only" on it and "Start a private lobby to add
bots" on mouseover, and the room's status no longer suggests adding a bot. The
lobby server refuses a bot for a public room too: bots there would take seats
people are queueing for and change who a ranked result is against.

### The lobby has a player cap, and tells the players over it

A lobby takes at most 64 players online at once (`--max-players`, or
`SHAG_LOBBY_MAX_PLAYERS`; `0` for no cap). Before this, nothing stopped a
lobby from taking more players than its server could hold matches for. A
player over the cap now gets a "Servers are full" card instead of a lobby that
quietly says it is offline. The card counts down to the next try, about a
minute, spread a little so everyone turned away doesn't come back in the same
second, and has a **Try now** key. The rest of the menu still works while they
wait. Bots don't count toward the cap. A player who just left, as "Return to
lobby" does when it reloads the page, gets back in even if the slot was taken
in between.
