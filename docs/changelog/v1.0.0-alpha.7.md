# v1.0.0-alpha.7

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## News

- Washoi is now listed on the Babylon.js
  [Games made with Babylon.js](https://www.babylonjs.com/games/) showcase.

## Changes

- HUD: ammo and armor bars animate without recalculating their layout on each
  animation frame. Switching weapons with the same ammo count now updates the
  fill for the new weapon's capacity, and the air meter reliably changes its
  warning and DROWNING label even when its displayed fill step stays the same.

- Combat feedback: halved the camera shake from plasmagun hits and nearby
  plasma impacts, making sustained plasma fire less disruptive to the view.

- CPU: hidden pickup-model sources no longer evaluate constant animation clips
  every frame. Their authored pose is applied once and retained by pickup clones;
  animated custom sources and held-weapon/barrel animations keep playing. This
  removes unnecessary animation work on both WebGL2 and WebGPU.

- CPU: a ragdoll now copies its physics pose onto the skeleton in one pass.
  Babylon's ragdoll rebuilt the whole 65-bone rig once for each of its 15
  physics bodies, every frame. The pose is now written parent-first and the rig
  rebuilt once: on washoi_dm5 the copy fell from about 35–57 µs to 5–9 µs per
  ragdoll per frame, with identical frames on WebGL2 and WebGPU and unchanged
  physics. A model with mirrored or uneven scale keeps Babylon's own copy.

- CPU: animated models no longer rebuild their skin matrices on frames where
  their pose did not change. Babylon copied every linked bone value and marked
  the skeleton dirty on each frame a model was drawn, so a player whose clip
  steps at 30 Hz, or a weapon holding still, rebuilt and re-uploaded its matrices
  every frame anyway. In the washoi_dm5 demo builds fell from about 3.7 to 0.75
  per frame and skin preparation from 0.10 to 0.05 ms a frame on WebGL2 (0.15 to
  0.06 ms on WebGPU), with palettes identical to Babylon's own.

- CPU: soft halos and flares no longer search every mesh in the scene on each
  camera pass to find themselves. Each soft material keeps its own list of the
  meshes drawing it, which cut that per-frame step from about 0.10 to 0.03 ms
  on washoi_dm5.

- Rendering: map sprites (halos, coronas, flares) turn toward the camera in the
  vertex shader instead of on the CPU. Their geometry was rewritten and uploaded
  every frame the camera moved; on washoi_dm5 that cost about 0.18 ms a frame
  and now about 0.01. Reflections and sky portals turn each sprite toward their
  own camera, where the CPU billboard used the main camera's. With `r_glow`
  above 0 the CPU billboard also runs, for the glow pass.

- Rendering: plain map surfaces that differ only in their images now share a
  draw. Their base, normal-map and gloss images become layers of texture
  arrays, and each surface picks its layer: on washoi_dm5 about 55 surfaces
  draw as 17–18 meshes (93 opaque map draws a frame become 63–70) and on
  washoi_dm2 49 draw as 11 (66 become 37). Frames are unchanged on WebGL2 and
  WebGPU.

- Downloads: optimized the Vanguard player model. Its package is about 69%
  smaller (1.20 MB instead of 3.81 MB), reducing player-model downloads on EU
  and US. Published package metadata identifies the updated archive for caching.

- Rendering: on WebGPU, the first seconds after a map appears no longer
  stutter. Each material's render pipeline was built the first time it came
  into view, and the browser compiled it before the frame could finish: on
  washoi_dm5 about 105 pipelines in the first seconds of play, frames of up to
  2.4 s and 5–7 fps for two seconds. The loading screen now draws the whole map
  once and waits for the GPU, which adds 0.8–2.8 s to loading on the tested
  maps; play opens at 62–74 fps. Babylon also gave every material a second
  pipeline once a player outline had been drawn over a transparent surface,
  because a stencil mask that nothing reads stayed in its pipeline key. The
  mask is now reset when stencil is off. WebGL2 is unchanged.

- Rendering: additive map sprites (halos, coronas, glows) that share a texture
  draw as one mesh with one vertex upload a frame, instead of one each.
  washoi_dm2's 62 sprites draw from 4 meshes, washoi_dm5's 76 from 42. Frames
  aimed at sprites match before and after on WebGL2 and WebGPU. Alpha-blended
  sprites, whose look depends on draw order, keep a mesh each.

- Rendering: the first-person weapon camera no longer evaluates every mesh in
  the scene to draw the handful of weapon meshes it can see. The layer filter
  that did this, on by default only in Firefox, is now on in every browser and
  on WebGPU: on washoi_dm5 the weapon camera's mesh selection fell from about
  1.0 ms to 0.15 ms a frame on WebGL2 and from about 0.55 ms to 0.09 ms on
  WebGPU, with identical pixels. `r_weapon_candidates 0` turns it off for
  diagnostics.

- Rendering: WebGPU now submits frames through render bundles (Babylon's
  non-compatibility mode) by default instead of only with `?ncm=1`. When the
  CPU is the limit, frames were 9–14% faster than before in throttled tests
  on washoi_dm5, with matching pictures. Water skips its occlusion query in
  this mode. `?ncm=0` turns it off for a session.

- Fix (WebGPU): after a few map changes the world could stop drawing, every
  frame rejected with "buffer binding is too small", or a draw threw on a
  missing light texture. Frozen world surfaces were re-checked when their
  lights changed, but not when only the lights' order changed, and Babylon
  re-sorts every surface's lights whenever any light is added, even a model's
  own. A surface then bound one light's data where its shader expected
  another's. The check now follows each world surface's lights in order. The
  dm5 → dm2 → amphi1 → dm11 sequence that failed now runs without errors.
