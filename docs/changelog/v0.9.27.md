# v0.9.27

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Summary

The v0.9.27 cycle follows the v0.9.26 network, client quality and post-game
screen release.

## Changes

Record player-facing and developer changes here as they land.

### Machinegun: spread, tracer and kick

The machinegun was a perfectly accurate laser drawn as a one-pixel line from
your eye, with a gun that barely moved when aimed.

- **Spread.** Its spread values were never read: every shot went exactly down
  the view. Hip fire now starts in a 2.5° cone that opens to 6° the longer the
  trigger is held; aimed, 0.4° opening to 1.2°. The cone closes again in a
  little over half a second off the trigger. The crosshair gap opens and closes
  with it.
- **Tracer.** A bright streak that leaves the muzzle and travels to where the
  bullet went; the wall mark, sparks and flash play when it arrives. Other
  players' tracers leave their gun.
- **Kick.** The camera jumps on every shot, harder when aimed, and the gun
  punches back half as hard aimed as from the hip, where it used to drop to a
  fifth; the barrel's rise stays small when aimed, so the sights stay centred.
  Both are visual only: the angle sent to the server leaves the kick out, so it
  never moves your aim.
- **Recoil follows the fire rate (every weapon).** The view model snaps back on
  each shot and is at rest before the next. It used to ease back over about a
  quarter of a second, longer than the machinegun's 0.1 s between shots, so the
  gun crept toward the camera and stayed there. Slow weapons keep their long
  return. Recoil also no longer rolls the gun: a leftover from an old plasmagun
  model tilted every weapon around the view axis on each shot, which read as a
  sideways kick rather than a punch back.
- **Your own shots are drawn where they went.** Tracers and pellets used to be
  drawn down wherever your view pointed when the server's report of the shot
  arrived, a round trip later. They now follow the shot itself.
- **Riotgun.** Its pellets are drawn where the server's pellets went (the fire
  event carries their seed), for you and for everyone watching; other players'
  shots were drawn with 20 pellets in a 12° cone instead of 18 in 8°.

Developer notes: `weapon_fire` gains event code 24 (a pellet seed and cone);
other shots keep code 4, so older demos decode as before. Client and server must
ship together. The spread changes what the server simulates, so re-sim bundles
recorded before it will not reconcile. The anti-cheat accuracy calibration now
fires aimed: from the hip the machinegun's own cone hides aim quality, so hip-fire
accuracy separates an aimbot from a human less well than before. Research and
design: `docs/research/machinegun_effects.md`.

### Head hits do 25% more

Up from 10%, which on the Machinegun took a round from 10 to 11 and went
unnoticed. A head hit now does 13 with the Machinegun (damage rounds after the
multiplier) and a quarter more with every other weapon that can land one; the
Riotgun still has no headshots, and leg hits still do 25% less.

### Slime ripples and reflects

Slime (the Amphitheatre's floor, `ETRtex/slime2`) was already drawn by the
engine's liquid material, but only water got the reactive surface: bodies
wading through slime left no wake, a splash drew a flat disc instead of a
ripple, and players were not reflected in it. Slime now gets the same ripples,
wakes and reflections as water. It still throws no spray, being thick, and lava
is unchanged.

### The start-up, handoff and map loading screens are the lobby's twin

The start-up screen, the "Game starting" handoff, the map loading screen and the
join screen over a live match wore their own look (a dark stage with line
ornaments, a large backdrop word, a separate step list), so moving between them
and the lobby was a cut to a different screen. They are now built like the
lobby, from one shared rail (`LobbyTwin`): the gold rail at the lobby's width
with the game's logo where the lobby has it, the screen's steps as the rail's
keys (the current one latched in ink, as the lobby marks the page you are on, a
failed one in the alarm colour), a panel where the lobby lists rooms that shows
the map or server and a log of what is happening, the engine in the player
card's place, and the Profile / Settings / Console keys below (Console works).
The stage is the lobby's blue with its drifting light: the start ring, the
match's title, or the map's name over its levelshot, with the status and bar
underneath. Changing screen now changes only what the rail and stage hold.
