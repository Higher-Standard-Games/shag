# v0.9.27

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Summary

The v0.9.27 cycle follows the v0.9.26 network, client quality and post-game
screen release.

## Changes

Record player-facing and developer changes here as they land.

### Offline renderer benchmarks

`timedemo` can now replay a local FFA fixture with a saved fly route and seeded
first-person bot spectating, warm up, measure repeated runs and export raw JSON.
Reports expose frame-time tails, scoped CPU/GPU timings, draw/actor counts and
invalid-workload reasons. Fixture creation has a saved ten-bot `wdm2` recipe and
cleans up its own authoring match. Browser route and paired hardware acceptance
are pending; fixed-work throughput mode is not implemented. See
[the timedemo guide](../manual/guides/timedemo.md).

WebGPU NCM timedemos also report per-phase bundle creation/reuse and rebuild
coverage, sampled at engine end-of-frame so camera cuts retain correct attribution.
Counts per frame and creation percentages help compare the opt-in bundle mode;
missing data stays distinct from zero. This adds diagnostics, not a claimed speedup.

`npm run benchmark:profile` captures the offline timedemo in visible Edge and
saves CPU profiles for its fly and first-person phases, alongside workload reports
and screenshots. It uses a separate browser profile and private local dev server.

`npm run benchmark:outlines` compares saved outline-off/on fixture variants in
alternating fresh sessions, with CPU sampling disabled and rejected workloads
retained. Separate `--trace-outlines` captures identify body/outline colour,
depth-only, stencil and overlay submissions. This adds measurements; player
outline appearance and the default setting are unchanged.

`npm run benchmark:crowd` measures a saved 0/2/5/10-player scene without bot AI or
network traffic, using the normal player rigs and outline styling. Fixed pose
ticks, exact body/draw coverage and separate CPU/GPU counters help identify crowd
rendering costs even when display refresh limits wall-frame timing.

The crowd benchmark can also compare an experimental lower-detail outline mesh
with the full-detail outline, while retaining normal body geometry and both
outline passes. A saved generator recipe recreates the asset; fixed-pose near and
occluded screenshots support quality checks. This remains a benchmark experiment.

`cl_player_outline_proxy 1` now exposes that experimental geometry in live play
and demo playback, including timedemo. The default is 0; normal player bodies and
outline colour/depth passes remain intact. Sources load once per scene after
opt-in, verify their provenance, and follow player/corpse disposal. Timedemo
reports distinguish reduced submissions, deliberate transparent full passes and
unexpected fallback, and `benchmark:outlines -- --proxy` runs paired map profiles.

Timedemo no longer scans scene/material readiness on every measured frame. That
diagnostic itself prepared shader variants and added CPU work to the benchmark.
Readiness is checked around measurement, with passive pending-data counts during
frames. Timestamped samples also let the profiling runner isolate slow gunplay frames.

Explosion fireballs and projectile trails now update their existing position
buffers without invalidating shader attributes every frame. Trail normals are no
longer recalculated for their unlit material. Bounds and the existing effect shapes
remain updated; explosion/trail material setup batches dirty-enabling properties
to reduce repeated scene scans. See the material-dirty profiling follow-up in the
[WebGPU research notes](../research/v0.9.27-webgpu.md).

### Rendering: off-screen players, GPU weather, WebGPU bundles

Remote players now reuse loaded weapon sources when switching guns instead of
importing the same model repeatedly. Each weapon keeps its own transforms,
skeleton, animations and materials; geometry and GPU texture storage can be shared.
Old asynchronous switch requests cannot attach a duplicate gun after a rapid
A-to-B-to-A switch. Cached sources retire with the remote renderer, while live
corpse weapons retain the resources they still use.

Dropped weapons also reuse barrel sources and models absent from the map's
preloader. Repeated drops no longer import those same GLBs for every weapon.
Picking up a weapon while its barrels load releases its detached model resources
and discards the late barrel result.

First-person weapons and barrels now reuse prepared GLB sources across switches.
Material conversion and view-layer setup happen once per source, while active
weapons keep independent rigs/materials and barrel spin. Clearing a view cancels
late loads; cached sources retire when the view is disposed or rebuilt. Routine
viewmodel/barrel loading diagnostics now print only with `developer 1`.
Cloned WebGPU view materials retain guarded light budgets and shader-hot-swap
suppression, which Babylon's material cloning does not inherit from a source.

Cached pickup/drop model cloning and player-instance disposal/team-colour
diagnostics, plus successful model tag loads, now print only with `developer 1`.
Normal play avoids their console formatting and history/DOM updates; loading
summaries and warnings/errors remain.

Explosion rays reuse retired materials instead of configuring a fresh material
for every ray of every explosion. Active rays retain independent colors and
fading; idle retention is bounded and cleared when the effect or scene is reset.

Timedemo starts its manual playback clock at the first frame hook. Waiting for
that hook after preparation could previously arrive as a large first playback
delta and skip demo events, especially on WebGPU. Actual stalls after playback
starts still invalidate the benchmark.

- **Players behind you are no longer drawn.** Every remote player in a room the
  camera could see was submitted every frame, including the ones behind it,
  because skinned bounds describe the bind pose rather than the animated one.
  Bodies now carry bounds that hold any pose, so each camera culls them on its
  own. In a recorded nine-player FFA demo this cut body submissions in the
  main view by about 60%, and no body on screen was culled. Their skeletons also
  stop animating while off screen, except near water, where a mirror may still
  show them. A dying player's corpse is drawn as before.
- **Players at medium distance animate at 30 Hz.** A player under about 60
  pixels tall still moves every frame, but their limbs are posed 30 times a
  second rather than every frame; nearby players are unchanged.
  `cl_player_pose_hz` sets the rate (0 poses everyone every frame).
- **Deaths no longer cost a sixth of every frame.** The larger gib fragments
  ray-tested the map's render triangles every frame until they bounced; with a
  few deaths in flight that was about 15% of the main thread on both renderers.
  They now bounce off the map's collision brushes instead. In the ten-player
  benchmark, median frame time fell from 20.2 to 14.1 ms on WebGL2 and from
  26.9 to 18.1 ms on WebGPU.
- **WebGPU no longer stalls while spectating in first person.** Every model
  load (weapons, effects) made every material in the scene recompile its
  lighting under WebGPU: Babylon raised all light limits and the renderer
  lowered them back. In first-person spectating, where models keep loading,
  frames took two to three times as long and benchmark runs fell apart.
  Under WebGPU only the renderer's light policy may now raise a material's
  limit, so a load leaves the rest of the scene alone.
- **Rain and snow simulate on the GPU under WebGPU.** WebGPU always used the CPU
  particle system. It now uses Babylon's compute-shader particles, as WebGL2
  uses transform feedback. `weather status` shows which one is running.
- **`?renderer=webgpu&ncm=1`** turns on WebGPU render bundles (Babylon's
  non-compatibility mode) for that session. It is an experiment: pictures match,
  but bundles are rebuilt often during play, so it is not yet faster. The
  translucent rings on procedural pickups draw in one pass in this mode.

Developer notes: renderer QA gains `rendererqacase=weather`, and reports record
`nonCompatibilityMode`. Status and GPU evidence are in
[the WebGPU research notes](../research/v0.9.27-webgpu.md#webgpu-work-order-status--implementation-log).

### SHAGGED: maps from scripts and AI agents

The map editor can now be driven without its window, by a script or an AI
agent. Maps are built from typed operations on the same document the editor
opens, then compiled and checked automatically.

- **Building:** boxes, per-face textures, entities and brush entities, plus
  generators for rooms (with doorways and a light), corridors, rooms joined
  to each other (`gen.connect`, sealed by construction), stairs, teleporters
  and jump pads, and a box carve.
- **Textures by name:** the editor's texture catalogue (2,101 textures from the
  installed packages, with image sizes) can be searched, and a face only needs
  the texture's name.
- **A report that says whether the map works:** Map Health, then a real
  compile with leak detection, then where a player can actually go from each
  spawn, flooded with the game's own movement (stairs, drops, hops, teleporters
  and jump pads). Problems name the object to fix. The bots' route graph is
  reported alongside.
- **Two ways in:** `npm run shagged` (a JSON command line) and
  `npm run shagged:mcp`, an MCP server that AI clients connect to
  (`claude mcp add shagged -- node scripts/shagged-mcp.mjs`).
- First test with a fresh AI agent that had only these tools: it read the
  guide, looked up textures and entity classes, and built a textured
  three-area, two-level map in one batch. The report correctly found that its
  upper room was sealed off (stairs ran through two walls without doorways);
  the agent's hand-built fix then leaked, which the next report catches. The
  run was cut short by a usage limit before it finished.

Developer notes: `@modelcontextprotocol/sdk` is a dev dependency (1.30.1).
The scripting guide is `docs/manual/guides/shagged-api.md`; design and
measurements are in `docs/research/SHAGGED_API.md`.

### Bots find their way around maps again

Bots plan routes on a graph of points across each map's floors, but a height
mix-up meant almost none of those points connected: on `wdm2` none of 63
floor points had a walking link (4 links in the whole map), so bots got around
only by jumping between spawns, items and teleporters. Walking links are now
tested from standing height: `wdm2` has 272 and `wdm5` 270. Bots can also load
maps built and published with SHAGGED, which they could not find before.

### Bots reach where players can, and play Search & Destroy

Against bots, Search & Destroy could not be played: in 12 minutes of bot
rounds on `wbomb2` and `wbomb6` nobody fired a shot and nobody planted. The
bots' navigation graph had no route from any spawn to either bomb site. The
same faults made every map smaller for bots than for players:

- **Walking links were straight lines.** Two neighbouring points linked only
  if a straight sweep between them was clear, with at most 40 units of rise,
  so stairs, ramps, a crate in a corridor or a doorway off the line cut the
  map apart. Links now come from a short walk with the player's own movement,
  which climbs stairs, walks around obstacles and steps off ledges short of
  fall damage. The sweep that remains no longer passes when blocked over its
  last tenth (a 1,460-unit link on `wbomb2` ran into a wall), checks for floor
  beneath it, and fits doorways a player fits through.
- **Jump pads landed in mid-air.** A pad's route ended at the point its throw
  aims at, which on most bomb maps hangs straight above the pad. Bots now fly
  each pad with the game's movement and know where it can land, and steer
  there.
- **Routes ended at the wrong spot.** A bomb site's marker floats above its
  floor, and the nearest point was often on a ledge beside it that nothing
  walks to. A route now ends at the nearest point it can actually reach, and
  starts from one the bot can walk to, so a bot thrown onto a barricade no
  longer heads for the far side of a wall.

From a typical spawn, bots now reach 55 of `wdm2`'s 97 route points (was 34),
72 of `wdm12`'s 158 (was 39) and 205 of `wbomb6`'s 650 (was 27). In bot
Search & Destroy on `wbomb2` the bomb was planted and armed in 3 of 4 rounds,
and a bot Duel on `wdm12` went from 0–0 to 5–4. Places only a trick reaches,
such as the Red Armor on `wdm2`, are not routed yet.

### Record a trick for the bots: `training_demo`

`training_demo <map> [name]` launches a map like `map`, on a server that stays
in warmup and records every tick of your play. Do the trick, then
`training_demo stop`: the demo is saved to
`public/data/demos/training/<map>_<name>.shagdemo`. These recordings are how
bots will learn routes that need a wall jump or slide jump.

### Bots learn movement tricks from your demos

`npm run bot:learn -- <training demo>` finds every wall jump and high jump in
a demo recorded with `training_demo`, replays it with the game's own movement
to check it, works out a run-up a bot can start from standing, and saves the
tricks that land reliably to `config/bot-routes/<map>.json`. Bots load them as
routes: on `wdm2` every spawn now has a route to the Red Armor through the
wall jump above the teleporter. Bots also now always consider the armors, Mega
Health and powerups, which before only counted when among the nearest twelve
items. Choosing the Red Armor in a live match is still to come in v0.9.28.

### `map` and `training_demo` no longer give up on slow server starts

A server started from the console took about 35 seconds to start while the
game waited only 30, so `map` failed even though the server came up, and the
server then waited for a player forever. The game now waits a minute, and a
server nobody joins shuts down after two.

### `demo:record` plays Amphi and round modes properly

`npm run demo:record -- --gametype amphi` ran plain Deathmatch, and every round
mode started without its round setup, because the tool jumped the match
straight to live. Amphi then counted no frags and extended a 16–7 round into
overtime. The tool now runs out the warmup and starts the match the normal way.

### SHAGGED editor guide

The manual has a new guide to the built-in editor: opening it, the four views,
camera and tool controls, texturing, entities, saving, and compiling and
playtesting. It sits with the scripting guide under Map Editing.

### SHAGGED editor reaches its server again

In the browser the editor always reported "SHAGGED server is unreachable",
even with `npm run shagged:server` running, so Server Maps stayed disabled
and maps saved on the server (including those built by scripts and agents)
could not be opened. The browser was rejecting the editor's network call
before it was sent. The editor now connects, lists the server's maps and opens
them.

### Machinegun: spread, tracer and kick

The machinegun was a perfectly accurate laser drawn as a one-pixel line from
your eye, with a gun that barely moved when aimed.

- **Spread.** Its spread values were never read: every shot went exactly down
  the view. Hip fire now starts in a 2.5° cone that opens to 6° the longer the
  trigger is held; aimed, 0.4° opening to 1.2°. The cone closes again in a
  little over half a second off the trigger. The crosshair gap opens and closes
  with it.
- **Tracer.** A bright streak that leaves the muzzle and travels to where the
  bullet went; the wall mark, sparks and flash play when it arrives. Other
  players' tracers leave their gun.
- **Kick.** The camera jumps on every shot, harder when aimed, and the gun
  punches back half as hard aimed as from the hip, where it used to drop to a
  fifth; the barrel's rise stays small when aimed, so the sights stay centred.
  Both are visual only: the angle sent to the server leaves the kick out, so it
  never moves your aim.
- **Recoil follows the fire rate (every weapon).** The view model snaps back on
  each shot and is at rest before the next. It used to ease back over about a
  quarter of a second, longer than the machinegun's 0.1 s between shots, so the
  gun crept toward the camera and stayed there. Slow weapons keep their long
  return. Recoil also no longer rolls the gun: a leftover from an old plasmagun
  model tilted every weapon around the view axis on each shot, which read as a
  sideways kick rather than a punch back.
- **Your own shots are drawn where they went.** Tracers and pellets used to be
  drawn down wherever your view pointed when the server's report of the shot
  arrived, a round trip later. They now follow the shot itself.
- **Riotgun.** Its pellets are drawn where the server's pellets went (the fire
  event carries their seed), for you and for everyone watching; other players'
  shots were drawn with 20 pellets in a 12° cone instead of 18 in 8°.

Developer notes: `weapon_fire` gains event code 24 (a pellet seed and cone);
other shots keep code 4, so older demos decode as before. Client and server must
ship together. The spread changes what the server simulates, so re-sim bundles
recorded before it will not reconcile. The anti-cheat accuracy calibration now
fires aimed: from the hip the machinegun's own cone hides aim quality, so hip-fire
accuracy separates an aimbot from a human less well than before. Research and
design: `docs/research/machinegun_effects.md`.

### Head hits do 25% more

Up from 10%, which on the Machinegun took a round from 10 to 11 and went
unnoticed. A head hit now does 13 with the Machinegun (damage rounds after the
multiplier) and a quarter more with every other weapon that can land one; the
Riotgun still has no headshots, and leg hits still do 25% less.

### Slime ripples and reflects

Slime (the Amphitheatre's floor, `ETRtex/slime2`) was already drawn by the
engine's liquid material, but only water got the reactive surface: bodies
wading through slime left no wake, a splash drew a flat disc instead of a
ripple, and players were not reflected in it. Slime now gets the same ripples,
wakes and reflections as water. It still throws no spray, being thick, and lava
is unchanged.

### The start-up, handoff and map loading screens are the lobby's twin

The start-up screen, the "Game starting" handoff, the map loading screen and the
join screen over a live match wore their own look (a dark stage with line
ornaments, a large backdrop word, a separate step list), so moving between them
and the lobby was a cut to a different screen. They are now built like the
lobby, from one shared rail (`LobbyTwin`): the gold rail at the lobby's width
with the game's logo where the lobby has it, the screen's steps as the rail's
keys (the current one latched in ink, as the lobby marks the page you are on, a
failed one in the alarm colour), a panel where the lobby lists rooms that shows
the map or server and a log of what is happening, the engine in the player
card's place, and the Profile / Settings / Console keys below (Console works).
The stage is the lobby's blue with its drifting light: the start ring, the
match's title, or the map's name over its levelshot, with the status and bar
underneath. Changing screen now changes only what the rail and stage hold.

### Loadouts in Deathmatch, no map weapons in kit modes, Loadout in the Esc menu

- **Deathmatch has kits.** Pick a primary and a secondary before you join, as in
  S&D and Amphi; you spawn with the blade and that kit, and a change applies at
  your next spawn. Spawn armor stays Deathmatch's own.
- **No weapons on the map where you choose a kit** (S&D, Amphi, Deathmatch):
  the kit is the arsenal. Ammo, armor, health and the Quad spawn as before, and
  players still drop their weapons when they die. Duel and Team Deathmatch keep
  their map weapons.
- **Loadout in the Esc menu**, right under Resume, showing the kit you chose.
  The picker says when a change applies (next round, or next spawn) and has a
  Back key; Esc backs out too. Your last kit in each mode is remembered and
  pre-selected.
- **Bots carry varied kits**, picked per bot, instead of all running Rocket
  Launcher and Machinegun.
- An item a mode leaves out (S&D's Quad, now the kit modes' weapons) could stay
  visible on the client if its id was the map's highest: the server no longer
  drops it from the list it reports, it keeps it permanently inactive.

Developer notes: `MatchConfig.loadouts` and the `match_state` field `loadouts`
(`"round"` | `"respawn"`) replace the client's check of the mode's name. Plan:
`docs/research/loadouts_everywhere.md`.

### Amphi: the Quad no longer follows you into the next round

The server already took the Quad away at a round start, but the client dropped
its own powerup timers only on death, and a round start respawns everyone
alive: the HUD's Quad timer and the Quad fire sound carried on into the new
round. A snapshot without a powerup's state now ends it on the client.
