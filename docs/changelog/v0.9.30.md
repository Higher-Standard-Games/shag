# v0.9.30

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Summary

The v0.9.30 cycle continues toward 1.0 for the four modes (Duel, Amphi, FFA,
Search & Destroy) and the first public server.

Carried over from v0.9.29:

- Duel bots time the Red Armor less well than the Mega Health and the other
  armors: it sits on the map a median of about 35 seconds after respawning.
- A cap on concurrent matches in the lobby (R11 in the deployment notes). The
  player cap bounds matches only through the players in them.

## Changes

### Warfork lighting and blend follow-up

Normal-mapped BSP surfaces use Qfusion's explicit tangent frame, fixing dark
sunlit floor strips and reversed relief without flipping the lightmap atlas.
Vertex-coloured material decals receive the map's RGB gain while retaining
their authored opacity, and use the same baked lighting as solid surfaces.
Alpha-tested fences and leaves avoid a second fallback lighting multiplier.

Additive stages distinguish `ONE ONE` from `SRC_ALPHA ONE`. Animated frame
stages load their images, restoring the jump-pad arrows and Nat's Bar emission.
MD3 materials use the matching texture orientation and primary shader animation.
Animated explosion materials thaw before updates, and expired rays are hidden
even if their materials were frozen during loading.

### PK3 image orientation

PNG/JPG textures use the same vertical upload orientation as TGA and KTX2.
This fixes scrambled crate and barrel texture atlases in Warfork maps without
changing their base or lightmap UV coordinates.

Translated BSP materials also use the OpenGL normal-map convention in Babylon's
coordinate basis, correcting inverted relief. Shaders using `alphaGen vertex`
enable vertex-alpha blending on their BSP meshes, preserving decal transparency.

Filter decals use framebuffer multiplication instead of a luminance-based
transparency approximation. Material stages keep their own UV state rather than
inheriting an environment pass. Additive shine after a material stage is retained,
including brightness animation and an independent pass for blended bases.
Self-lit opaque tubes bypass fallback vertex darkening, and dim additive decals
retain authored gray `rgbGen const` gain.

BSP materials finish texture/fog/lightmap initialization before entering the render
loop; baked samplers also bind during cached draws. This fixes map-loading GPU
binding errors on WebGL2 and WebGPU. Explosion flash gain is reduced to match the
unshadowed dynamic-light path and avoid washing nearby walls to yellow-white.

### Warfork baked lighting and material highlights

Translated BSP gloss images control specular strength instead of making
low-gloss brick behave like a polished surface. Authored gloss intensity and
exponent are retained. Ordinary opaque lightmapped surfaces no longer multiply
fallback vertex lighting into baked lighting a second time.

FBSP lightmaps preserve their original color bytes and apply worldspawn's
lighting gain and ambient compensation in the material. Baked surfaces no
longer receive an extra synthetic sun/fill factor, while dynamic game lights
still contribute. Paired deluxe directions drive normal-map shading and
highlights on WebGL2 and WebGPU, including the baked-light path for inline
brushes. Shared pages no longer acquire different gains depending on material
binding order. Renderer gloss settings preserve specular/gloss materials' workflow.

Corrected FBSP model-lighting grids to read 30-byte records and the compressed
index table, use inward sample bounds, and skip invalid sites during interpolation.
This removes the grid alignment mismatch on wfdm11, wfdm1 and wdm2; models/weapons
retain their existing artistic lighting adjustments.

### Wood façade texture upload

Fixed KTX2 sidecars with base dimensions that are not divisible by four
rendering black despite being marked ready. These images decode to RGBA while
aligned images retain compression. This restores wfdm11's 554×554 wood façade
on WebGL2 and WebGPU without disabling KTX2 or changing its lightmaps.

### Ready-built Warfork map trial

Duel and FFA offer `wfdm5` (viciious' lair) alongside `wfdm11` (Warehouse C).
Retired wfdm1 from the pools while awaiting its updated upstream build.
Both use Warfork's ready-built BSPs. Archived the fast local wfdm11 compile
and installed the upstream version with its authored 2048px lightmaps,
preserving the map's additional model textures.

### Authored FBSP lightmap sizes and plasma attachments

FBSP maps honor worldspawn's `_lightmapImageSize`, retaining the 512 default
when absent. This prevents 2048-pixel Warfork lightmaps being split into unrelated
512-pixel pages; malformed page data reports a loading error. Restored Plasma's
`tags.txt` to the weapon package and load attachment rules through the PK3 index,
fixing barrels floating away from the gun after the loose weapon tree was moved.

### Warfork visual media installed for the map trial

Installed the Warfork texture and shader packs for local map testing, with a
small compatibility sound package preserving the existing audio paths. Archived
the replaced base packs and 45 legacy texture packs outside the served tree;
the old texture packs otherwise overrode Warfork shader definitions. Rebuilt
the published PK3 index and verified wfdm11 brick, tile and decal textures on
WebGL2 and WebGPU. Custom weapon, player and soundtrack packages are retained.

### Weapon GLBs ship in the weapon package

Weapon GLBs and ammo-counter configs are packaged in `z_weapons.pk3` and loaded
through the indexed PK3 filesystem for first-person, pickup, dropped and remote
models. The loose weapon export tree moved out of served data. Added
`tools:weapon-models` for reproducible packaging and restored `convert:weapons`
for IQM conversion/texture embedding into the unserved source directory.

### Data archive location

The repository-root `data/archive/` is ignored by Git and reserved for local
media migration snapshots, outside the served `public/` tree. The data migration
research plan defines how to build a curated, separately publishable runtime payload.

### Warfork Warehouse C map trial

Duel and FFA lobby map pools now offer only `wfdm11` (Warehouse C, Warfork)
for testing. Other installed maps remain accessible through developer commands.

### UI animations behave consistently across operating system settings

Removed reduced-motion overrides throughout the game UI and editor. Lobby
radar sweeps, search stripes, HUD pulses, transitions and leaderboard scrolling
keep their normal animation when Windows Animation effects is disabled.

### Launchers finish aiming before firing; sniper hipfire has spread

Rocket and Grenade Launchers raise their sights before a shot from the hip,
waiting the full 160 ms ADS time (doubled from 80 ms for both launchers).
Releasing fire cancels that shot. Automatic aim
keeps a small 1° rocket / 4° grenade spread; completed manual ADS is accurate.
Electrobolt still fires instantly from the hip, now with a wide 20° spread;
completed 375 ms manual ADS fires straight. The view-model raise finishes in the same
time as the server's firing gate, and projectile/tracer directions match the
authoritative shot.
Only RL and GL auto-aim on primary fire. Plasma fires immediately from the hip
with its existing 3° spread and aims only when you press Mouse 2.

Electrobolt hipfire now has the Riotgun's heavy upward/backward weapon kick
(4× normal), with reduced recoil while aiming. Hit confirmation sounds now run
from `hit_3` on light hits to `hit_0` on heavy hits; their damage tiers were reversed.

### The editor's exit matches demo playback

SHAGGED's exit button is a gold ✕ in the top-left corner, matching the demo
player. The toolbar leaves room beside it, keeping exit away from the FPS counter.
The redundant VIEW heading above the View button is removed so the FPS counter
has space in the top-right corner.

### Chat masks profanity consistently

Lobby, room and in-game chat now replace recognized English profanity with `***`,
including private rooms, team and spectator messages. Filtering happens on the
server before delivery and storage, so the sender sees the same text as everyone
else and histories and new demo recordings contain the masked version. Ordinary
game vocabulary and words containing harmless substrings remain intact. This is
a lightweight word filter with no automatic penalties; names are unchanged.
Strong profanity and two explicit slurs also match inside words, catching
added-letter bypasses. The general dictionary still uses whole-word matching to
preserve words such as "assassin" and "classic", with a Scunthorpe exception for
the stronger substring list.

### Video settings change in the lobby

Switching to the Pretty preset, or turning reflections on, during a match
could leave the screen showing the bare scene instead of the game until the
preset went back to Balanced. Until the renderer can change its passes
mid-match safely, the Video page in the match menu's Settings is locked, with a
note saying video settings change in the lobby; there it works as before.
Fullscreen still works in a match with Alt+Enter. In a match, **Reset page** is
off on Video and **Reset all** leaves video settings alone.

### Loading screens leave the console key to the keyboard

The start-up, map loading, match handoff and join screens show the lobby's
Profile, Settings and Console keys so nothing moves when the lobby takes over.
Console was the only one that worked there; all three now wait for the lobby.
The backquote key still opens the console on every screen.

### Highlight reels fit the demo

A highlight reel is at most a third of its demo, so a short recording no longer
offers a five-minute "highlight" of almost the whole match. A demo under three
minutes (shorter than an Amphi round) has no reel; 3–6 minutes offers 1 minute,
6–15 minutes adds 2, and 15 minutes or more adds 5. The Highlights key and the
lengths keep their places, disabled with a "Too short for a highlight reel"
tooltip, so cards line up whatever the demo's length.

### Players stay visible in highlight reels

A highlight reel's third-person shots (fly-bys) could leave a player out of the
picture. A first-person shot hides the followed player's body so the camera is
not inside their head, and cutting to a fly-by never showed it again: the shot
framed an empty spot. The body is now hidden only while the camera is behind
their eyes. In one reel, the followed player had been hidden in every
third-person frame; now in none.

### Demos are coloured from the followed player's side

A demo is watched through one player's eyes, but every player wore the enemy
colour, so a highlight reel's third-person shots showed the player you follow
as an opponent. The followed player now wears your own side's colour (pink by
default), and everyone else is coloured from their side, as in live play:
opponents cyan, teammates pink in team modes.

### A watched player's beam leaves their gun

Watching someone through their eyes (a demo or live spectating), their
Voltaic beam rose from below the bottom of the screen instead of from the gun
in view. Their body and its gun are hidden behind the camera then, and the beam
fell back to a point at the body's chest. The beam, and the Electrobolt rail,
now leave the barrel on screen, as your own do. Seen from outside, as in a
highlight reel's fly-by, they still leave the player's hand.

### Pretty no longer blurs every light

The Pretty preset turned the glow layer up to full, with Babylon's default
32-pixel blur, so every lamp and lit panel became an overbright, blurred blob.
Bloom was not the cause: Pretty with the glow off looked like Balanced. The
glow's blur is now 16 pixels, keeping it close to what is lit, and Pretty sets
glow to 0.35, a hint on lit strips and signs. Players who chose Pretty before
this keep their saved glow until they pick a preset again.

### The Mega Health no longer shows through floors

The Mega Health's cross was drawn in the first-person weapon's rendering
group, which clears depth before drawing so the gun is never hidden by a wall.
The cross was never hidden either: on wdm2 it showed through the floor above.
It is drawn with the world again and still reads through its shell, which
writes no depth. Every pickup is now tested to draw in the world's group.

### The gun's ammo counter works in demos

In a demo, the counter on the gun showed 0 while the HUD showed the followed
player's ammo. The HUD fed its number only to the live game's weapon, not to
the demo's own. The demo now gives its gun the same number the HUD shows.

### Frags and deaths on the demo timeline

A demo's frags and deaths are marked in a lane under the seek bar, coloured
from the followed player's side: gold for their frags, red for their deaths,
dim for everyone else's frags. Switching POV recolours them. Clicking a mark
seeks to three seconds before it, to see the frag coming. The marks come from
reading the whole recording in the background once playback starts (about
half a second for a ten-minute duel) without holding up playback. Suggested
by a player.

### The demo bar's keys match the menus

The demo player's buttons were three styles at three heights: a gold play
keycap, flat ink chips and the POV name plates. They are now the menu's raised
3D keys, all one height: ink at rest, gold for what is on (play, the game HUD,
the player you follow), with the same violet lift on hover. The speed key has
room for its widest value, so the bar no longer shifts as it changes.

### Aiming down sights shows in demos

Watching a player in a demo, their gun never came up to the sights and the
view never zoomed when they aimed. Snapshots do not carry the aim button, and
the demo's gun followed the viewer's own mouse instead. Recordings from format
v3 on include every player's buttons, so the demo now reads who held alt fire
in each frame: the gun comes up to the sights and the view zooms, with the
same zoom the local camera uses (now one shared `AdsZoom`). Live spectating
does not have the buttons, so it still shows the hip view.

### Demos play the game's music

Watching a demo or a highlight reel kept the menu music going. Playback now
switches to the game's playlist, as joining a lobby game does, and leaving it
switches back to the menus' music. Timedemo benchmarks stay silent.

### No dark line along the top of the screen

A thin dark line ran along the top of the screen in demos and matches. It was
the closed developer console: slid up out of view, it still cast its drop
shadow a few pixels down into the screen. The closed console is now hidden once
it has slid away, and still slides in and out as before.
