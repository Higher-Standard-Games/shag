# v0.9.30

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Summary

The v0.9.30 cycle continues toward 1.0 for the four modes (Duel, Amphi, FFA,
Search & Destroy) and the first public server.

Carried over from v0.9.29:

- Duel bots time the Red Armor less well than the Mega Health and the other
  armors: it sits on the map a median of about 35 seconds after respawning.
- A cap on concurrent matches in the lobby (R11 in the deployment notes). The
  player cap bounds matches only through the players in them.

## Changes

### Chat masks profanity consistently

Lobby, room and in-game chat now replace recognized English profanity with `***`,
including private rooms, team and spectator messages. Filtering happens on the
server before delivery and storage, so the sender sees the same text as everyone
else and histories and new demo recordings contain the masked version. Ordinary
game vocabulary and words containing harmless substrings remain intact. This is
a lightweight word filter with no automatic penalties; names are unchanged.
Strong profanity and two explicit slurs also match inside words, catching
added-letter bypasses. The general dictionary still uses whole-word matching to
preserve words such as "assassin" and "classic", with a Scunthorpe exception for
the stronger substring list.

### Video settings change in the lobby

Switching to the Pretty preset, or turning reflections on, during a match
could leave the screen showing the bare scene instead of the game until the
preset went back to Balanced. Until the renderer can change its passes
mid-match safely, the Video page in the match menu's Settings is locked, with a
note saying video settings change in the lobby; there it works as before.
Fullscreen still works in a match with Alt+Enter. In a match, **Reset page** is
off on Video and **Reset all** leaves video settings alone.

### Loading screens leave the console key to the keyboard

The start-up, map loading, match handoff and join screens show the lobby's
Profile, Settings and Console keys so nothing moves when the lobby takes over.
Console was the only one that worked there; all three now wait for the lobby.
The backquote key still opens the console on every screen.

### Highlight reels fit the demo

A highlight reel is at most a third of its demo, so a short recording no longer
offers a five-minute "highlight" of almost the whole match. A demo under three
minutes (shorter than an Amphi round) has no reel; 3–6 minutes offers 1 minute,
6–15 minutes adds 2, and 15 minutes or more adds 5. The Highlights key and the
lengths keep their places, disabled with a "Too short for a highlight reel"
tooltip, so cards line up whatever the demo's length.

### Players stay visible in highlight reels

A highlight reel's third-person shots (fly-bys) could leave a player out of the
picture. A first-person shot hides the followed player's body so the camera is
not inside their head, and cutting to a fly-by never showed it again: the shot
framed an empty spot. The body is now hidden only while the camera is behind
their eyes. In one reel, the followed player had been hidden in every
third-person frame; now in none.

### Demos are coloured from the followed player's side

A demo is watched through one player's eyes, but every player wore the enemy
colour, so a highlight reel's third-person shots showed the player you follow
as an opponent. The followed player now wears your own side's colour (pink by
default), and everyone else is coloured from their side, as in live play:
opponents cyan, teammates pink in team modes.

### A watched player's beam leaves their gun

Watching someone through their eyes (a demo or live spectating), their
Voltaic beam rose from below the bottom of the screen instead of from the gun
in view. Their body and its gun are hidden behind the camera then, and the beam
fell back to a point at the body's chest. The beam, and the Electrobolt rail,
now leave the barrel on screen, as your own do. Seen from outside, as in a
highlight reel's fly-by, they still leave the player's hand.

### Pretty no longer blurs every light

The Pretty preset turned the glow layer up to full, with Babylon's default
32-pixel blur, so every lamp and lit panel became an overbright, blurred blob.
Bloom was not the cause: Pretty with the glow off looked like Balanced. The
glow's blur is now 16 pixels, keeping it close to what is lit, and Pretty sets
glow to 0.35, a hint on lit strips and signs. Players who chose Pretty before
this keep their saved glow until they pick a preset again.

### The Mega Health no longer shows through floors

The Mega Health's cross was drawn in the first-person weapon's rendering
group, which clears depth before drawing so the gun is never hidden by a wall.
The cross was never hidden either: on wdm2 it showed through the floor above.
It is drawn with the world again and still reads through its shell, which
writes no depth. Every pickup is now tested to draw in the world's group.

### The gun's ammo counter works in demos

In a demo, the counter on the gun showed 0 while the HUD showed the followed
player's ammo. The HUD fed its number only to the live game's weapon, not to
the demo's own. The demo now gives its gun the same number the HUD shows.

### Frags and deaths on the demo timeline

A demo's frags and deaths are marked in a lane under the seek bar, coloured
from the followed player's side: gold for their frags, red for their deaths,
dim for everyone else's frags. Switching POV recolours them. Clicking a mark
seeks to three seconds before it, to see the frag coming. The marks come from
reading the whole recording in the background once playback starts (about
half a second for a ten-minute duel) without holding up playback. Suggested
by a player.

### The demo bar's keys match the menus

The demo player's buttons were three styles at three heights: a gold play
keycap, flat ink chips and the POV name plates. They are now the menu's raised
3D keys, all one height: ink at rest, gold for what is on (play, the game HUD,
the player you follow), with the same violet lift on hover. The speed key has
room for its widest value, so the bar no longer shifts as it changes.

### Aiming down sights shows in demos

Watching a player in a demo, their gun never came up to the sights and the
view never zoomed when they aimed. Snapshots do not carry the aim button, and
the demo's gun followed the viewer's own mouse instead. Recordings from format
v3 on include every player's buttons, so the demo now reads who held alt fire
in each frame: the gun comes up to the sights and the view zooms, with the
same zoom the local camera uses (now one shared `AdsZoom`). Live spectating
does not have the buttons, so it still shows the hip view.

### Demos play the game's music

Watching a demo or a highlight reel kept the menu music going. Playback now
switches to the game's playlist, as joining a lobby game does, and leaving it
switches back to the menus' music. Timedemo benchmarks stay silent.

### No dark line along the top of the screen

A thin dark line ran along the top of the screen in demos and matches. It was
the closed developer console: slid up out of view, it still cast its drop
shadow a few pixels down into the screen. The closed console is now hidden once
it has slid away, and still slides in and out as before.
