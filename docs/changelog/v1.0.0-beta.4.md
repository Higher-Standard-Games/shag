# v1.0.0-beta.4

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### Small health bubbles are taken at full health

A +5 health bubble now overheals to 200, like the mega, and decays back to 100
at 1 HP a second. Before, it stopped at 100 and stayed on the map when you
walked over it at full health. Bots go for bubbles at full health as well; one
is left on the map only at 200 HP.

### Fewer frame drops in fights (WebGPU)

Heavy frame drops in a full dm5 duel came from two places. Major garbage
collections paused the game for up to 200 ms: Babylon names every render pass
and command encoder with the frame number, and the browser kept each of those
names until a collection had to walk them all. Those names are now the same
string every frame. And while an explosion was alive, every expiring spark
re-uploaded the explosion buffers whole, sized for 96 explosions, which took
about 7 ms a frame; only the live, changed part is uploaded now. Two smaller
sources of garbage, in the soft-particle depth pass and the world fog of
effects, are gone as well. Over the whole ten-minute demo, frames over 25 ms
fell from 328 to 22 and frames over 50 ms from 11 to none. The measurements
are in `docs/research/webgpu-frame-spikes-2026-11-03.md`.

### Smoother weapon switches (WebGPU)

A weapon switch built the new view model from scratch and threw the old one
away: about 10 ms for the switch and another 45 ms spread over the frames
after it, which showed as a stutter on nearly every switch. The view now keeps
each weapon it has shown, with its barrels and ammo counter, and brings it out
again on the next switch to it: 2.6 ms for the switch, 9 ms over the frames
after it.

Rockets and grenades also stopped interrupting the WebGPU frame replay twice
per shot: their fade-in ended at full opacity, which moved them between draw
queues. They now fade to just short of it. Over the dm5 duel, replay stops
fell from 957 to 771.

The blade's slash trail and other players' blades went the same way: each
swing built a trail mesh, material and texture and threw them away, and each
switch to the blade built a new blade for that player. Both are now kept and
reused. With these changes the replay stops over the dm5 duel fell to 482,
and to about 30 a minute once the match is under way.

### Weapons, projectiles and pickups no longer vanish (WebGPU)

With WebGPU frame replay on, a reused view weapon (the blade after switching
back to it) and reused projectiles (plasma bolts from the pool) could stay
invisible until something else made the renderer record a new frame, which
in practice meant firing a burst of new shots. Pickups were affected the same
way. A frame recording leaves out a mesh hidden by a disabled parent, since
forcing the parent on would show its other children, and the pools put
things away by disabling their parent node; when one came back, nothing in
the draw list had changed, so the old recording kept replaying without it.
The replay now stops whenever a mesh the recording could not draw is shown,
and projectiles and pickups hide their own meshes, which a recording can
hold, so bringing one back needs no new recording.

### No weapon shaders compiled in the middle of a match (WebGPU)

The view weapons were compiled behind the loading screen before the gameplay
lights existed, and the muzzle-flash light changed the order of each weapon's
lights whenever a weapon was switched to. Both gave a weapon a shader the
loading screen had not built, so the first time it was held in a fight it
compiled one: a 45 ms frame and, half a second later, a stall of 130 to
180 ms while the GPU process built the pipeline. The gameplay lights are now
in place before the weapons are compiled, and every view weapon keeps the
same lights in the same order from load on. Over the first six minutes of the
dm5 duel no shader is compiled during play.
