# v0.9.36

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

- Firefox WebGL reuses static PBR materials for remote held weapons, avoiding
  fresh material clones on weapon switches and respawns. Weapon rigs and
  animation stay independent; animated/custom materials retain their clone
  path. Chrome and WebGPU keep the existing behavior. Actual-asset tests show
  lower weapon setup cost with identical rendered pixels; overall combat FPS
  remains variable and is not claimed as a measured gain.

### Netcode: no more teleporting back on a busy or slow connection

- **A queueing upload no longer throws you back and forth.** When your upload
  backs up (another upload, a cloud sync, weak Wi-Fi), the server can take most
  of a second to see your commands. Past 32 unacknowledged commands the client
  used to give up, jump to the server's position — a whole round trip old —
  and discard what you had pressed, on every snapshot while the delay lasted;
  the server then ran those commands anyway and moved you forward again. The
  client now replays up to two seconds of commands, and the server keeps them
  all.
- **A stall now costs nothing in the end.** When your commands stop arriving,
  the server holds you still and owes you the time, instead of guessing your
  movement and correcting you later. When they arrive, it runs every one of
  them, two per tick until it has caught up. Other players see a brief hold,
  then a quick catch-up. Past half a second of silence it goes back to
  guessing, so nobody can hang frozen or in slow motion by withholding
  commands, and catch-up only repays time already waited, so sending extra
  commands gains nothing. In a simulated match with an upload queue like a
  tester's (to about 900 ms), visible corrections over 5 units went from 300
  in 30 seconds to none; 800 ms spikes went from 72 corrections of up to
  160 units to none.
- **Crouch slides no longer snap at higher ping.** Correcting your position
  replays the commands the server has not acknowledged yet, and that replay
  started from your newest movement state: a slide entered after the
  acknowledged command "ended" on the first replayed step, started its
  cooldown and refused to start again. At 100 ms that was a 36-unit snap, back
  and forth, on every slide. The replay now starts from the movement state you
  had at the acknowledged command.
- **Snapshots that agree with your prediction are left alone.** The client
  compares each snapshot with what it predicted for the same command and, when
  they match, keeps its own state instead of re-simulating. That is nearly
  every snapshot, and reconciliation now costs a fraction of what it did.
- **Bots send one command per server tick by the clock,** instead of one per
  timer firing, which on Windows hosts was about half the tick rate.
- **The net graph shows corrections and where the delay is.** `corr` / `max`
  count visible prediction corrections (the old `recon` row never counted
  anything), and `up` / `down` show extra delay on your upload and download
  over the best of the last 30 seconds — a high ping with a high `up` is a
  congested upload.
