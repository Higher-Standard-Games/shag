# v1.0.0-beta.12

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### Players

- The post-game screen now plays a victory clip on the winner's model, one of
  six picked at random with equal odds: three dances and three taunts, new in
  `washoi_player_animations.pk3`. The rebuilt archive must be published with
  the release.
- After about three kills in ten, the killer's third-person model taunts over
  its upper body while its legs keep running. Bots taunt too. Every viewer and
  demo replay sees the same taunt, chosen from the death event with no network
  change. The next shot, aiming down sights, a weapon switch, swimming,
  climbing, a wall jump or a slide ends it. `animtest once <clip>` plays a
  clip over the current one.

### Bots: measured against people

- `bot:demo-motion` measures how players move and look in recorded matches,
  people and bots by the same code: speed, airtime, distance from walls, view
  turning, flicks and their corrections, hop chains, pitch. `bot:motion
  --keep-demo` keeps its match demo so today's bots are measured the same way.
  Before, bot movement targets were guesses; there was no measure of people.

### Bots: a calmer view out of a fight

- Out of a fight with no enemy in sight, a bot's view eases where it wants to
  look (at most 120°/s, small changes ignored), rests about 10° below level at
  the floor ahead, and glances aside every few seconds instead of leaning
  toward open space all the time. Turnarounds still snap after the reaction
  time. Before, every new wanted view was turned to at once: bots turned
  110–145°/s out of a fight against people's 35, and ran with the view dead
  level where people look 11° down. Measured now: 96–102°/s, 9° down, and
  about a third fewer view reversals.

### Bots: hear as players hear

- Bots hear footsteps when a player's client plays them: on the ground faster
  than 70 u/s and not sliding, where they used to need 250 u/s, so a person
  walking slowly was silent to bots while bots were never silent to people.
  Jumps are heard from 60 u/s up (was 200) out to 720 units (was 800), and
  landings are no longer heard: players hear no landing sound, only the pain
  of a hard one. Ranges come from the game's sound falloff and the volume each
  sound plays at.

### Bots: hunt a known enemy instead of always shopping

- A bot under 25 armor used to go for items whenever it was out of a fight,
  which is every bot after it spawns; hunting was 1–2% of the time it spent
  roaming. Now, with an enemy to hunt, it shops only for an item worth the run
  against the gametype's `aggression` weight, and hunts otherwise; with no idea
  where anyone is it still runs its item cycle. In 4-bot Deathmatch on
  washoi_dm2 item runs fell from 86% to 55–74% of roaming time (item runs,
  hunting and retreats), chasing nearly doubled, and deaths and pickups held.

### Bots: head-to-head testing

- `bot:ab` plays many headless matches of one bot variant against another and
  says whether either is stronger, with a 95% interval on the frag gap and a
  sign test on wins. A single two-bot Duel cannot tell a change from luck.

### Bots: reactions and flicks like a hand

- A bot's wait before a big turn is drawn log-normal round its preset's
  reaction time instead of ±20% evenly, so now and then it is slow to notice,
  as a person is. Some big reactive turns overshoot by 4–12% and come back,
  more often at lower aim accuracy.

### Fixed: bots knew no one's pickups, not even their own

- The server names the picker of an item pickup by its bare numeric id, where
  every other event carries the client key, and the bot's perception matched
  neither. So a bot never knew which enemy took an armor, Mega Health or
  powerup (the cautious mood against an armoured enemy could never start),
  never heard a pickup as a sound from its taker, and timed even the items it
  took itself as though it had only heard them, with its timing error.

### Bots: a read of the match

- Bots keep a read of how the match is going, from what they saw, heard and
  did: their own health and armor over time, an estimate of each enemy's
  (spawn values, the hits they land split by the armor rule, the pain each hit
  gave away, the major items the enemy was seen or heard taking), who took the
  major items, each fight and how it ended, and the score. Nothing uses it to
  decide yet; the strategy layer that follows does. Fresh estimates are within
  25 points of the truth 82–89% of the time.

### Bots: play to win, and learn the opponent

- Hard and Insane bots read the match and change plan: understacked, they
  stop taking even fights and collect; losing the major items, they time and
  deny them; with the enemy weak or the score behind late, they press; ahead
  late, they protect the lead; set on by several enemies, they get out. Medium
  bots only recover and protect a lead; Easy bots play as before. Within a
  match a bot also learns which strategy works against its opponent, and
  contests an item its opponent keeps taking. A bot that knows a hit would
  kill switches to the gun that does it.
- Bots run into recorded trickjumps instead of stopping on a mark first,
  joining the trick's run-up once a simulation from where they will be says it
  lands. On washoi_dm1 time standing still fell from 15% to 10–12%.

### Bots: leave a losing fight, and learn each map

- A bot that is recovering or protecting a lead leaves a fight that has cost
  it 40 damage without an edge, and a bot retreating from an enemy in sight
  first gets out of its sight, then heads for health. Before, a retreat ran
  for the best health item, past the enemy if that was the way.
- At the end of a match each bot hands in a report, with no names: its fights
  by map area, against a person or a bot, and how each strategy paid. The bot
  server keeps them for 90 days in `bot-intel.db` in its data directory
  (`--no-intel` to keep none). `bot:intel` turns them into a checked-in
  baseline per map, `config/bot-intel/<map>.json`, which bots start from.

### Bots: strafe-jump along the route

- Bots strafe-jump along the route they are running, bending with it, where
  before a hop needed a long straight stretch that these maps rarely have and
  almost never started. Time in the air while running rose from 18–22% to
  28–32%; people spend about 42%.

### Bots: who took the armor

- The server names the taker of weapons only, so bots never knew an opponent
  had taken an armor or a Mega Health. They now see an item vanish and who is
  at it, and in a Duel know that an item they find gone was taken by the
  opponent. The strategy layer acts on it; its item-control strategy is played
  only when the bot is stacked enough to win the fight at the item.
- Bots invited from a room store their match report in `bot-intel.db`;
  before, only bots added directly did.
- Bots no longer assume an unseen person is fresh from spawning. How people's
  health and armor grow with time since they spawned is measured from their
  demos by `bot:people` into `config/bot-intel/people.json`, and a bot's
  estimate of a person leans on it the longer they are out of sight. In a dm2
  Duel the bot had put a person at about 80 when they averaged 148.

### Bots: tricks learned from people's matches

- `bot:learn` learns tricks from match demos as well as training demos, from
  the people in them. A match server records positions every other tick, and
  the learner replayed one command per position, so every trick in a match
  demo missed; it now replays every command.
- The dm2 and dm5 route libraries grow from 77 and 46 routes to 161 and 121,
  learned from two Duels and a climbing demo by a person, among them the wall
  jump up to the dm2 Red Armor. Bots with them beat bots without by about one
  frag a match on both maps (24 Duels each; not yet shown).
- A trick that lands where the bots had no waypoint adds one there, linked
  to the waypoints around it. Links are now made only where a player can walk,
  and a trick whose landing can walk nowhere is left out. Before, links ran
  through walls and over rails, and bots stood against them: in a person's
  dm2 Duel the bot stood still more than twice as long as with the old
  routes. Headless, bots now stand still 4–6% of the time with the new
  routes, where they stood 8–10%. `bot:motion --routes-root` compares two
  route libraries.
- A bot creeping onto the start of a learned trick stays crouched between
  pushes. Before, it stood up and crouched again several times a second,
  and its model bobbed.
