# v1.0.0-beta.12

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### Deployment

- US activation now removes old build stages from `/srv/washoi/.pending/`,
  keeping the activated build and the one before it. Each stage is about
  0.6 GB and none were ever removed, so 28 of them had taken 14 GB of the
  US host's 40 GB disk.
- The release procedure ends with a disk check on both hosts that suggests
  old backups and staging copies for removal.

### Players

- The post-game screen now plays a victory clip on the winner's model, one of
  six picked at random with equal odds: three dances and three taunts, new in
  `washoi_player_animations.pk3`. The rebuilt archive must be published with
  the release.
- After about three kills in ten, the killer's third-person model taunts over
  its upper body while its legs keep running. Bots taunt too. Every viewer and
  demo replay sees the same taunt, chosen from the death event with no network
  change. The next shot, aiming down sights, a weapon switch, swimming,
  climbing, a wall jump or a slide ends it. `animtest once <clip>` plays a
  clip over the current one.
- The room's **Add bot** key has an arrow beside it that opens a skill menu:
  Easy, Medium, Hard or Insane. Add bot itself still lets the server choose:
  Hard, or Insane for a strong rating, which was the only choice before.

### Weapons

- Picking up a weapon you already hold, from the map or from the floor, plays
  the pickup sound and shows its name. It gave the ammo silently: the sound and
  the toast followed only a weapon newly added to the inventory.
- The blade's outline is as thick as every other weapon's, under Quad too.
  Outline widths were divided by the weapon root's scale only; the Arc Blade
  model's meshes sit under a further 0.13 scale, so its outlines drew at about
  a seventh of their width.
- The blade's charge outline grows smoothly from the normal outline width to
  four times it at full charge.

### Maps

- Fog volumes (`fogparms` brushes) are drawn: what lies inside them fades into
  the fog's colour with distance, as qfusion draws it, including its clear
  distance (DM17's `fogparms ( .04 0.36 .04 ) 500 5`). DM17's green pits and
  fog wall showed as black holes: nothing drew the fog, and the fog brush's
  own face was drawn as an opaque black surface. The fog works on WebGL2 and
  on WebGPU with frame snapshots.
- Four new maps: Washoi DM16 (FFA), Washoi DM17 (Duel and FFA), Washoi DM18
  (FFA) and Washoi Amphi3 (Amphi). They are compiled from warfork-maps
  `wswmaps/wdm16`, `wdm17`, `wdm18` and `wda2` at `4067818` with MakeBSP:
  Warfork's own BSPs of these maps are 2015 builds from before its 2026
  model updates. Placed models keep q3map2's collision (none, or per-triangle
  for the solid ones), so no model hull closes a doorway.
- `func_button` works as in Warsow. A button with `health` opens its doors when
  it is shot or caught in a blast, and ignores touch. Before, a button opened
  its doors for anyone inside its bounding box, and no shipped map had one;
  DM16's button box spans the doorway, so its door would have opened for
  anyone walking up to it. A button's brush is now drawn and solid and
  presses in while it rests; DM16's is an invisible brush behind its button
  models, as in Warsow. No snapshot or demo change: the press
  comes from the lift callers' cooldowns already in `lift_state`.
- `misc_particles` emitters are drawn: the smoke behind Amphi3's and Amphi2's
  glass and the bubbles over DM17's slime. Amphi2 had five emitters that drew
  nothing. The map-media packager now collects an entity's `shaderName`, so
  the sprite images ship in the map's texture bank.
- Map particles are drawn as their image: they came out black, because the
  sprite material took its colour from a diffuse term that is zero with
  lighting off. They now draw before the map's additive surfaces, so Amphi3's
  glass shows over the smoke behind it instead of the smoke showing through.
- Some textures on DM16, DM17, DM18 and Amphi3 were 2-4 times too large:
  Warfork replaced their images with larger ones in 2026 (the teleporter
  signs, `decal_drop`, the refly bricks, `cement_3`, `rocks005`), and a map's
  texture scale is in image pixels. The sources are rescaled for the new
  images, so a teleporter sign shows the whole sign instead of a magnified
  corner.
- MakeLight has no q3map2 `_floodlight`. The new maps use its macro-occlusion
  ambient instead (`ambient 6` with the floodlight colour; DM18, which took
  its warm orange light from its floodlight, `ambient 24`).
- DM16's handrails no longer leave an invisible ledge beside them: they are
  flat sheets, and the per-polygon collision they had pushed through both
  sides. They use the compiler's default solid collision.
- The teleporter frames on DM16 and DM17 stand on the floor. Warfork's 2026
  teleporter model has its base 10 units below its origin, and the maps still
  placed it for the old model.
- DM17's barrels name `textures/metal/orangewall_vertex`, which no media set
  defines; `config/map-material-aliases.shader` maps it to Warfork's
  `orangewall` set.

### Video export

- The export dialog has an **Advanced** section. Its **Video** choice adds
  **Full match with game HUD**: the whole recording, at normal speed, through
  the eyes of the player chosen under **Follow player**, with the game's own
  HUD in every frame (vitals, weapons, timer, kill feed, chat, crosshair)
  and stereo game audio. Recordings of 10 seconds to
  20 minutes qualify; the MP4 is 1080p60 H.264 at 12 Mbit/s. Before, export
  made highlight reels only, without HUD.
- The HUD reaches the video as the page's own DOM, drawn through an SVG image
  at 1920×1080 with the UI scale of that size. Its fonts are cut down in memory
  to the faces and characters the HUD shows (the whole Rajdhani family is
  1.8 MB, mostly Devanagari), which took the HUD's cost from about 100 ms to
  about 25 ms a frame.
- HUD timers and fades (the update loop, frag and pickup messages, chat fade,
  damage arrows, death countdown, hints) run on presentation time, which an
  export owns and steps frame by frame; CSS animations are paused and stepped
  the same way. In live play presentation time is the wall clock, so nothing
  changes there.
- A full match's audio is mixed after its picture, in 30-second windows at one
  level for the whole mix, and added to the finished file without re-encoding
  the video. Highlight reels mix in one piece as before.

### Engine: performance and code quality

- Fewer per-frame allocations: the free-fly camera reuses its vectors, predicted
  projectile impacts are pruned in place, the water reflection finds the nearest
  pool in one pass, and the voltaic sound reuses its working sets.
- The local game host kept interpolation state for every entity that ever
  existed; every projectile left an entry behind for the session. Entities that
  are gone are now forgotten.
- The lobby re-read every demo file (about 400 MB on the production server)
  each minute to list them. It now reads only new or changed files.
- The map builder decides once per texture, rather than once per face, whether
  its faces are drawn.
- The weapon rack and the snapshot broadcaster detect weapon changes without
  rebuilding JSON every frame or tick.
- Player names in the kill feed, the phase banners, the hints and the console
  pass through one HTML escaper that also escapes quotes. The phase banner's
  escaper created a DOM element on every call.
- Rain started again during its fade-out went silent: the old fade kept
  lowering the volume and then paused the sound. One fade runs at a time.
- A WebSocket client that closed without a reason was logged with an empty
  reason instead of its close code, and client errors are logged as warnings.
- `PK3Loader.listFiles` treats characters such as `.`, `+` and `(` in a pattern
  literally; only `*` and `?` are wildcards. Listing a directory is linear in
  the index size instead of quadratic.
- Removed dead code: the engine's unused `GameServer` scaffold and the
  `BspTest` console helpers, which pointed at maps that no longer exist.
- Singletons reset to a typed `null` instead of a cast, the network host
  accepts the FPS simulation without an `any` cast, and the effects that
  `GameEvents` and `FrameEffects` set up report readiness through
  `isInitialized()` instead of reading private fields.

### Bots: measured against people

- `bot:demo-motion` measures how players move and look in recorded matches,
  people and bots by the same code: speed, airtime, distance from walls, view
  turning, flicks and their corrections, hop chains, pitch. `bot:motion
  --keep-demo` keeps its match demo so today's bots are measured the same way.
  Before, bot movement targets were guesses; there was no measure of people.

### Bots: a calmer view out of a fight

- Out of a fight with no enemy in sight, a bot's view eases where it wants to
  look (at most 120°/s, small changes ignored), rests about 10° below level at
  the floor ahead, and glances aside every few seconds instead of leaning
  toward open space all the time. Turnarounds still snap after the reaction
  time. Before, every new wanted view was turned to at once: bots turned
  110–145°/s out of a fight against people's 35, and ran with the view dead
  level where people look 11° down. Measured now: 96–102°/s, 9° down, and
  about a third fewer view reversals.

### Bots: hear as players hear

- Bots hear footsteps when a player's client plays them: on the ground faster
  than 70 u/s and not sliding, where they used to need 250 u/s, so a person
  walking slowly was silent to bots while bots were never silent to people.
  Jumps are heard from 60 u/s up (was 200) out to 720 units (was 800), and
  landings are no longer heard: players hear no landing sound, only the pain
  of a hard one. Ranges come from the game's sound falloff and the volume each
  sound plays at.

### Bots: hunt a known enemy instead of always shopping

- A bot under 25 armor used to go for items whenever it was out of a fight,
  which is every bot after it spawns; hunting was 1–2% of the time it spent
  roaming. Now, with an enemy to hunt, it shops only for an item worth the run
  against the gametype's `aggression` weight, and hunts otherwise; with no idea
  where anyone is it still runs its item cycle. In 4-bot Deathmatch on
  washoi_dm2 item runs fell from 86% to 55–74% of roaming time (item runs,
  hunting and retreats), chasing nearly doubled, and deaths and pickups held.

### Bots: head-to-head testing

- `bot:ab` plays many headless matches of one bot variant against another and
  says whether either is stronger, with a 95% interval on the frag gap and a
  sign test on wins. A single two-bot Duel cannot tell a change from luck.

### Bots: reactions and flicks like a hand

- A bot's wait before a big turn is drawn log-normal round its preset's
  reaction time instead of ±20% evenly, so now and then it is slow to notice,
  as a person is. Some big reactive turns overshoot by 4–12% and come back,
  more often at lower aim accuracy.

### Fixed: bots knew no one's pickups, not even their own

- The server names the picker of an item pickup by its bare numeric id, where
  every other event carries the client key, and the bot's perception matched
  neither. So a bot never knew which enemy took an armor, Mega Health or
  powerup (the cautious mood against an armoured enemy could never start),
  never heard a pickup as a sound from its taker, and timed even the items it
  took itself as though it had only heard them, with its timing error.

### Bots: a read of the match

- Bots keep a read of how the match is going, from what they saw, heard and
  did: their own health and armor over time, an estimate of each enemy's
  (spawn values, the hits they land split by the armor rule, the pain each hit
  gave away, the major items the enemy was seen or heard taking), who took the
  major items, each fight and how it ended, and the score. Nothing uses it to
  decide yet; the strategy layer that follows does. Fresh estimates are within
  25 points of the truth 82–89% of the time.

### Bots: play to win, and learn the opponent

- Hard and Insane bots read the match and change plan: understacked, they
  stop taking even fights and collect; losing the major items, they time and
  deny them; with the enemy weak or the score behind late, they press; ahead
  late, they protect the lead; set on by several enemies, they get out. Medium
  bots only recover and protect a lead; Easy bots play as before. Within a
  match a bot also learns which strategy works against its opponent, and
  contests an item its opponent keeps taking. A bot that knows a hit would
  kill switches to the gun that does it.
- Bots run into recorded trickjumps instead of stopping on a mark first,
  joining the trick's run-up once a simulation from where they will be says it
  lands. On washoi_dm1 time standing still fell from 15% to 10–12%.

### Bots: leave a losing fight, and learn each map

- A bot that is recovering or protecting a lead leaves a fight that has cost
  it 40 damage without an edge, and a bot retreating from an enemy in sight
  first gets out of its sight, then heads for health. Before, a retreat ran
  for the best health item, past the enemy if that was the way.
- At the end of a match each bot hands in a report, with no names: its fights
  by map area, against a person or a bot, and how each strategy paid. The bot
  server keeps them for 90 days in `bot-intel.db` in its data directory
  (`--no-intel` to keep none). `bot:intel` turns them into a checked-in
  baseline per map, `config/bot-intel/<map>.json`, which bots start from.

### Bots: strafe-jump along the route

- Bots strafe-jump along the route they are running, bending with it, where
  before a hop needed a long straight stretch that these maps rarely have and
  almost never started. Time in the air while running rose from 18–22% to
  28–32%; people spend about 42%.

### Bots: who took the armor

- The server names the taker of weapons only, so bots never knew an opponent
  had taken an armor or a Mega Health. They now see an item vanish and who is
  at it, and in a Duel know that an item they find gone was taken by the
  opponent. The strategy layer acts on it; its item-control strategy is played
  only when the bot is stacked enough to win the fight at the item.
- Bots invited from a room store their match report in `bot-intel.db`;
  before, only bots added directly did.
- Bots no longer assume an unseen person is fresh from spawning. How people's
  health and armor grow with time since they spawned is measured from their
  demos by `bot:people` into `config/bot-intel/people.json`, and a bot's
  estimate of a person leans on it the longer they are out of sight. In a dm2
  Duel the bot had put a person at about 80 when they averaged 148.

### Bots: go for the Red Armor, and hunt when stacked

- In Duel every major item counted the same to a bot: the mode's major-item
  bias pushed the Red Armor, the Yellow Armor and the Mega all to the top of
  the scale, and the run there halved an item's pull every 1,200 units for
  every item alike, so the nearest won. A Red Armor up a climb stood untaken
  for 20–30 s at a time in a person's Duel. The bias now raises major items
  in order, Red Armor first, a run to a major item counts less where the mode
  values them more, and denying the Red Armor counts above denying the Mega.
  Against a bot that takes every Red Armor, a Hard bot went from about 1.2
  frags a match behind to 0.7 ahead (24 Duels; not yet shown).
- A Hard or Insane bot with at least 150 health and armor, 40 over its
  estimate of the opponent, plays Pressure: it hunts instead of shopping for
  more.
- A hit holds a bot on its attacker for 1.5 s (`hitAttentionMs` in the
  difficulty preset): it turns round, and fights once it sees them. It was
  held a quarter second, shorter than a reaction and a turn, so a bot shot
  in the back by a chaser turned part way and ran on to its item.

### Bots: Search & Destroy teams attack together

- Attacking bots split into two groups: the carrier with two others, and a
  pair. Each group takes its own way into the target site, waits outside it,
  and all go in together once the carrier's group and the other are there,
  8 s after the carrier's group is, 25 s after setting off, or as soon as one
  of them falls. A site with one way in is attacked together. Before, all five ran one route behind the
  carrier. The ways in are found per map by `bot:lanes` and checked in at
  `config/bot-lanes`; snd1, snd4 and snd6 have two or three per site.
- When a carrier falls, one attacker fetches the bomb while the next two cover
  it, and the two defenders nearest guard it. Before, defenders ignored a
  dropped bomb.
- Defenders keep one bot on each site and send the others to the site the
  carrier is heading for once one of them sees it; an armed bomb is retaken
  together instead of one at a time. After a plant, attackers hold towards
  the ways in. Each side remembers which site worked this match.
- Defenders hold each site from set spots, high ground that sees the site and
  the attack's ways in, looking at the likeliest way in; after a plant the
  attackers do the same against the defence. Before, they wandered the site
  floor looking at its middle. On snd6 the defence went from winning 15% of
  its rounds to 28% against the same team without holds (`bot:lanes` finds
  the spots, kept in `config/bot-lanes`).
- A bot with a gametype order that was wedged against a wall stayed there all
  round: stuck bots with an order were never recovered. On snd1 the carrier
  and its group stood at one spot near the attackers' spawn until the round
  ran out; snd1 attacks now win most rounds instead of timing out.
- At range, bots fight from the edge of cover: they step out to shoot and back
  behind a pillar or doorframe while the weapon reloads, or between bursts of
  a fast weapon. Measured even in Duel (14–10 wins over 30 matches).
- Outside Duel too, a bot above its enemy keeps the high ground instead of
  stepping off the ledge.
- Bots call what they see in team chat by the map's place names ("2 enemies
  at Old Town", "Bomb carrier at Warehouse B", "Bomb dropped at Station") and
  act on each other's calls. A person's `a`/`b` in team chat sends their bot
  teammates to that site; a person carrying the bomb is not made to wait.
- `%L` in chat is replaced by the sender's place on maps that name places.
- `bot:ab --gametype snd` plays team against team and reports rounds won
  attacking and defending, plants and kills per death.

### Bots: Search & Destroy carriers plant

- On the Search & Destroy maps, the bomb carrier and its escort used to run
  circles at the attackers' spawn for most of each round: the bots' waypoints
  sat at the map's spawn points, which on these maps float up to 136 units
  over the floor. A spawn waypoint floating more than 64 units now goes onto
  the floor beneath (snd1 14 of 31 spawns, snd4 27 of 30, snd6 10 of 33; also
  dm7 6 and dm11 4).
- Orders to a site point at a spot on the site's floor, inside it; the site's
  own point floats too, and a carrier sent there ran on through the site too
  fast to plant. The attack commits to its site once a round instead of
  re-choosing it as the bomb moves.
- A gametype order is discounted for distance less the more it matters, so a
  bomb site across the map no longer loses to a pickup beside the carrier.
- In 5-vs-5 Hard matches of about seven minutes, plants went from none on
  snd1 and 2 in 5 rounds on snd4 to 1 in 7 on snd1, 5 in 6 on snd4 and 6 in 7
  on snd6. Defenders now lose most rounds on snd4 and snd6.
- `bot:motion --gametype snd` runs Search & Destroy, and `--trace` explains
  what bots were doing while they stood still. The bot option `weights`
  multiplies a gametype's weights for head-to-head tests.

### Bots: the blade's charged blast

- A bot keeps a blade charge through a moment out of sight or a dodge, up to
  2 s, and lets it go on the aim, waiting up to half a second for the view to
  get there. Before, losing sight let go early, which cancels a charge under
  half a second on the server, while the bot's own clock ran on and let the
  next charge go too early as well; it could charge again and again without
  a blast. A bot also no longer aims down sights with the blade still out
  mid-switch, which started a blade charge the switch then cancelled.

### Bots: tricks learned from people's matches

- `bot:learn` learns tricks from match demos as well as training demos, from
  the people in them. A match server records positions every other tick, and
  the learner replayed one command per position, so every trick in a match
  demo missed; it now replays every command.
- The dm2 and dm5 route libraries grow from 77 and 46 routes to 161 and 121,
  learned from two Duels and a climbing demo by a person, among them the wall
  jump up to the dm2 Red Armor. Bots with them beat bots without by about one
  frag a match on both maps (24 Duels each; not yet shown).
- A trick that lands where the bots had no waypoint adds one there, linked
  to the waypoints around it. Links are now made only where a player can walk,
  and a trick whose landing can walk nowhere is left out. Before, links ran
  through walls and over rails, and bots stood against them: in a person's
  dm2 Duel the bot stood still more than twice as long as with the old
  routes. Headless, bots now stand still 4–6% of the time with the new
  routes, where they stood 8–10%. `bot:motion --routes-root` compares two
  route libraries.
- A bot creeping onto the start of a learned trick stays crouched between
  pushes. Before, it stood up and crouched again several times a second,
  and its model bobbed.

### Demo playback

- The HUD's frag count shows the followed player's score. In a demo it stayed
  at 0 all match, because it looked for a local player, and a demo has none;
  a live spectator saw 0 for the same reason.
- Holding **Tab** shows the scoreboard over a demo, as in a match, with the
  followed player's row marked. Moving to the previous or next player's eyes
  is now **Q** / **E** (it was Tab and Shift+Tab).
- A demo opens with the players' names over its first three seconds, as a
  highlight reel's title card shows them. At its end the picture fades to black
  under an end card: the winner, the top fragger in team matches, and the
  demo's frags and length, with **Replay** and **Exit** keys. A recording stops
  as the post-game begins, so the winner comes from the final scores. A
  full-match video carries both: the names over its opening, and five seconds
  at its end that fade to black and hold the end card, centred and without keys.
- The manual said a recording runs through the post-game, and that a
  full-match video shows the post-game scoreboard; both were wrong and are
  corrected.
