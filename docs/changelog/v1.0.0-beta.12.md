# v1.0.0-beta.12

**Release Date:** unreleased — living changelog

## Changes

### Players

- The post-game screen now plays a victory clip on the winner's model, one of
  six picked at random with equal odds: three dances and three taunts, new in
  `washoi_player_animations.pk3`. The rebuilt archive must be published with
  the release.
- After about three kills in ten, the killer's third-person model taunts over
  its upper body while its legs keep running. Bots taunt too. Every viewer and
  demo replay sees the same taunt, chosen from the death event with no network
  change. The next shot, aiming down sights, a weapon switch, swimming,
  climbing, a wall jump or a slide ends it. `animtest once <clip>` plays a
  clip over the current one.

### Weapons

- Picking up a weapon you already hold, from the map or from the floor, plays
  the pickup sound and shows its name. It gave the ammo silently: the sound and
  the toast followed only a weapon newly added to the inventory.
- The blade's outline is as thick as every other weapon's, under Quad too,
  and its charge outline grows to its full width again. Outline widths were
  divided by the weapon root's scale only; the Arc Blade model's meshes sit
  under a further 0.13 scale, so its outlines drew at about a seventh of their
  width and the charge outline was barely visible.

### Maps

- Three new maps: Washoi DM16 (FFA), Washoi DM17 (Duel and FFA) and Washoi
  Amphi3 (Amphi). They are compiled from warfork-maps
  `wswmaps/wdm16`, `wdm17` and `wda2` at `4067818` with MakeBSP:
  Warfork's own BSPs of these maps are 2015 builds from before its 2026
  model updates. Placed models keep q3map2's collision (none, or per-triangle
  for the solid ones), so no model hull closes a doorway.
- `func_button` works as in Warsow. A button with `health` opens its doors when
  it is shot or caught in a blast, and ignores touch. Before, a button opened
  its doors for anyone inside its bounding box, and no shipped map had one;
  DM16's button box spans the doorway, so its door would have opened for
  anyone walking up to it. A button's brush is now drawn and solid and
  presses in while it rests; DM16's is an invisible brush behind its button
  models, as in Warsow. No snapshot or demo change: the press
  comes from the lift callers' cooldowns already in `lift_state`.
- `misc_particles` emitters are drawn: the smoke behind Amphi3's and Amphi2's
  glass and the bubbles over DM17's slime. Amphi2 had five emitters that drew
  nothing. The map-media packager now collects an entity's `shaderName`, so
  the sprite images ship in the map's texture bank.
- Map particles are drawn as their image: they came out black, because the
  sprite material took its colour from a diffuse term that is zero with
  lighting off. They now draw before the map's additive surfaces, so Amphi3's
  glass shows over the smoke behind it instead of the smoke showing through.
- Some textures on DM16, DM17 and Amphi3 were 2-4 times too large:
  Warfork replaced their images with larger ones in 2026 (the teleporter
  signs, `decal_drop`, the refly bricks, `cement_3`, `rocks005`), and a map's
  texture scale is in image pixels. The sources are rescaled for the new
  images, so a teleporter sign shows the whole sign instead of a magnified
  corner.
- MakeLight has no q3map2 `_floodlight`. The new maps use its macro-occlusion
  ambient instead (`ambient 6` with the floodlight colour).
- DM16's handrails no longer leave an invisible ledge beside them: they are
  flat sheets, and the per-polygon collision they had pushed through both
  sides. They use the compiler's default solid collision.
- The teleporter frames on DM16 and DM17 stand on the floor. Warfork's 2026
  teleporter model has its base 10 units below its origin, and the maps still
  placed it for the old model.
- DM17's barrels name `textures/metal/orangewall_vertex`, which no media set
  defines; `config/map-material-aliases.shader` maps it to Warfork's
  `orangewall` set.

### Video export

- The export dialog has an **Advanced** section. Its **Video** choice adds
  **Full match with game HUD**: the whole recording, at normal speed, through
  the eyes of the player chosen under **Follow player**, with the game's own
  HUD in every frame (vitals, weapons, timer, kill feed, chat, crosshair,
  post-game scoreboard) and stereo game audio. Recordings of 10 seconds to
  20 minutes qualify; the MP4 is 1080p60 H.264 at 12 Mbit/s. Before, export
  made highlight reels only, without HUD.
- The HUD reaches the video as the page's own DOM, drawn through an SVG image
  at 1920×1080 with the UI scale of that size. Its fonts are cut down in memory
  to the faces and characters the HUD shows (the whole Rajdhani family is
  1.8 MB, mostly Devanagari), which took the HUD's cost from about 100 ms to
  about 25 ms a frame.
- HUD timers and fades (the update loop, frag and pickup messages, chat fade,
  damage arrows, death countdown, hints) run on presentation time, which an
  export owns and steps frame by frame; CSS animations are paused and stepped
  the same way. In live play presentation time is the wall clock, so nothing
  changes there.
- A full match's audio is mixed after its picture, in 30-second windows at one
  level for the whole mix, and added to the finished file without re-encoding
  the video. Highlight reels mix in one piece as before.
