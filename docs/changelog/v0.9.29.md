# v0.9.29

**Release Date:** 2026-10-14

## Summary

Demos and spectating show the player's own screen in v0.9.29, games against
bots count, and the release gets its papercuts fixed and its manuals checked.

- **Demos and spectating.** A watched player, live or in a demo, is seen through
  the same first-person camera and HUD as the game: their strafe lean, slide
  dip, ledge lift and crouch come from data now recorded in every snapshot
  (demo format v5; older demos still play). Bodies fall as ragdolls, slow motion
  is smooth, and highlight reels have transitions, fly-by shots, a movie mode and
  an exit.
- **Bots.** Private games against bots are rated: each bot preset is an account
  on the Private board. An invited bot matches the inviter's skill, and bots are
  named for the noodle shop.
- **Chat.** Team chat (Y, or `say_team`) is filtered on the server, `/mute`
  ignores a player for the match, and IME composition no longer sends or closes
  chat.
- **World and sound.** Warfork world fog and sky portals, map ambience, sounds
  that fade with distance, an announcer for every match and a mix for the
  effects.
- **Release papercuts.** Loading recovers from browser and lobby failures, the
  first visit downloads package metadata instead of every archive, packages can
  come from an HTTP mirror, and settings import counts what it applied.
- **Documentation.** The game manual is refreshed with its own theme and a
  play-in-browser notice; the engine manual was checked page by page against
  the code; `AGENTS.md` matches the code, and the project's procedures are
  skills in `.claude/skills/`.

Still open, carried to v0.9.30:

- Duel bots time the Red Armor less well than the Mega Health and the other
  armors: it sits on the map a median of about 35 seconds after respawning.
- A cap on concurrent matches in the lobby (R11 in the deployment notes). The
  player cap bounds matches only through the players in them.

Plan and status: `docs/research/v0.9.29-demo-player.md`,
`docs/research/v0.9.29-bot-ratings.md`, `docs/research/v0.9.29-release-audit.md`
and the `v0.9.29-papercuts*.md` notes.

## Changes

### Team chat and match-local mute

Press Y to talk to your team, or use `say_team` in the console. Team messages
are filtered on the server before transmission, so opponents and spectators
cannot receive them. Global chat remains on T/Enter. Type `/mute <playername>`
and `/unmute <playername>` in chat for a local, case-insensitive ignore that clears
when the match ends and the next warmup begins. System notices remain visible.
CTF was removed from the game's roadmap.

### Game manual refreshed

The FPS manual now explains separate public/private skill ladders, rated bot
practice, profile history and the shared demo controls. Setup instructions and
broken section links were corrected. Its VitePress theme follows the game's
gold rail, ink panels, violet accents and angular keys, with bundled fonts and
dark/light reading modes. Use `docs:fps:dev`, `docs:fps:build` and
`docs:fps:preview` for the game manual; the existing docs scripts serve the engine.
The published manual presents only the four release modes; unreleased mode notes
are excluded from navigation, search and generated pages.
ASCII diagrams and file trees were replaced with themed Mermaid diagrams and
readable tables. Architecture, match handoff and bot training now have diagrams;
HUD positions and project files no longer depend on fixed-width text layouts.

### Engine manual checked against the code

The engine manual's build failed on a dead link; it builds again, and links to
files outside the manual now point to GitHub, where they resolve. Every page
was checked against the source and corrected where it had drifted:

- The introduction and feature pages said SHAG has no map editor; they now
  describe SHAGGED.
- The ranking pages still called bot calibration pending; they now describe bots
  rated as preset accounts, and the lobby guide's results section is reordered
  from report to recovery.
- The networking guide drew Ping and Pong the wrong way round, listed the
  client's `Ack` as a server message, showed a `V2NetworkClient` API that does
  not exist and claimed client-side knockback prediction.
- The network server page called its certificates WebTransport; they serve
  `wss://`. The server's `--help` says so too.
- Ground speed is `sv_movementSpeed` (280) and sprint `sv_runSpeed` (400), not
  `cl_speed`. The SHAGGED map rules quote the real player box (32 × 32 × 56) and
  speeds.
- The water section of the shader guide is a reference with one table of
  settings instead of a log of changes.
- The camera guide covers the shared first-person view behind watched screens.
- `.env.example` lists `VITE_PK3_MIRROR_URL`, `SHAG_LOBBY_MAX_PLAYERS` and
  `SHAG_MATCH_RESULT_OUTBOX`; `daynight` lists all its subcommands in its usage.

Sentences about removed commands and old APIs are gone throughout.

### Chat composition, scrollback and config transfer

Confirming text with an IME no longer sends chat prematurely. Open chat keeps the
latest 100 messages and does not pull readers to the bottom when new messages
arrive. Settings import reports rejected lines instead of counting them as
successes; exported string values preserve quotes, backslashes and line breaks.

### Loading recovery, settings and network diagnostics

Asset requests now have header/body deadlines so a stalled mirror or optional
texture request can release its fallback. Structurally corrupt ZIPs retry from
origin, and final errors retain the actual cause. Malformed legacy movement
preferences fall back safely. Greyed-out settings now disable native keyboard
controls as well as pointer input, and repeating HUD warnings honor reduced motion.
Sliders still apply immediately, but combine preference saves until release or a
brief pause and refresh the settings view at most once per animation frame.

Netgraph resets between connections, refines its snapshot-cadence estimate, and
draws at 10 Hz without resizing its canvas each frame. Server WebSocket output
is bounded at approximately 8 MiB per connection; a saturated reader disconnects
through the existing recovery flow without stopping healthy broadcasts. Settings
and HUD manuals, plus the headless replay example, now match the implemented UI.

### FPS and movement speed visible by default

The FPS counter is now a compact gold plate attached to the top-right corner,
using the game's HUD font. Both FPS and movement speed start enabled and have
Off/On controls in Settings → Gameplay → View & info. `show_fps` now saves its
preference; existing saved choices are respected. The readout updates four times
per second and skips unchanged text writes. Resetting movement preferences now
uses their declared defaults rather than treating a saved value as the default.

### FPS HUD styling belongs to the game

The Washiki HUD stylesheet now lives in `src/games/fps/client/hud/styles/`, beside
the FPS HUD widgets. `FPSHud` loads it directly from the game; the styles and
injection order are preserved. Generic engine HUD styling remains separate.

### Browser controls and optional preferences

Switching away, hiding the tab or releasing mouse capture now clears held
controls, so returning and clicking back into the game does not resume a stale
movement or fire input. Mouse releases outside the canvas and cancelled touches
also clear correctly. Bindings remain intact across input cleanup and reinit.
Blocked browser storage now falls back to the default theme at startup; theme
changes still update the UI for this session when saving the preference fails.
Malformed saved cvars and controls now fall back to usable defaults while keeping
valid preferences. Settings reports "Session only · could not save" when browser
storage rejects a write. Denied mouse capture and fullscreen requests show a
retry hint instead of failing silently.
Details: [quality audit](../research/v0.9.29-quality-audit.md).

### More visible warmup prompt

The ready-count banner, prompt and F4 keycap are larger and stay on one line.
While you have not readied up, the text inside the gold count cell gently grows
and shrinks while its box stays still. It settles once you are ready, and
reduced-motion preferences disable the pulse.

### Pickup hover clearance

Procedural health, armor, ammo and powerup visuals float higher so their downward
bob clears the floor at server-landed item positions. The bob is 20% faster;
pickup triggers and collection ranges stay at their authoritative positions.

### Recoverable demo playback

Recording downloads now offer Cancel, recover from stalled headers or body
transfers, and show Try again after a failure. Other playback buttons stay
disabled during a transfer; cancelling, leaving Demos or starting a live game
prevents the old request from starting playback later. Files are validated before
leaving a live session. If map or playback startup fails, the menu returns with
the error and Retry. Reopening a menu also cancels its pending close animation,
so recovery cannot be hidden by an older animation.

### Demos show the game's HUD

Watching a demo now looks like playing it. The game's own HUD is on screen —
health and plates, weapon slots and ammo, the timer and frags, the kill feed,
damage indicators and the crosshair — where playback showed one line of text;
a HUD switch on the playback bar hides it. Demo playback runs on the game's
own event handling with the followed player as the one watched, so every
sound, effect and message the game has reaches demos too, instead of the six
kinds of event a separate copy handled. Looking up and down is interpolated
like turning, where it stepped at the recording rate.

The playback bar wears the HUD's look — the gold play key, ink chips, the
followed player in gold — and fades away while a demo plays untouched, so the
screen is the player's own; moving the mouse, pausing or pointing at it brings
it back.

Found on the way: darkening decals and glow-lit surfaces failed to compile
their shaders on maps that have them, such as wdm17, since the world fog
reached them, and did not draw.

### A watched screen leans, dips and crouches as the player's did

Watching someone, live or in a demo, the view stayed level while they
strafed, did not dip into a slide or lift through a ledge grab, snapped by a
fixed amount when they crouched, and climbed steps in jerks: snapshots carried
none of what their own camera moved by. Each snapshot now carries a player's
keys, their slide and ledge grab phases and their exact crouch eye height, and
the watched view runs the same lean, dip, lift and step easing as the player's
own camera. New demos are format v5; older ones still play, without these.

### Bodies fall in demos

A player killed by anything short of a gibbing hit falls as a ragdoll in the
game; in a demo the same death burst into gibs, because playback had no
ragdoll system. Demos now drop the body as the game does, on the demo's
clock: paused, it lies still; in slow motion it falls slowly; a seek clears
it. After a few seconds it bursts, as in the game.

### The first-person view no longer tilts in the air

Watching a demo, the view could lean sideways while the player was in the air,
up to 29°. Babylon rolls a camera through its up vector and rebuilt that only
when the roll changed: the walk bob's roll falls to zero at a jump, the up
vector kept that frame's pitch, and turning in the air tipped the view over.
The game's own camera rolls the same way. Every first-person camera now
rebuilds its up vector from its rotation each frame.

### Watching someone is their own screen

A demo, a chase camera on a live player and the view of a teammate while dead
in a round now build the view one way, the way the player's own camera does.
The walk bob, the dip on landing, the speed FOV and the idle breathing come
from the same effects the local camera runs, where demos rebuilt only the bob
and live spectating took that rebuild. Live spectating also follows the player
as they are drawn — the interpolated sample — instead of smoothing the latest
snapshot on its own, two different ways, which lagged the view behind the
world. What a watched view still lacks: the strafe lean and the slide dip,
because snapshots carry no input and no slide phase.

### Highlights and demos you can leave, and slow motion that moves

Neither the demo player nor the highlights reel had a way out: the menu hid
when playback started and nothing brought it back. A gold ✕ in the top-left
corner, mirroring the FPS counter, and Esc now leave playback and return to
the Demos page.

Highlights' slow motion froze and jumped. A shot watching the player who gets
fragged held its last picture through the slowed part once they died; it now
cuts to the killer at the frag. The fly-by camera moved once per recorded
frame instead of every screen frame, and speed changes snapped; the camera now
follows the smooth playhead and slow motion eases in and out.

Highlights use the demo player's controls in the HUD's look: a tick on the
timeline per shot, previous and next shot, and a card naming each shot. A reel
opens in movie mode — the game HUD off, the controls down to a strip along the
bottom — and either player's controls minimize the same way. The HUD switch
shows the HUD only from a player's eyes; fly-by shots go without it.

The reel draws its transitions: a fade from and to black at its ends, a
white flash into each replay, and a dip through black between two players'
shots. They were planned for every shot and never drawn. Fly-by shots look at
the killer, and at the victim while the two fit one frame, curve smoothly
through their path, and pull in front of a wall rather than film it. The
victim's eyes are shown when the victim saw it coming, never twice in a row,
where a tick number used to decide.

Playing a second demo after leaving the first hung at "Warming up effect
shaders": a texture the first map's unload had disposed was handed to the next
map, and its materials could never finish compiling. Fixed, and the warm-up no
longer waits forever on a material that never compiles.

### Bot games are rated

A private match against bots was recorded and never rated: a bot game waited
for a reviewed calibration of that bot configuration on that map, and none was
ever installed, so every bot game sat at "pending" and a player who only
played bots never appeared on any board. Each bot preset is now an account —
Bot · Easy, Bot · Medium, Bot · Hard, Bot · Insane — rated in the Private
pools like a player, on the Private board with its own record. A second bot of
the same preset in one match plays at that account's rating without changing
it. Custom-difficulty bots, and bots changed mid-match, stay unrated with
their records kept. The lobby rates the bot games it already holds when it
first opens its store on this version. The calibration commands
(`ranking:pilot`, `ranking:references`) are gone. On the board a bot's row carries a
cyan BOT tag, and the "matches await bot calibration" notes are gone from the
board and Profile.

**Bots match your skill.** The room's **+ Add bot** always fielded a Medium
bot. It now fields the level whose rating in that mode is nearest yours, so
bots get harder as you get better — Medium until you have a rating — and each
bot's seat shows its level.

### Bots are named for the noodle shop

Bots played as Wraith, Phantom, Specter and the rest of an arena-shooter
roster; the game server's own bots had a second list of Quake names. Both
now play from one list — Ramen, Washiki!, Noodlesoup, Tonkotsu, Gyoza,
Slurp-san, Broth Lord and more — and the lobby reserves every one of them.
Udon and Soba stay team names only. When the list runs out a bot takes a name
again as `Ramen_2`, which the lobby reserves too; the game server's bots used
`Ramen2`, which it did not.

### New demos play without restarting the dev server

A demo recorded while the dev server ran, or a pk3 added to `public/data`,
was answered with the app's own page, and the demo player reported "bad magic
bytes": the watcher ignores `public/data`, so Vite knew only the files there
at startup. The dev server now serves `public/data` from disk as it is.

### Player model media in one PK3

Player bodies and animation clips now load from
`data/player_model_vanguard.pk3`, through the same package loader and HTTP
mirror as map media, instead of the old `games/default/animations/` URLs.
The current 27 GLBs form a 4.06 MiB archive. Live players, teleport ghosts,
demos, animation inspection and benchmark inputs use it; missing optional
victory dances no longer trigger failed loose-file requests. Original FBX
files and export intermediates remain available outside served public files,
and `npm run tools:player-model` rebuilds the package from those sources.
Details: [media investigation](../research/v0.9.29-media.md#vanguard-package-implementation).

### pk3 packages can come from an HTTP mirror

The pk3 packages, maps and textures, are most of what a player downloads, and
until now all of it came from the server that serves the game. Built with
`VITE_PK3_MIRROR_URL`, the client downloads each pk3 from that mirror instead,
as long as the mirror's copy matches the game server's size. A missing or
stale file on the mirror, or a mirror that is down, falls back to the game
server, so a mirror that lags behind costs only the files it lacks. One
mirror is configured; it can redirect to others. Setup:
[PK3 filesystem](../manual/guides/pk3-filesystem.md#http-mirror).

### Recovering from browser and lobby failures

The lobby reconnects automatically after an outage and offers Try now. It tries
to restore the same account's room, including private invites, while full-server
refusals keep their longer retry wait. Connection cancellation and early socket
closure now finish their pending attempts. Accepted player keys remain available
for reconnect and export when browser storage fails; Profile and Welcome warn
players to save those keys before reloading.

Startup failures show a visible error, Reload and a credential-free diagnostic
instead of leaving the loading screen spinning. The demo archive also has a
timeout and Try again, and ignores obsolete replies. Escape is reserved for
menus, blocked session storage cannot strand the match menu or post-match return,
account restore explains full servers correctly, and Profile hides a revealed
key when reopened or the account changes. Incoming lobby requests are limited
to 64 KiB. Details and validation: [papercuts](../research/v0.9.29-papercuts.md).

### The first visit loads package metadata instead of every archive

Builds now publish a searchable PK3 index with the manifest. A browser can find
files, shaders and maps by fetching those two small JSON files, then download
the packages needed for play. On the current 107-package set, indexing transfers
344 KiB instead of roughly 511 MiB before compression. Missing or stale metadata
keeps the previous scan path, and mirrors continue serving package downloads.
The generator runs before `npm run build` and through `npm run tools:manifest`
or `npm run tools:pk3-index`. It preserves package override order and refuses
partial publication if an archive fails or changes. Release package curation
remains separate. Details: [release audit](../research/v0.9.29-release-audit.md).

### Smaller fixes before the release

- **Plainer messages.** A room that cannot start says "Couldn't start the match
  server. Try again in a moment." instead of the spawner's internal error, and
  room errors say "room" as the rest of the menu does ("That room is full."
  rather than "Lobby is full.").
- **The room list counts plainly.** Its heading is "Rooms" and the counter reads
  "1 of 3 open", or "none", instead of "0 open · 0".
- **No scoreboard over the loading screen.** Holding Tab while a match loads no
  longer draws the scoreboard on top of it.
- **Map names.** wdm11 and wdm12 show as "Warehouse C" and "Courtyard" in the
  map vote instead of "WDM11" and "WDM12"; `config/maps.json` no longer lists
  the retired `tdm` mode.
- **Fixed keys stay readable.** In Settings › Controls, a long action name such
  as Scoreboard puts its Fixed badge on a second line instead of over its key.
- **Link previews.** The page has a description and title tags, so a shared
  room link previews as Washiki Washoi! rather than "SHAG Engine".
- **A quieter console.** Each map reports the entity types the client does not
  handle in one line instead of one warning per entity, and leaves out markers
  that need no handler; a missing `engine-settings.json` and the menu before
  any match are no longer reported as warnings.
- **Account switching no longer races the reconnect.** Pasting a key to switch
  accounts closes the connection on purpose, and that no longer starts the
  outage retry, which could cut off the switch on a slow connection.

### Maps hum, spin and have their skies

- **Ambient speakers play.** A map's looping `target_speaker` sounds now play
  where they stand and fade out with distance as in Quake, and global or
  periodic speakers play too. Before, no map ambience played at all.
- **Rotating brushes exist.** `func_rotating` objects, such as the ceiling fans
  in wdm17 and wda1, are built and spin. Before, they were missing from the map
  entirely, not just standing still.
- **Cloud skies.** A sky shader's stages draw as Quake 3 cloud layers, tiled,
  scrolling, tinted and blended as the shader says, over the far box or as the
  whole sky. wdm17's violet night sky, which has no far box, failed to load
  before. A stage that names `foo.tga` also finds `foo.ktx`.
- **`rgbGen const` reads correctly.** The shader parser read `rgbGen const r g b`
  one value off and turned zeros into ones, so `( 0 0 0 )` came out white and
  violetsky's dim grey came out blue. `alphaGen const 0` stayed 1 the same way.
  Shaders that use constant colours may now look as their authors intended.
- Turning sound effects off silences ambient loops too, and turning them back on
  restores them.

### World fog and sky portals from Warfork maps

New Warfork maps set atmospheric fog in their worldspawn, and SHAG now draws it
as Warfork does (commit 815e344): `fog "fog_dist r g b [mindist]"` fogs the map,
about 95% opaque at `fog_dist` and clear inside `mindist`, with the optional
`fog_height` band (ground mist or a cloud deck), `fog_sun` glow and `fog_sky`
horizon tint. The sky is fogged at its shader's height, so a low sky can stay
clear inside the fog's start. Additive glows fade with distance instead of
turning fog-coloured, and a shader with `nofog` keeps it off its surfaces.
`r_fog 0` turns it off; in developer mode the `r_fog_*` cvars try fog on any
map, and the `fog` command shows what is drawn. WebGL and WebGPU alike.

Maps with a `misc_skyportal` (wdm1, acidwdm2) now show their sky area through
the sky, seen from the portal as the player turns, instead of a plain skybox.

Also fixed on the way: `skyparmssides` skies lost their height (Warfork reads
six sides, then the height), a sky without a height now stands at 2048 as in
Warfork, and fog volumes drew linear fog where they meant EXP2.

### Effects in the fog

World fog now reaches the effects too: muzzle flashes, impact sparks, tracers,
rail and voltaic beams fade with distance into the fog as Warfork's additive
effects do, explosion sparks fade with the fog where they go off, and lava,
slime, water, splash spray, glow-lit surfaces, darkening decals and debris
take the fog as ordinary surfaces. Before, they stayed bright and sharp
through any fog. A sky portal's rotation speeds now follow Quake's angles
(positive yaw turns left), checked by test.

### Sky portals no longer halve the frame rate

On a map with a sky portal (wdm1, acidwdm2) the frame rate fell from 165 to 23
fps when looking at the sky. Each portal pass rebuilt the whole post-processing
pipeline, because it followed the portal's camera as if it were the view, and
it rebuilt every material's light setup, because its mesh list was refilled
through Babylon's observed render list. Both are gone, and the portal now draws
only the entities its own PVS sees, as Warfork does. The portal costs about
1 ms of CPU a frame on acidwdm2 while the sky is in view, WebGL and WebGPU
alike, and nothing at all otherwise: it is drawn only when a sky face is in the
view's PVS and frustum.

### Water behind walls costs nothing

Water's depth and colour passes, each a further render of the scene, ran
whenever a pool was in the frustum and the PVS, even with walls between it and
the player. On acidwdm2, looking away from its pool with the pool behind walls,
they took a third of the frame rate. Each liquid surface now asks an occlusion
query whether it is hidden; a hidden surface is not drawn, and its passes stop.
There, the frame rate rises from 119 to 194 fps on WebGL and from 132 to 195 on
WebGPU; looking at the pool costs the same as before. A view inside a
surface's bounds skips the query: wdm17's slime pools are one mesh spanning
most of the map, and from inside its bounds the query found the slime hidden
for good, so it vanished.

Where the water is in view the passes still run, but they no longer render the
whole map: seen from above a flat surface, the water shows only what lies below
it, so meshes wholly above the water are left out of both. At the yellow armor
on acidwdm2, beside the pool, the frame rate rises by 15 to 30% in most
directions, and the water looks the same. A view under the surface, or a
waterfall, still captures everything.

High water no longer draws the scene twice: it reads depth from its colour
capture's own depth buffer instead of a separate depth pass, 5 to 18% faster
where the water is in view, with the same depth bands. Medium keeps its depth
pass. The Balanced video preset, the game's defaults, now uses Medium water
(depth tint and splash crowns); Pretty keeps High, which adds the see-through
refraction.

### Sounds fade with distance as in Warsow

An enemy's shot sounded like your own: positional sounds faded linearly over
2400 units, so a shot 500 units away still played at four fifths of full
volume, a touch louder than your own gun. They now fade on Warsow's inverse
curve, full volume within 125 units and half at 250, with its attenuation
classes: weapon fire is Normal (plasma Idle), rocket and grenade blasts and
the quad spawn carry (Distant), plasma impacts Idle, movers Static, and a map
speaker uses its own `attenuation`. Your gun and everyone else's play at the
same level, so distance alone sets them apart. The listener follows the view
every frame; it used to move only when a sound played, so after a respawn or
in spectating the next sounds were placed from where the old camera stood.
Details: [sound research](../research/v0.9.29-sound.md).

The hit sound now says how hard you hit, `hit_0` for a light hit up to
`hit_3` from 75 damage; it used to cycle through the four whatever the damage.
A player's pain says how hurt they are, as in Warsow: the server sends the
victim's pain level with each hit (20, 35 and 60 health its steps), where the
pain sound used to follow the damage of the hit.
A player's pain is heard at most every 400 ms, as in Warsow, so a burst of
machinegun hits no longer stacks pain sounds on one victim.

### The announcer calls every match

Duel, FFA and Amphi spoke only through Search and Destroy's round code, which
ran in every mode: their countdown opened with the bomb defenders' "defend
the objectives" line and repeated each of 3, 2, 1 on every frame. Every mode
now has the match called properly, as Warsow's: "get ready to fight", 3, 2, 1
once each, "fight", "overtime" at each
extension and "game over" at the end. A player not ready in warmup hears
"please ready up" once a minute. While the match is live the announcer calls
the lead: taken, tied and lost, for you, or for your team in a team mode.
Duel, FFA and team deathmatch now count down 5 seconds, as Amphi and Warsow,
where they counted 3.
All announcer lines share one voice and wait
for each other instead of talking over one another; Search and Destroy keeps
its per-side round lines on the same voice.

### A mix for the effects

Settings → Audio has a Mix card: Effects, Players, Hit sound and Announcer,
each a share of the effects volume (Warsow's `cg_volume_effects`,
`cg_volume_players`, `cg_volume_hitsound`, `cg_volume_announcer`, all 100% by
default). Turning the effects down used to turn the hit sound and the
announcer down with them.

A player's pain and death share one slot, and their jumps another, so a new
sound cuts the last instead of playing over it. When 48 sounds are already
playing, the one nearest its end is cut, not the oldest: a long explosion
tail is no longer cut short by a footstep.
