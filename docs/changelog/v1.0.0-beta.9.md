# v1.0.0-beta.9

**Release Date:** Nov 10, 2026

## Changes

- Player model tuning: select Police as the test default, reuse the shared animation clips with load-time retargeting, normalize held-weapon attachment frames, and use the player manager for `animtest`. The compressed body uses a locally served Draco decoder.
- Gameplay settings pair per-side Police/Male, Female/Female and Robot/Bobot model and voice choices, with lightweight rendered previews. Changes apply to existing players, including demo views and local water reflections. Death rendering preserves rig export units through a dedicated bone rotation frame.

- Robot uses the shared animation set, with a rendered settings preview. Thin outlines remain the default (enabled, size 25), with coverage for Gameplay reset.

- Smooth split normals for player outlines while retaining authored body lighting, shared geometry and snapshot replay. Normalize the new bodies to the standing player height with feet on the ground, preserve held-weapon size, and prevent ragdolls growing when physics takes over.

- Keep cached outline templates outside player source hierarchies, preventing subsequent spawns from inheriting an extra mesh that covers the textured body with the outline colour.

- Include outline shader readiness in snapshot draw tracking so cold compilations cannot leave new player outlines missing during replay.

- Add Cartel with male sounds and RiotKitty with female sounds; label the existing female model Female Police. Model menus show model names alone.
- Redesign Who's who as side-by-side team cards with live model previews and colour controls below. One lazy render surface supplies both previews and pauses when hidden.
- Package the shared animation clips separately in `washoi_player_animations.pk3`, preserving the owner-maintained body archive when rebuilding clips.

- Complete RiotKitty's fallback preview, refresh its package discovery, reuse preview bodies across model changes, and allow failed preview loads to retry.

- Use each model's natural full-body Running clip when running without aiming or recent fire. Weapon-ready poses bend the upper spine with view pitch, carrying the attached hand weapon and muzzle while keeping the lower body upright.
- Add untinted Off to Brighter players; retain Tint, Some and Lots. Off preserves authored body colours and the coloured outline. Player style uniforms refresh during WebGPU snapshot replay.
- Clear the local Voltaic beam during frozen round phases, disabled controls, weapon switches and non-beam/missing weapon states, including cached viewmodel retirement.

- Increase the five calibrated player bodies by 10% for readability around their fixed hull, keeping feet grounded and held-weapon size stable.

- Keep hips and legs on their movement animation during third-person blade swings, including jumps. Restore upper-body locomotion without restarting the stride; preserve full-body one-shot and demo clock behaviour.

- Fix water floating in mid-air under WebGPU snapshots: a liquid surface PVS hid before a recording now gets the current camera in replayed frames, instead of drawing where the recording frame saw it.

- Tune running separately for legs and upper body: `cl_player_run_legs` (0.75) and `cl_player_run_upper` (0.5) set each half as a share of the speed-matched rate.
- Walking or running backwards plays the new backwards run clip instead of the forward run reversed. Shared animation clips move to `models/players/animations/` in `washoi_player_animations.pk3`.
- Rename the player asset settings after what they hold: `config/player-animations.json` lists the shared clips and their archive, `config/player-model.json` the default body and its archive. The crowd benchmark measures the Police body (recipe `player-crowd-v2`); the retired vanguard body is no longer needed, and `tools:player-model`, which rebuilt the body archive from it, is removed. Headless `anim bones` reads the Draco-compressed body, and `anim inspect` names each clip's joints.

- Play a death animation once before the ragdoll on some deaths: a kill from behind plays `death_from_back`, a headshot from the front or side `death_headshot`, and a riotgun blast or an explosion that does not gib `death_flying_back`. The body turns to the shot and flies the same arc as before; the ragdoll takes over from the animated pose the moment it touches a surface or the clip ends. Death events from hitscan and melee kills now name the killing weapon and where the blow landed (event code 26), which a riotgun kill needs: it arrived as `bullet`, like the machinegun. Older demos keep code 11 and play their deaths from behind and their explosions with the new clips, and every other death as a plain ragdoll.

- Rest third-person guns on the supporting hand: each body records, in its standing idle, where its other palm is and which way its front faces; each gun turns so its bore line rests a fixed height above that palm, then swings around its rear end to point along the front. The gun used to follow the gripping hand's fingers alone and passed above and beside the other hand, most visibly when looking up or down.
- Hold the blade in its own upper-body stance (`blade_idle`) while the legs keep their movement clip, instead of the two-handed rifle pose, with the blade standing up from the fist, edge forward and held just below its guard. A swing plays over the stance and hands back to it; swimming, climbing, walljump and slide keep their own arms.

- Give ragdolls the map's collision again: since plain map surfaces were merged into texture-array meshes, the collision built for each surface was disposed with the surface, and dead bodies fell through floors and walls out of the map. The ragdoll system now builds the world's collision from its final meshes once the map is loaded, and again on a map change.
- Stop death bodies passing through floors and walls when the ragdoll takes over from a death animation. The thrown body flies as a sphere as wide as its reach, so it meets a surface before any limb can pass into it, and the ragdoll does not inherit the speed into that surface.

- Draw the armor shard, green, yellow and red armor and the megahealth from packaged models (`washoi_models.pk3`) instead of their generated sculptures, which are kept in the recipe list, commented out. The models float, bob and turn like classic Quake items: all turn together once every 4.096 seconds and each bobs on its own phase, lifted clear of the floor. The megahealth floats 10 units higher than the armors, and its blue core glows: an emissive mask built from the strongly blue parts of its texture, breathing slowly, while small white sparkles rise from it, turning with it, and fade.
- Draw the +5, +25 and +50 health and every ammo type from packaged models too: one health bubble and one ammo box (`washoi_models.pk3`). Their generated sculptures and crates are kept as unregistered recipes. The bubble is sized by tier (+50 a tenth larger than the +25, +5 a tenth smaller) and coloured by tier: each tier draws a copy of the texture turned to its hue (+50 red-orange, +25 yellow-orange, +5 the model's own blue), with an emissive copy that lights its blue trim. The cross, found as the model's innermost part, is fullbright: its texels carry only glow, so it shows its full colour in any light. The ammo box carries its weapon's symbol on all four sides, the flat Warsow simple-item icons, packaged as WebP in `washoi_gfx.pk3`; the crates' busier HUD ammo icons are no longer stamped on pickups.
- Sparkles rise from the megahealth, the green, yellow and red armor, the +25 and +50 health and the ammo boxes, more on the bigger prizes. All of them are thin instances of one dot drawn in a single call, and only the eight nearest drawn items within 900 units of the camera sparkle; before, each megahealth sparkle was a mesh and a draw call of its own.

- In free-for-all the server hands each player a body and a colour of their own: the least-worn colour, so up to eight players never share one, and the least-worn of the five bodies. Every viewer and the match's demo show a player the same way; before, each client picked colours at random for itself and every opponent wore the viewer's enemy model. Your ally and enemy model choices still apply in Duel and the team modes, and outline and brightness settings everywhere. The look rides as one optional byte in each player's snapshot record, so demo format is now v7; older demos play with the colours picked locally as before.
- **Gun size** in Gameplay → View & info (`cl_gunsize`): Normal, Small (a tenth smaller) or Smallest (a fifth smaller). The gun shrinks into its corner of the screen and returns to full size while aiming down sights. `cl_gun 0` hides your own first-person gun entirely; it has no menu entry. `cg_weaponfov` needs no `developer 1`.

- **Rejoin match** works for a seat you already played. Its recovery ticket is valid from the start of the match, the lobby hands it back as often as you ask, and the game server uses it to take the seat back: a connection still open on the seat is dropped, a held seat is resumed with its entity and score, and a seat released after its hold comes back under its old player id with its score and deaths, as one result seat (that match stays unrated, as any interrupted play). Before, the button only claimed a seat nobody had taken, and not in the first two minutes: a player who dropped after playing and reloaded could not get back in. A dropped seat is now held for 60 seconds instead of 20, so a reload makes it back before a Duel or Amphi is forfeited.

- Use the new app icon (`public/appicon.png`, 1024 px) as the game's logo everywhere it is installed or shown: the favicons and `favicon.ico`, the Apple touch icon, the web manifest's Android icons, the desktop installer icons in `build-resources/` (`icon.ico` and the Linux PNG sizes) and the Android launcher icons, whose adaptive icon now sits on the logo's own dark background instead of white. The web manifest names the game, Washiki Washoi!, instead of SHAG Engine.

- Spectator freefly follows `cl_fov`, and a change shows at once. It used a fixed field of view of its own (about 89° at 16:9), so changing `cl_fov` while flying around, for map shots for example, did nothing.

- **Timeouts** in Duel, Amphi and Search & Destroy. Each player may call one a match with `/timeout` in chat (or `timeout` in the console); it pauses the match for up to two minutes, and anyone playing ends it with `/timein`, after a three-second count. A paused server runs no ticks at all, so players, projectiles, item timers and the match clock stand still and carry on exactly as they were, a fall included; chat goes on, carried by a new message while no snapshot is sent. The client stops predicting and holds remote players at the newest snapshot instead of extrapolating past it. In Duel and Amphi a player whose connection drops in a live match pauses it for them, which costs nobody their timeout; their seat is held through it, and if it runs out with them still away they forfeit. The announcer calls the timeout, "time in" and the fight, and the screen shows who called it and the time left. New messages: `Pause` (0x0D), `PausedChat` (0x0E) and `CallPause` (0x8D); the demo format is unchanged and a recording leaves the pause out.

- Draw the ultra health as the packaged megahealth with its blue core turned purple, glowing and breathing like the megahealth's and with as many sparkles, instead of its generated sculpture, which is kept as an unregistered recipe. It floats as high as the megahealth.

- Draw `func_static` brush models. Nothing built them, so scenery a mapper keeps out of the world model never appeared: washoi_dm1's tower standing in the sky was missing while its spotlights' halos floated in the air.

- Draw the Quad Damage from its packaged model (`quad.glb` in `washoi_models.pk3`): a spiked ring around a Q, a tenth larger than the megahealth and floating as high, with the megahealth's breathing blue glow and as many sparkles. The energy disc behind the Q is lifted out as its own mesh, glowing in its own colours and turning in the badge's plane once every three seconds. Its generated badge is kept as an unregistered recipe; no item draws a generated sculpture any more.

- Honour the maps' gametype keys on items: `gametype` (the modes an item is in), `notduel`, `notteam` and `notfree`. An item left out of the mode keeps its pickup id but is never active, so it is neither drawn nor picked up. Before, every item spawned in every mode: on washoi_dm1 a Duel had both the megahealth meant for duels and the ultra health meant for every other mode on the same spot. Amphi reads the duel keys; S&D answers to `bomb` and `notteam`.

- A player always sounds like the body they are drawn with. In free-for-all you, too, wear the body the server gave you: your water reflection and the demo following you show it, and your own pain, death, jump and drowning cries use its voice. Before, your own sounds and reflection used your ally model setting while everyone else saw and heard the assigned body, so a player handed the female body heard themselves in the male voice. Your own colour is still yours.

- Enemies start White and untinted, and their model is **Random**: each game you join draws one of the bodies your own side does not wear. Your side stays Pink, and **Brighter players** now starts Off, so bodies keep their own colours inside a team-coloured outline. Before, enemies were Cyan, every body was tinted, and enemies wore Police like your own side. The enemy colour no longer carries the "Everyone else in free-for-all" note; free-for-all opponents wear colours the server gives them.

- Bots learned routes on washoi_dm1 (26) and washoi_dm3 (31) from the owner's training demos: jumps, climbs and drops that replay reliably from a standstill. Those two maps had no learned routes before.
