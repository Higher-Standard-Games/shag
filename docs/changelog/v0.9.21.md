# v0.9.21

**Release Date:** 2026-08-06

## Summary

Focus areas this cycle:

1. **Post-Babylon-9 follow-ups** — the WebGL2 optimization tail from the v0.9.20 upgrade: selective static-BSP material freeze, and any browser tuning owed on area lights / god rays / glow / SSR.
2. **Audio V2 expansion** — pickups, teleport/jumppad/respawn, continuous weapon loops/stops, and announcer policy (building on the v0.9.20 soundscape checkpoint).
3. **Reflective water** — the follow-up teed up in `docs/research/BABYLON9_SSR.md`: support the Warsow reflective-shader key with a planar `MirrorTexture`, or a bespoke water material that replaces the Warsow water shaders.

In practice the cycle turned into a fourth thing that outgrew the other three:
**Search & Destroy**, taken from a design document to a mode that has been
played end-to-end against bots. Most of the second half of this log is either
S&D or the lobby, bot, spectator and HUD work it dragged in behind it — and
most of that half is a bug that only a played round could have found, which is
why it reads as a list of things that were wrong rather than a list of
features. Reflective water (3) has not been started.

Search & Destroy ships playable and unresolved in one specific way: **attacking
bots still hold at their spawn**, which is the first entry under Known
Unverified Items.

## Changes

- **Fixed dead jumppads & teleporters on ~12 maps** *(merged to main)* — the BSP
  trigger extractor only accepted `target_position` / `misc_teleporter_dest` / `info_notnull` as a
  trigger destination, so any `trigger_push` / `trigger_teleport` aimed at `target_location`
  (wdm12/17/18/19, wbomb6, wda1, wdm11, wtutorial1) or Quake 3's `info_teleport_destination`
  (wbomb2, wctf1, wda4) was silently dropped and did nothing. Surveyed all 38 shipping maps; the
  allow-list now covers every destination classname in use. (wbomb1's pads already resolved via
  `target_position` — verified, unchanged.)
- **Pickup sounds** *(merged to main)* — health / armor / ammo / weapon pickups
  now play the Warsow `sounds/items/*` bank for the local player (2D, distance-independent), driven
  by the existing `pickup_notify` event alongside the HUD toast. Armor tiers (shard/green/yellow/red)
  and mega health each get their own sound; also plays in demo playback for the followed player.
- **BSP collision fixes** *(merged to main)* — three fixes in
  `SweepCollisionBackend`:
  (1) **Baked `misc_model` decorations no longer collide.** Decorations (wdm2's floor lamps, and also
  the jumppad flames/rings and teleporter models that sat solid on top of the pads) are triangle-soup
  drawSurfs whose shader carries SOLID content. Skipped by shader prefix (`models/`, `mapobjects`).
  World triangle soup **still collides**: q3map2 emits triangle soup for vertex-lit world geometry
  too, and 336 walkable surfaces across 10 maps (wbomb1-6, wctf2, wdm1, wdm17, wtutorial1) are held
  up by nothing else — dropping all soup collision put holes straight through walkways, 30 of them
  bottomless. Measurements in `docs/research/bsp-triangle-soup-collision.md`.
  (2) **Detail brushes (`CONTENTS_DETAIL`) are solid again.** `shouldCollideWith`/`ContentFlags`
  treated detail as non-solid, but detail is a vis/portal flag — Q3 collides detail brushes. This had
  made SOLID|DETAIL geometry walk-through (wbomb1: 462, wdm12: 199 brushes — glass panels/railings).
  (3) **Face collision used a symmetric player hull.** `traceTriangleSwept` offset the box from the
  surface by `max(|mins|, |maxs|)` per axis, so on a floor it used the head half-height (40) instead
  of the foot half-height (24) — resting players and bots **exactly 16 units above every patch and
  triangle-soup surface**. Now uses the same asymmetric support point `traceBrush` always did, so
  curved/vertex-lit floors and brush floors agree. Measured across the pack: 65 spawn/item points
  settle 2–16u lower, none higher, none lose support.
- **Fixed remote players/bots rendering ~97 units off their real position** *(merged to main)* —
  bodies appeared to warp around the map as they turned, and to hover off the floor. The simulation
  was never wrong (a headless yaw sweep with zero movement input shows
  0.0000u drift); this was purely the render transform. The Mixamo clips carry heavy root motion on
  `mixamorig:Hips` — at `MODEL_SCALE` 36 that is 82u forward for run, 39–51u sideways for the strafes,
  246u for slide. `RemotePlayerRenderer` parents the model to an anchor it rotates to the player's
  yaw, so that displacement sits in the anchor's LOCAL space and the yaw **rotates** it: the body
  traced a circle around the spot it should have been standing on, and which direction it flew
  depended on which locomotion clip was playing. `PlayerModelManager.resetRootMotion()` was written to
  cancel exactly this, but walked `getChildTransformNodes(true)` — direct descendants only.
  vanguard.glb nests as `__root__ > RootNode > mixamorig:Hips`, so the walk only ever saw `RootNode`
  and **the Hips branch was dead code**. Now walks the full subtree (only `RootNode` and
  `mixamorig:Hips` match those names anywhere in the 70-node tree, so it stays targeted) and restores
  the Hips **bind pose** rather than only zeroing X/Z — which also kills the vertical bob that floated
  the body. The same direct-descendants-only traversal was fixed in the two clone-time cleanup passes
  that clear `.animations` / stop animatables. Headless repro: 97.5u offset swinging through a full
  turn before, 0.4u after. Regression tests in `src/engine/__tests__/PlayerModelRootMotion.test.ts`.
- **Armor pickups redesigned as Warsow-style armor** *(merged to main)* — the
  beveled hex badge read as a featureless white blob in play: it was fully emissive, so bloom ate both
  the tier colour and the geometry. It is now built the way segmented plate works — a solid
  elliptical torso core with five BANDS of small blocks laid around it and turned to face outward, so
  the silhouette reads round while every surface stays a flat facet. Shoulder caps are the outermost
  plates of the top bands, scaled up and canted, so they are part of the band and cannot drift off the
  body. The material is LIT and saturated with only a low emissive floor, so facets catch light
  instead of blowing out. Tier is carried by colour and size the way Warsow does it (green/yellow/red
  are one silhouette), so the 1–4 chevron tally is gone; the 5 AP shard is the same construction with
  the collar and skirt bands dropped. Took three passes to get here — a single tapered arc read as a
  bucket, then boxes bolted to a flat plate read as detached cubes, then bands with nothing behind
  them left see-through holes; the solid core is what makes it one object.
- **Health pickup polish** *(merged to main)* — the cross emblem was a dark mesh
  with glowing outlines, which collapsed into a black blob at distance. It is now solid
  accent-coloured with its up/down faces filled in the tier colour at full strength (50 HP orange,
  25 HP yellow) — a first attempt at 0.72 strength still read black, because you look DOWN at a floor
  item so those caps are most of what you see. The orbiting ring gained a banded colour→accent
  gradient that scrolls around it and is semi-transparent, so it layers over the shell instead of
  hiding it — and every health tier now has one (25/50 HP previously had no ring at all). Gradient is
  a `RawTexture`, not a `DynamicTexture`, so it builds under the headless NullEngine.
- **No black outlines on pickups** *(merged to main)* — the cel-shaded edge
  rendering added mid-cycle read as heavy ink over what should be solid objects. Removed from both
  armor and the health cross; the armor plates are lit so facets separate on shading alone, and the
  cross relies on body-vs-fill colour contrast instead.
- **Weapons land instead of appearing** *(merged to main)* — map-spawned weapons
  were placed flat on the floor by a downward raycast, so a whole map's worth read as identical props
  laid out in a row. They now take the same Havok toss a player-dropped weapon gets, dropping and
  tumbling to rest. The horizontal component is deliberately small: the pickup trigger is
  server-side at the mapper's entity origin, so the visual has to settle next to where it spawned
  rather than skating off its own trigger or over a ledge. Seeded off the pickup id, so a given spawn
  always lands the same way and respawns aren't a lottery.
- **Ragdolls fly along the knockback that killed you** *(merged to main)* — the
  cartoonish launch is intentional and unchanged in strength, but its DIRECTION was wrong. The client
  threw away the server's knockback vector: it renormalized the horizontal part to a flat 600 u/s,
  ignored Y entirely in favour of a fixed 300 u/s pop, and — when a kill had no horizontal component —
  picked a **random compass heading** at full speed. That last case is the most common death in the
  game (a rocket or grenade at your feet, where `knockbackDir` is the explosion→victim vector), so
  corpses regularly flew somewhere unrelated to the frag. Now the body launches along the full 3-D
  direction at a single speed (670 u/s, the magnitude of the old 600+300 launch), and a degenerate
  direction pops straight up instead of inventing a heading. Fixed the server half too: the kill event
  forwarded the RAW authored direction while the gameplay impulse used a normalized, upward-biased
  one, so corpse and push disagreed — both now use the same vector.
- **Weapon rates: plasmagun down, riotgun blast down** *(merged to main)* —
  plasmagun was the highest sustained DPS in the game at 12.5 shots/s × 15 = 187.5; retargeted to
  10 shots/s. 125Hz cannot express that exactly (12.5 ticks), so it lands on 13 ticks = 9.62 shots/s =
  **144 DPS**. Riotgun's blast went 100 → **90**, done by cutting pellets 20 → 18 rather than pellet
  damage, because damage is `Math.round`ed per hit and 4.5 rounds straight back to 5. Verified as
  already correct (no change): lasergun at 7 damage/cell and 20.83 shots/s = 145.8 DPS, which is as
  near the 140 target as a 125Hz tick allows.
- **Teleporter in-going effect** *(merged to main)* — entering a teleporter read as
  just vanishing (the entry ghost sits behind the camera post-teleport, and there was no teleport
  sound at all). Added the Warsow `sounds/world/tele_in.ogg` entry cue (2D for yourself, 3D at the
  entry for remote players) plus an inward "implosion" speed-line burst at the entry — the mirror of
  the outward exit burst — so a teleport now reads as being pulled in. Preloaded on map load.
- **Machinegun reload** *(merged to main)* — replaced the old near-free 40-round /
  0.13s "reload" (a cosmetic hitch) with a real magazine: **30-round clip + 120 reserve + 1.5s reload**.
- **Removed the gunblade weapon (full clean sweep)** *(merged to main)* — it was
  vestigial (fired once then dry, in no gametype loadout, spawned by no map; Blade is the proven
  default). Deleted the weapon def, the `GUNBLADE_BLAST` projectile def, and the `GUNBLADE_AMMO` def,
  plus every reference: table/registry entries, bot profile, `isDefaultWeapon` checks, HUD
  slot/colour/label entries, fire + impact sounds, damage-type case, the `defId==='gunblade'` /
  `case 'gunblade'` demo back-compat hooks, the `weapon_gunblade`/`ammo_gunblade` pickup mappings, and
  the `gunblade_blast` model preload. Blade's charged `blade_blast` is fully independent and simply
  reuses the gunblade blast **art files** (model/impact/sound media in the pk3) — those paths are
  unchanged (`TODO: custom blade art`). Old gunblade-era demos no longer render their blast
  projectiles (accepted). Tests updated (MG clip fixture, weapon/alias coverage lists).
- **Voltaic beam reload** *(merged to main)* — the continuous beam used a 60-cell
  clip that emptied in ~2.9s then stalled for a 1.3s reload (~31% downtime). Restructured to a
  120-cell clip + 60 reserve + 0.5s reload — same 180-cell total, but the beam now runs ~5.8s
  between brief reloads (effective DPS 100 → 134). See `docs/research/weapon-values-audit.md` for the
  full actual-timings/DPS table and the remaining flagged balance items (plasmagun DPS, machinegun
  clip, vestigial gunblade). Weapon timing/reload comments corrected to match the tick-quantized
  reality; documented that `reloadTime` is read only by the `clip` reload model.
- **Quad Damage implemented** *(merged to main)* — `item_quad` is placed on **25
  of the 38 shipping maps, exactly once per map**, and did nothing: no entity def, no pickup type, no
  effect. It is now a real item. 4× damage for 30s, 120s respawn, 15s first-spawn delay (same gate as
  armor and mega health), and it does **not** survive dying — killing the carrier takes the quad away.
  Re-taking it while held *extends* the timer rather than resetting it.
  - **Scope is Q3-classic, chosen deliberately**: the multiplier scales damage to others, damage to
    yourself, **and** knockback. A quad rocket jump throws you four times as far and usually kills you.
    The multiplier is applied in `CombatSystem.processDamage`, the single point every damage source
    funnels through (hitscan, projectile, splash, fall), so no weapon can miss it — and it stacks with
    the headshot multiplier.
  - **The carrier glows.** Red pulsing emissive + red outline at 1.5 Hz, driven per-frame from a new
    `PlayerModelManager.applyPowerupGlow()` that only writes emissive and outline (no material cloning
    per frame). Red is deliberately *not* the item's colour — it reads as danger, not as a team tint.
  - **The item is procedural**, like the health/armor/ammo pass earlier this cycle: a faceted
    double-pyramid gem inside four counter-rotating blades (four-fold, for "quad"), with the tallest
    beacon of any pickup. There is no quad model in the pk3 to load — only a HUD icon — so procedural
    was the only option, not a preference.
  - **Three sounds**, each with a different audience: `quad_spawn` 3D at the item and audible
    map-wide (the cue that starts the fight over it), `quad_pickup` 2D local, and `quad_fire` layered
    over your own weapon on every shot while it is up.
  - **HUD**: a gold countdown slab above the vitals block, flipping to pulsing red for the last 5s.
    Gold is sampled from Warsow's own `gfx/hud/icons/powerup/quad.tga` (rgb 224,192,0), so the world
    item and the HUD agree.
  - `item_warshell` (6 maps) and `item_regen` remain deliberately **unimplemented and untracked** —
    they stay invisible rather than spawning an item that does nothing when grabbed.

## Technical Notes

- `TriggerTable.DEST_CLASSNAMES` unified into one shared allow-list (both trigger types accept the
  same point-entity classes, matching the engine's classname-agnostic `G_PickTarget`). Regression
  tests added from the real wdm12 (`target_location`) and Q3 (`info_teleport_destination`) records.
- New `client/FPSPickupAudio.ts` mirrors `FPSCombatAudio`: label→sound classifier + `preloadFPSPickupAudio()`
  hooked into the map-load predecode (live + demo). No protocol change — the sound is chosen from the
  existing `pickup_notify` label, so small/large health tiers collapse to one bubble sound.
- Cleanup: the three audio banks' predecode calls collapsed into one `client/FPSAudio.ts#preloadFPSAudio()`
  aggregator (was the same `Promise.all([...])` repeated at each map-load site); unified the two
  near-identical `TriggerTable.DEST_CLASSNAMES` lists into one shared allow-list.
  (`SweepCollisionBackend.buildMeshFace()` was briefly deleted as dead code and is back — world
  triangle soup still needs it; see the collision entry above.)
- **Powerup plumbing is generic, quad is the only entry.** `shared/items/Powerups.ts` holds the pure
  rules (durations, multiplier, mask packing) with no ECS or Babylon import, so the server, the client
  HUD and the tests share one definition. Expiry is stored as an **absolute server tick**, not a
  remaining-time countdown: ticks are the authoritative clock, they survive rollback/re-simulation
  unchanged, and nothing has to decrement per-tick. Adding warshell later is a new entry in
  `POWERUP_KINDS` plus its effect hook — not new plumbing.
- **Wire format**: new entity delta flag `0x40` carries a 1-byte powerup bitmask, sent **every**
  snapshot for any entity that has the component rather than delta-compressed — same reasoning as
  movement state, since a flag stuck after a baseline-ACK mismatch would leave a player glowing with
  a quad they no longer hold. `packPowerupMask` also packs only *active* powerups, so a dropped packet
  can't strand the glow on. Two new events: `powerup_state` (18, owner-only countdown) and
  `powerup_spawn` (19, broadcast + position).
- `PickupType` / `EntityType` / `FPSPickupType` / `ProceduralPickupFamily` each gained a `powerup`
  member. The two EntityType→pickup-category ternary chains in `EntityManager` that had to be edited
  in lockstep were replaced with one `pickupTypeOf()` helper.
- Verified (no change): enemy footsteps/jumps already emit as 3D positional events — the server sends
  per-player `velocity`+`grounded` for all players, and `FPSMovementAudio` plays remote movement via
  `playSfx3D`. Audibility (remote volume/attenuation) is a separate tuning knob, left as-is.
- **The camera claim is now total while dead.** `applyDeathCamera` returning false is what lets the
  fallback death tilt run, so in a round mode it returns true on every frame the local player is
  dead even when no controller has a picture — a level view with nothing to look at beats a rolled
  one. `SpectateEyeCamera` refuses a handoff without a seed sample rather than starting unprimed.
- **Spectated presentation is display-only by construction.** `handleSpectatedWeaponState` writes
  HUD and viewmodel state and never touches inventory or prediction, and `HUDState` splits the
  subject's `dead` from the local player's `localDead` so the death panel and the vitals block can
  disagree about whose body they describe.
- `CollisionWorld.trace()` and `SweepBackendTraceOptions` gained an optional `contentMask`, with
  `SHOT_CONTENT_MASK` (`CONTENTS_SOLID`) exported beside them. Callers that pass nothing keep the
  old `SOLID | PLAYERCLIP` behaviour, so movement is unchanged.
- **Wire**: the JoinGame has-team byte widened in place rather than gaining a field — 0 none, 1
  assigned by the lobby, 2 picked in-game.
- `MapLoadingOverlay.hold()/release()` remember a completion that arrives while held, so the last
  release runs it; `disable()` drops all holds and their timers so none can fire against a disabled
  overlay.
- `lightmapIntensityFor(isFBSP, scale)` in `MapLoader` is a pure function with the format defaults
  and the cvar clamp in one place; a missing, zero, negative or NaN scale falls back to the format
  default rather than rendering the map unlit.
- `torusBuilder` added to the Babylon pre-bundle list — the site beacon is the first import of it in
  the codebase, and the deps guard caught it, which is the exact failure mode that test exists for
  (a first import mid-match triggering a dep-optimize reload).

## Changes, continued

- **Search & Destroy** *(merged to main)* — a round-based bomb-and-defuse
  gametype, Warsow Bomb compatible, replacing Team Deathmatch on the menu. Three modes with distinct
  jobs rather than four with overlapping ones: Duel for 1v1, FFA for practice, S&D for tactical team
  play. TDM's code stays and its tests pass; it simply stops being offered. Test pool is `wbomb2`
  ("Reactors"), the map Warsow's own bomb mode was developed on.

  The design, the map survey and the rules are in
  [`docs/research/Search_And_Destroy.md`](../research/Search_And_Destroy.md). The short version:

  - **The rules are ported, not invented.** `progs/gametypes/bomb/*.as` — Warsow's actual bomb
    implementation — ships inside `data0_21pure.pk3`. Reading it corrected four mechanics that had
    been inferred from map geometry and from Counter-Strike, including two radii this design had
    conflated (arm/defuse is 100 units from the *planted bomb*, not the site), and the fact that arm
    and defuse progress is **shared and decaying** rather than per-player — an interrupted defuse
    bleeds down instead of vanishing, so trading a kill and stepping back in is a real play.
  - **Timers are Warsow's** where we had guessed: 30 s bomb, 4 s arm, 7 s defuse, plant below
    400 ups on ground flat enough to place it on. Round length (90 s) and the score limit (10, with
    Warsow's two-round "tennis rule") stay ours, chosen against traversal times measured on the real
    maps, and both are one config field from Warsow's 60 s / 16.
  - **Round-end rules are asymmetric on purpose.** Killing every defender wins outright; killing
    every attacker only wins if the bomb is not armed — otherwise the defenders still have to walk
    over and defuse it.
  - **Spawn kits.** Blade always, plus one primary (RL / Voltaic / EB) and one secondary (Riotgun /
    PG / GL / MG), with Warsow's spawn ammo and 100 armor. One power weapon rather than Warsow's
    pair, because unlike Warsow Bomb we still spawn map weapons. A change never applies to the round
    in progress, even for a dead player. `loadout <primary> <secondary>` in the console.
  - **One item per round.** Round modes restore the whole map at the freeze instead of running
    per-item timers — a 25 s armor timer means nothing in a 90 s round, so taking it is a decision
    rather than a timing exercise. An `alternating` mode (an item taken this round sits out the next)
    is implemented and tested but off by default.
  - **Client.** A procedural dynamite bundle that rides the carrier's spine bone, turns on the
    ground, or sits planted with a timer light; a HUD slab with phase, fuse and the shared progress
    meter, both role-aware; Warsow's own beep and per-side announcer lines. The blink and the beep
    run off the same server-computed interval, so they are one signal.

  Four latent ordering bugs surfaced on the way, none visible until a round mode existed to trip
  them: frags were about to award *rounds* (in a round mode the team score is the round count); the
  round counter advanced when the round went live rather than at its freeze, so the freeze set up for
  the previous round's attacker (invisible in the first half, wrong at the side swap); round modes
  respawned at the go, making the freeze five seconds of standing around dead; and `getSpawnLoadout()`
  took no player, so a per-player kit was impossible.

- **Search & Destroy, played** *(merged to main)* — the mode above has now been
  played against bots, and almost everything below is a bug that only a played round could have
  found. Grouped by what was wrong rather than by commit:

  - **Nobody could plant.** The ground check rejected every surface, so the win condition was
    unreachable. Warsow traces one unit down and requires the surface normal's z ≥ 0.90; our up axis
    is Y, and the check had been written against the wrong one.
  - **Scoring counted the same damage six times.** Damage-based scoring accumulated once per
    contributing system rather than once per hit, so a single rocket could award six times its worth.
    Scoring now credits what a player actually did rather than who landed the last shot.
  - **Defenders were told what the attackers were doing.** The objective snapshot went out unfiltered,
    so the defending team's HUD showed the attackers' plan. It is now built per team — and the bomb
    reports *who* is arming or defusing it, which the carrier HUD needs and the enemy must not see.
  - **The bomb was thirty times its size**, and dying was a dead end: no spectate, no follow, no way
    to watch the round you were still in. Death now hands you a spectator camera that can follow
    living teammates, which is what a no-respawn mode is for.
  - **Warmup stopped being a warmup** once round logic existed — it inherited round gating and froze.
    Objective publication is now gated on the match actually being live, which also keeps the freeze
    honest.
  - **Attackers spawned in the wrong half of the map.** Attackers use *beta's* spawn set and defenders
    *alpha's*, whichever team is attacking — Warsow's own inversion, where `INITIAL_ATTACKERS =
    TEAM_ALPHA` names the alpha team but spawns them on `team_CTF_betaspawn`. Confirmed against map
    data: on wbomb2 alpha's spawns average ~3400 units from the sites and beta's ~3800.
  - **The match could start without you** and then drop you into it mid-round.

  HUD and presentation, all from watching rounds rather than reading code: the objective moved out of
  the timer cluster into its own corner and left-aligned there; team scores are shown; the countdown
  number went away because the beep is the clock and two clocks disagreeing is worse than one; the
  arm/defuse bar belongs to whoever is actually on the bomb, attacker or defender; a round-result
  banner and a `RoundEnd` phase to hang it on; `target_explosion` groups extracted per bombsite so the
  detonation goes off where the map says it should; site markers visible through walls; a blast camera;
  and three seconds of team spawn protection, because friendly fire plus a shared spawn is a mistimed
  rocket ending a teammate's whole round.

- **Bots can play it** *(merged to main)* — S7 is done: bots consume the gametype's
  agent directives as plain data and contain no S&D code of their own. Three fixes to make that real:
  bots can now *stop* moving (there was no way to express "hold this position", which a defender
  needs); objectives are no longer gated behind a nav graph that cannot route to them, so an
  unreachable path stops silently cancelling the objective; and the floor grid was sampling roofs,
  which put nav nodes in the ceiling.

- **Lobby: teams you can actually choose** *(merged to main)* — a team mode needs a
  team, and the lobby could not reliably give you one. Side switching, with bots backing a shuffle
  vote so a lobby of one human and any number of bots can still reach a majority; the chosen side
  carried through to the game server instead of being discarded on join; the local player identified
  from the lobby server's Welcome rather than guessed as `players[0]`; bots that readied without
  anything showing it, which left a lobby that could never start; ADD BOT relabelled to say what it
  does; gametype shells listed in menu order; and loadout-picker styling.

- **Dev-server stability** *(merged to main)* — Vite was reloading the page
  mid-match while dep-optimizing Babylon on first import. Babylon is now pre-bundled, and the import
  scan catches the forms a `from` grep misses, so a match survives a hot reload.

- **Both manuals rewritten** *(merged to main)* — the engine manual had drifted
  since January and the game manual since May. Seventeen engine pages existed but were linked from
  nowhere in the sidebar; the command reference documented a `network` command that has never
  existed, along with `unbind`/`bindlist`, and named four others wrongly; `network-server.md` claimed
  the server runs at 20 Hz; `project-structure.md` predated `src/bot/` and `src/lobby/` existing. On
  the game side Search & Destroy was undocumented entirely — implemented, on the menu, and named
  nowhere in the manual — and the weapon page still quoted pre-retune plasma and Voltaic numbers.
  Both sites now build clean with no dead links.

- **The biome gate was measuring generated code** *(merged to main)* — `files.includes`
  was `src/**` and nothing told Biome to skip anything inside it, so the fps manual's VitePress output
  (`src/games/fps/dist-docs/`, which its config writes *inside* the submodule) and VitePress's
  dependency pre-bundle cache were linted as source: 98 files carrying ~2200 of the ~2300 errors.
  The 11 cache files alone — vendor megabundles — accounted for 815 of them.
  Anyone who built or served that manual gave themselves a gate failing with phantom errors. Biome now
  reads `.gitignore` (`vcs.useIgnoreFile`), with explicit negations as a fallback for checkouts without
  VCS metadata. Real source measures **79 errors / 343 warnings**, against a baseline of 902/575
  recorded while the same artefacts were inflating it — a baseline eight times the true figure is a
  rubber stamp, not a ratchet, and it has been ratcheted down. Verified by hand: a file with one `any`
  and a missing semicolon passes the gate at 902 and fails it at 79.

- **Spectating is first person now** *(merged to main)* — dying in a no-respawn mode hands you a
  camera, and that camera was showing you the back of a head. Two spectate implementations had
  diverged: the demo viewer hid the followed player's own model, the live path never did, so the
  teammate you were watching stood in front of the lens and filled the screen. Both now run through
  one `SpectateEyeCamera` that sits in the spectated player's eyes, smoothed toward their position
  and view angles, snapping rather than sliding when they teleport.
  - **Their HUD, their gun.** Health, armor, ammo, the weapon in hand and the viewmodel all follow
    whoever you are watching, under a `Spectating <name>` banner. Display only — none of it touches
    your own inventory or the prediction that runs again when you respawn.
  - **The death screen gets one moment.** The panel re-armed on every camera change, so it
    reappeared at the handoff and again at each cycle. It now shows once per death.
  - **Three seconds of corpse** before the handoff, so the frag reads before the view moves.
  - **Fire cycles to the next teammate**, on the press rather than while held.
  - **The camera let go.** Joining a round in progress puts you in dead, to be revived at the next
    freeze — and the release was gated behind a follow target that had already been cleared, so the
    camera stayed bound to whoever it was watching through the freeze, your respawn, and every round
    after it. You were alive, armed and at full health, watching a bot.
  - **The view is level.** Reported three times, and the first two diagnoses were wrong. The ~40°
    death tilt applies whenever nothing else claims the frame, and there were three ways to reach
    that state: an unprimed follow, a handoff with no seed, and a round ending on the clock with
    nothing to look at — which is how it showed up under TIME UP with no spectate involved at all. A
    round mode now claims every frame while you are dead, and the eye camera pins roll to zero.

- **You could walk during the freeze** *(merged to main)* — the server has frozen input for the
  round freeze all along, but the client did not know it, so it predicted the movement, drew it, and
  let the correction warp you back. `MatchStateManager.isMovementFrozen()` mirrors the server's own
  rule and gates input at the source.

- **Shots stopped at brushes you can see through** *(merged to main)* — a hole in a wall on wbomb2
  that a player cannot fit through and a bullet should. `CollisionWorld.trace()` took no content
  mask, so every caller fell back to `SOLID | PLAYERCLIP` — including weapons. Playerclip is a
  movement-only brush by definition; traces now take a mask and shots pass `SOLID`.

- **The bomb rests on the floor** *(merged to main)* — dropped and planted bombs floated at hip
  height. The server held two conventions in one field: a round-spawned bomb was already traced to
  the floor, a dropped one carried the carrier's origin, 24 units up. `BombPlayerView` gained
  `groundPosition`, resolved by the caller because it needs a collision trace, so both agree. (A
  blanket client-side subtraction was tried first and backed out — it would have buried the
  round-spawn case by the same 24 units.)

- **The carried bomb is visible, and says who has it** *(merged to main)* — it had been invisible
  all session: `BombRenderer` took the remote player renderer as a constructor value that did not
  exist yet. It now also falls back to the carrier's hitbox when the model has no spine bone to ride,
  rather than not drawing at all. Nobody could tell who was carrying, including the carrier.

- **Bomb site markers are beacons** *(merged to main)* — the A/B markers were a billboarded letter
  on a flat plate, which read as a piece of UI dropped into the world. Replaced with a framed
  hexagon glyph under a slowly spinning tilted torus, in the site's colour, still visible through
  walls.

- **The round tells you what is happening** *(merged to main)* — Warsow's announcer set was shipping
  unused. Per-side lines on the freeze naming the job you have this round, `fight` on the go, a
  spoken 3-2-1 over the end of the freeze, and separate cues for placed and armed, which had been the
  same announcement. **Last call** warns each side five seconds before its own deadline — the
  attacker at 9 s of round time (an arm takes four), the defender at 12 s of fuse (a defuse takes
  seven) — both derived from the rules rather than written as seconds, so retuning the rules moves
  the warning with them. The generic ready cue was dropped from S&D: the per-side line already covers
  the freeze, and the two played over each other.

- **The detonation is heard and felt across the map** *(merged to main)* — the blast sound was
  positional at a site nobody survives standing next to, so it played to nobody; it is 2D and
  preloaded now. The shake inherited the rocket preset's 540-unit range, which by definition excludes
  anyone alive to watch a site go up — per-blast strength unchanged, range is map-scale.

- **A ring on the floor, and the plant meter where you are looking** *(merged to main)* — the
  arm/defuse radius was invisible and a loose bomb was easy to lose against a detailed floor. Both
  are the same missing circle: the bomb now draws one wherever it is sitting, sized from
  `DEFAULT_BOMB_RULES.armDefuseRadius` rather than a number chosen to look right — a ring that
  disagreed with the rule would teach the wrong distance. Gold loose, amber planted, red and pulsing
  once armed, hidden while carried. The arm/defuse meter also draws below the crosshair, because
  those are the only actions in the mode that ask you to stand still and watch a bar finish while
  holding a sightline someone is about to come through.

- **The loadout picker shows the weapon** *(merged to main)* — seven cards of authored prose about
  what each gun is for, replaced by the weapon itself: one live preview beside the rows, turning
  slowly, and hovering a row aims the stand at it without changing your kit. It runs on its own
  engine and canvas rather than borrowing the game scene — the picker opens before you spawn, so that
  camera can be in the dark, inside geometry or facing a wall, and a menu that shows a black
  silhouette half the time is worse than no picture. Built on first open, disposed on close, every
  failure path silent: a blocking dialog on the way into a round must cost you nothing but a missing
  picture.

- **The lobby's teams survive the whistle** *(merged to main)* — a 2v2 lobby became a 3v1 match, and
  picking Udon put you on Soba. Four separate causes. Bots were constructed with no team and handed
  their construction-time value to the game server, so every bot joined asking for nothing and was
  auto-balanced into a split nobody had agreed to; they now track their lobby side by id, through
  switches and shuffles. The balance guard could not tell a player picking a side in-game from a
  lobby delivering an already-balanced roster — both arrive as JoinGame carrying a team string — so
  it rejected the roster, whose counts are lopsided for most of the sequence in which players arrive.
  The has-team byte now reads 0 none / 1 lobby-assigned / 2 picked in-game: same wire size, and an
  older client's 1 lands on the permissive path, which is the safe direction to be wrong in. A
  reconnect ate the side you chose. And join confirmation was a five-second race that, once lapsed,
  stopped looking at all — the server could spawn you with full health and a kit while the client sat
  in freefly with no way back. It is a state now, so the server can take as long as it needs.

- **Team colour is allegiance** *(merged to main)* — your side is cyan and theirs is pink wherever
  you look, published from the same cvars the player models read. Lobby columns, scoreboard, chat
  senders and the ESC join buttons had five independent hardcoded palettes between them: Udon was
  pink in the lobby and red on the scoreboard, which is a different team as far as a player is
  concerned, and the scoreboard listed the sides in the opposite order to the lobby — so a correct
  assignment read as being switched. A duplicate rule set that styled the ESC buttons in the opposite
  colours, with specificity rather than intent deciding which applied, is gone.

- **Bots no longer start the match without you** *(merged to main)* — a bot readies the instant it
  joins and connects in milliseconds; a browser has a BSP to download, build and upload to the GPU.
  So for the first seconds of every lobby-spawned match the roster was "two bots, both ready, no
  humans connected", and `allReady()` read that as a finished warmup. You then arrived mid-round,
  which a round mode correctly answers by spawning you dead until the next freeze — loading in
  already fragged, having never seen a warmup that happened before you got there and lasted a tick.
  A `--single-match` server now waits up to 30 s for its humans before bots may start alone; a
  standing dev or training server still starts immediately.

- **The loading screen holds until the game is ready** *(merged to main)* — the overlay faded on
  `loading:complete`, which reports that the MAP is loaded, not that there is anything worth looking
  at. `hold(name)` / `release(name)` park the fade until every holder is done, with a 20 s
  force-release that names the holder in the warning, because a stuck loading screen is worse than
  the flicker this fixes.

- **A blocked path stops an item run, never an objective** *(merged to main)* — `_navigateTo`
  answers an unroutable path with a zero direction. That is right for an item, which is not going
  anywhere, and a bot planted beside a lift it cannot use reads as waiting. It is wrong in a round
  mode with a clock, where it looks exactly like "the attacking bots just stand in spawn". Objectives
  now steer straight at the target instead: walking into a wall is visible and recoverable, standing
  still is neither. (One candidate for that report, not a confirmed cause — see Known Unverified
  Items.)

- **Every pk3 added since the repo went ESM was invisible** *(merged to main)* — a browser cannot
  list a directory, so packs are discovered by fetching `public/data/pk3_manifest.json`. The
  generator that keeps it honest was still CommonJS and had been throwing on `require` since the
  repo moved to `"type": "module"`, so `npm run tools:manifest` did nothing. A pk3 on disk but absent
  from the manifest does not exist as far as the client is concerned — the map inside it is
  unfindable and the load fails before any BSP is parsed, which reads as a broken map rather than a
  missing line of JSON. Converted to ESM; `acidwdm2` loads, and replaces `wdm12` in the duel pool.

- **Quake 3 maps rendered dark** *(merged to main)* — the single 2.0 lightmap gain was tuned against
  Warsow FBSP maps, and a Q3 map bakes expecting the engine to apply overbright *and* a gamma ramp on
  top. Nothing was mis-parsed: the IBSP lightmap, vertex and face paths all read correctly. IBSP now
  defaults to 3.0, FBSP keeps 2.0, and the new `r_lightmapscale` multiplies whichever base applies.

- **Touching a gun while holding the blade arms you with it** *(merged to main)* — three paths
  through `addWeapon`, and only one honoured that. A weapon you already own gave you ammo and left
  you on the blade — the common case after a loadout spawn, because the kit puts the blade in your
  hands and your chosen guns in your pack — and a pickup declined at capacity left you holding a
  knife with two guns on your back. All three arm you now. A real weapon already in hand is
  deliberately left alone: crossing a machinegun while holding a rocket launcher is not a request.

## Known Unverified Items

- **Bots hold at their spawn in Search & Destroy — open, and the largest thing this release ships
  without.** Attacking bots hop in place at spawn instead of moving on the site, including after
  picking up the bomb. The whole directive path was traced statically and each link checks out: every
  attacker branch issues a directive, entity ids round-trip, roles are assigned every live tick, the
  directive lifetime outlives the snapshot gap, the route-cost gate exempts objectives, and
  navigation now steers straight at a blocked objective rather than stopping. The blocked-path fix
  above is a candidate, not a confirmed cause, and it has not been played since. Localising it needs
  one number from the `bot:server` terminal — `NavGraph built → N nodes`. If that is zero or single
  digits the behaviour is fully explained and directives are not implicated at all.
- **The pickup art pass has had one QA look, the weapon spawn toss none.** Armor and the health cross
  were iterated against screenshots (v5 → v8); the ring gradient, ring transparency and the weapon
  spawn toss have still only been verified by tests. Tuning dials live at the top of
  `ProceduralPickupGeometry.ts` (`ARMOR_BANDS`, `ARMOR_CORE_INSET`, the overlap constants) and
  `EntityManager.ts` (`SPAWN_TOSS_UP`, `SPAWN_TOSS_SPREAD`).
- **Mostly not playtested in the real client** — the collision/root-motion evidence above is headless
  measurement. The S&D rounds since have put **wbomb2** in front of a human on this build, incidentally
  covering one of the load-bearing-soup maps; nobody was looking for collision faults at the time, but
  nothing obvious surfaced. Still wants a deliberate pass on wbomb1 and wbomb3-6, wctf2, wdm1, wdm17
  and wtutorial1 (the rest of the load-bearing vertex-lit triangle soup) plus wdm2/wdm1 for the
  decoration change.
- **31 newly-live jumppads/teleporters** on wdm11/12/17/18/19, wbomb2/6, wctf1, wda1/4 and wtutorial1
  have never fired in this engine. Worth watching bot pathing across them specifically — the nav graph
  now has trigger nodes on maps where it previously had none.
- **Floating dropped-weapon pickup** seen once in QA (weapon model hovering in a corridor with the
  pickup highlight, no owner nearby). Different code path from the fixes here; re-check on this build
  before chasing.
- Gunblade-era demos no longer render their blast projectiles (accepted, see above).
- **Quad has never been seen in the client.** Everything above is tests + headless measurement: the
  gem/blade silhouette, the red carrier pulse, the HUD slab and all three sounds are unverified in
  play. Most likely to want tuning: `QUAD_GLOW_*` / `QUAD_PULSE_HZ` in `RemotePlayerRenderer.ts`, the
  `QUAD_*` geometry constants in `ProceduralPickupGeometry.ts`, and `quad_fire` volume (0.55) — a
  10-shots/sec plasmagun layers that sound fast.
- **Quad balance is unplayed.** 4× / 30s / 120s are the Warsow-standard numbers, not measured ones,
  and the Q3-classic knockback scope interacts with an already-strong KB curve — a quad rocket may
  throw further than intended. All three live in `shared/items/Powerups.ts`.
- ~~**Search & Destroy has never been played.**~~ **Resolved** — it has, against bots on wbomb2, and
  the "played" entry above is the list of what that found. S7 is also done: bots follow the gametype's
  agent directives. What remains unverified is the *tuning*, which no bot match can settle: the 10 s
  freeze, the 90 s round, the score limit of 10, the two-round lead and the 384-unit fallback site
  radius are all still unmeasured against humans. **No S&D round has been played human-vs-human.**
- **The `alternating` item mode still should not ship without playtest.** Unchanged from before: it is
  invisible to players without HUD support, and it leaves the second half inheriting the first half's
  depleted map, which breaks the mirror the side swap exists to provide. Off by default.
- **Bots do not know quad exists.** They have no desire term for it, so they will walk past the most
  valuable item on the map and will not contest a carrier. Worth doing before quad affects match
  outcomes in bot games. (Bots *do* now follow S&D objectives — this gap is quad-specific.)
- **The lobby's team flow has only been exercised solo-plus-bots.** Side switching swaps with a
  counterpart rather than emptying a side, which is the right behaviour for one human and some bots;
  what it does in a lobby of eight humans all switching at once is untested, and the swap picks its
  counterpart deterministically (bots first) rather than by any fairness rule.
- **The lightmap gain is a per-format bet on one map's evidence.** IBSP 3.0 was confirmed in play on
  `acidwdm2` and nowhere else. It cannot be distinguished from "acidwdm2 wants 3.0" at n=1; if a
  second Quake 3 map lands on a different number the honest conclusion is that the gain belongs
  per-map, and `r_lightmapscale` is the escape hatch until then.
- **LightGrid dimension mismatch on IBSP maps.** `BspLoader` warns that the map bounds imply more
  light volumes than the lump holds (9472 vs 8640 on `acidwdm2`) and says lighting will be aligned
  incorrectly. That is entity and model lighting, not the lightmapped world, so it is a separate bug
  from the darkness above and was not touched by it.
- **Enemy outlines leak roughly a pixel through thin geometry** — visible at spawn on some walls,
  not through two corners' worth of wall. The outline pass draws a fattened copy of the mesh along
  its normals, so a body close enough to a thin wall pushes that copy through it; there is no depth
  trick to blame and no cheap fix that keeps the outline. Knobs are `cl_player_outline_size` (25) and
  `cl_player_outline`. Reported and left as-is deliberately.
- **The site beacons, the bomb ring, the centre plant meter and the loadout weapon preview are all
  visual and have had at most one look.** The beacons and the ring have not been seen in play since
  their last revision at all.
- **Interactive consoles for `bot:server` and `net:server` were scoped, not built.** Both terminals
  are firehoses with no filter, search or live introspection, which is the reason the bot bug above
  is still open; the client console's `CommandRegistry` is the obvious substrate to reuse.
