# v0.9.22

**Release Date:** September 10, 2026

## Summary

v0.9.22 replaces legacy Quake 3/Warsow water presentation with an engine-owned,
quality-scalable water stack. It also makes liquids part of deterministic player
simulation: players can wade, swim, dive, surface, drown, take lava damage and
interact with water through ripples and splash spray.

The Epic tier combines depth-aware colour absorption, screen-space transmission,
cached room cubemaps, authored-light highlights, procedural waves, reactive wakes
and short-range soft player reflections. The implementation is bound to semantic
liquid surfaces, so supported map shaders retain their BSP geometry while the
engine replaces their legacy scrolling material stages.

## Major Features

### Engine-owned water rendering

- Classifies water, slime and lava from BSP contents and shader semantics while
  preserving explicit compatibility overrides.
- Replaces supported water surfaces with one shared material family and restores
  the original map material when replacement is disabled or cannot initialize.
- Adds four quality levels: legacy/off, Low, Medium and Epic. Epic is now the
  default for continued testing; saved user settings still take precedence.
- Uses slow analytic waves and procedural normals instead of coloured scrolling
  legacy layers. Impact ripples and directional swimming wakes perturb the same
  normal field without displacing collision geometry.
- Adds per-channel, ray-length-based absorption, depth-aware transparency,
  shoreline/contact shading, surface sheen and bounded authored-light highlights.
- Adds a half-resolution, depth-guarded world-colour capture for distorted
  transmission on Epic water. Water, viewmodels, particles and transparent effects
  are excluded from the capture.

### Cached environment and player reflections

- Generates up to four 256-pixel static room cubemaps after BSP lighting is ready.
  Probe positions are lifted within local ceiling bounds and box-projected around
  each connected horizontal water region.
- Adds reflection strength, roughness, blur, detail and contact controls that can
  be tuned live without recapturing the map.
- Adds a separate quarter-resolution nearby-player mirror layer for at most two
  actors. Static BSP geometry writes its occlusion mask while the cached cubemap
  remains the environment colour source.
- Reflects both remote players and a reflection-only local body, reusing normal
  player models and locomotion clips. The local body never appears in the main,
  depth or refraction camera and has no gameplay hitbox.
- Player reflections blur and fade with distance, distort with the water surface,
  and retain a small top-down visibility floor for readable solo testing.

### Swimming and liquid gameplay

- Adds deterministic foot/waist/eye liquid sampling and carries water level through
  predicted movement state and the multiplayer snapshot protocol.
- Adds wading, surface swimming, diving, surfacing, swimming animation selection
  and a prone swimming hull compatible with the shared movement simulation.
- Adds underwater view treatment, configurable distortion, submerged viewmodel
  motion, air depletion, a low-air HUD meter, drowning damage and lava damage.
- Adds water-aware electrical discharge and remote-player water presentation.

### Reactive splashes

- Emits splashes and shader ripples from local prediction, remote water-level
  transitions, projectile impacts and strong droplet re-entry.
- Replaces hard filled-disc ripples with bounded shader rings and directional
  wakes. Supported replacement water does not also draw the legacy ring effect.
- Reworks splash particles into pooled, velocity-aligned spray quads and lightweight
  geometric crowns. Spray is excluded from water captures and scales by entry/exit
  strength while retaining hard particle and ripple budgets.

### Diagnostics and documentation

- Adds `water sample`, `water splash`, `water ripple` and `water render` diagnostics.
  They expose collision contents, probe heights, ripple delivery, replacement
  counts and player-reflection admission/failure state.
- Adds debug views for depth/thickness, cached reflection, reactive slopes and
  undistorted transmission capture.
- Rewrites the shader-system guide and records the design decisions, abandoned
  FluidRenderer/continuous-planar approaches, measurements and visual checkpoints
  in `docs/research/WATER.md`.
- Adds a repeatable real-browser/WebGL fixture for acidwdm2 water rendering,
  interaction, quality-tier and reflection/occlusion checks.

## Performance and Safety

- Static environment reflections are captured once per map rather than rendered
  every frame. Expensive camera-owned passes suspend after water remains offscreen.
- Epic uses one half-resolution transmission capture plus the bounded actor layer;
  Medium keeps depth absorption without the transmission or actor capture.
- Actor reflection fails closed when geometry or occluder budgets are exceeded,
  preventing missing walls from exposing hidden players.
- Map-owned resources, observers, temporary materials, render targets and local
  reflection bodies are restored or disposed during map and renderer teardown.

## Validation

- Complete Vitest suite: 2,466 tests across 233 files. The first run identified
  missing/stale Babylon optimize-dependency entries; `npm run deps:babylon`
  regenerated the manifest and the complete rerun passed.
- Targeted final water/liquid/console suite: 352 tests across 24 files.
- Typecheck, production TypeScript/Vite build and VitePress documentation build
  passed.
- Simulation, physics, server-tick and server-fuzz regressions passed. The npm fuzz
  wrapper ran its default 10,000 ticks despite the requested 1,000-tick argument.
- Wdm2 BSP load/info passed through the PK3 index.
- Chrome/WebGL2 acidwdm2 checks passed on an RTX 4070 SUPER for replacement-water
  readiness, all quality tiers, postprocessed transmission, gameplay ripple
  delivery, local/remote player capture and complete wall occlusion.

## Known Limitations

- The local-player wiring has GPU fixture coverage using the real model path but
  was not exercised in a live networked match for this release.
- Static cubemaps do not update moving doors or scenery and are approximate around
  interior islands. Sloped/multi-height regions and pools beyond the probe budget
  use the authored/sky fallback.
- The actor layer still submits nearby opaque occluder geometry each active frame.
  Admission between pools and the two-actor ranking can pop; alpha-tested blockers
  are conservatively solid and only opaque actor parts participate.
- Local reflection animation is a locomotion proxy, not a complete first-person
  aiming or weapon pose. Partial bank-edge occlusion needs wider map coverage.
- The documented compound headless replay command completes its first replay but
  then treats `JUMP` and `W` as standalone unknown console commands. This existing
  CLI syntax limitation does not affect the water or simulation regression results.
- Physics validation retains the known expected-Y 432 versus actual 422 diagnostic,
  and wdm2/acidwdm2 retain known lightgrid mismatch warnings.

## Upgrade Notes

- Restart the development server and hard-refresh after updating: stale Vite shader
  modules can otherwise fail GPU readiness during hot reload.
- Existing saved cvars override new defaults. For the complete test presentation:

  ```text
  r_water 1
  r_water_quality 3
  r_water_actors 1
  r_water_debug 0
  map acidwdm2 duel
  ```

- Use `water render` to distinguish a disabled actor layer, an unloaded/out-of-range
  model, a budget rejection and an active reflection capture.
